*The Lagrangian Grassmannian Λ(d) is the space of all ways to split phase space into "position" and "momentum" halves: every [Lagrangian subspace](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/lagrangian-polarizations) of a 2d-dimensional phase space is one point of it. It is a compact manifold, U(d)/O(d), of dimension 10 when d = 4, and it contains a circle that cannot be shrunk to a point, the Maslov circle. It also sits on the edge of [the Siegel domain](/wiki/phase-space/modular-field/siegel-domain) as that domain's distinguished (Shilov) boundary. In the program it is the vacuum manifold of the polarization: the vacuum's polarization is one point of Λ(d), and the circle direction is identified with the dark-energy field.*

**Status:** Established — standard mathematics [T] (#1 §2; #11 §6) — not machine-checked; its d = 1 case appears in the toy model's Goldstone spectrum.

## Key facts

- **Definition and size [T].** #1 §2: "The space of Lagrangian polarizations of ℝ^{2d} is the Lagrangian Grassmannian Λ(d) ≅ U(d)/O(d), compact, of dimension d(d+1)/2 = 10 for d = 4."
- **The Maslov class [T].** #1 §2: "Its fundamental group is π₁(Λ(d)) = ℤ, generated by the **Maslov circle** via the map det²: U(d)/O(d) → U(1)." This circle carries the dark-energy field — see [τ as the Maslov phase](/wiki/phase-space/modular-field/maslov-phase).
- **As the space of polarizations under P2′.** #5 §B: "The space of H-compatible real structures on (ℝ^{2d}, I) is {J} ≅ U(d)/O(d) = Λ(d), the Lagrangian Grassmannian — π₁ = ℤ, Maslov circle intact, τ restored". Under [original P2](/wiki/program/postulates/original-p2), with η rigid, it collapses to a discrete set of splittings; [P2′](/wiki/program/postulates/p2-prime) restores it.
- **Shilov boundary [T].** #11 §6: "**Λ(d) is the Shilov boundary** of the Siegel domain [T]"; #5 §C marks the fact "[standard]".
- **Ten compact moduli.** #7 §4: "J carries only the 10 compact moduli of U(d)/O(d) — not a full second metric. So the 'second vielbein' E₀[J] is confined to a compact 10-dimensional moduli space" — the basis of [constrained bimetric gravity](/wiki/gravity/tuning-test/constrained-bimetric). #5 §D counts them as "τ plus nine, of which inverse-Higgs constraints likely eliminate most [O]" — see [the field content of the broken vacuum](/wiki/phase-space/modular-field/field-content).
- **Λ(4) and Λ(1).** The notes write Λ(d) with d = 4 physical, and #11 writes Λ(4); the two agree. In the toy model d = 1: "the compact angle φ, which parametrizes the Lagrangian Grassmannian Λ(1)" (toy-model note §2), which #11 §10 calls "τ's circle" — see [model I vacua](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/toy-models/coset-model/vacua) and [the polarization as a Goldstone angle](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/goldstone-angle).
