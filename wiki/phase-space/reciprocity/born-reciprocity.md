*Born reciprocity is the map ρ: (x, p) → (p, −x), which exchanges position and momentum, with one sign change, as a quarter-turn of phase space. The rules of quantum kinematics are unchanged by it, and the [Fourier transform](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/fourier-transform) carries it out on wavefunctions. Max Born proposed in 1938 and 1949 that a fundamental theory should respect it, and hoped for a physics with a fundamental mass and a fundamental length on equal footing. Observed dynamics does not respect it — interactions are local in position — and the program's starting assumption, [its hard core](/wiki/program/hard-core), is that ρ is an exact symmetry of the laws that the vacuum breaks.*

**Status:** Established — literature; #11 cites Born from model memory [1†, 2†] — the round-3 citation audit confirmed both references' metadata but did not read the primary text (`PARTIALLY VERIFIED (primary text not read)`); the source check is part of [the † literature battery](/wiki/method/literature-battery).

## Key facts

- **The map.** As a matrix on (x, p), ρ = [[0, 1], [−1, 0]]; it "is symplectic and squares to −1" (toy-model note §1), so it lies in the group of [the symplectic form](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/symplectic-form). Its square is the parity ρ²: (x, p) → (−x, −p) (#1 §2), and #1's P3 calls ρ "the ℤ₄ generated by the complex structure I".
- **A symmetry of kinematics.** #11 §3: "Quantum kinematics is exactly invariant under ρ: (x,p) → (p,−x); the Fourier transform implements it unitarily [1†, 2†]." #9 §1 adds that "the canonical commutator is blind to it" ([the canonical commutator](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/canonical-commutator)). Ordinarily this makes ρ a change of description rather than a symmetry of a specific action — see [unitary equivalence versus Noether symmetry](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/unitary-equivalence).
- **Born's papers.** #11 [1]† M. Born, "A suggestion for unifying quantum theory and relativity," Proc. R. Soc. Lond. A 165, 291 (1938); [2]† M. Born, "Reciprocity theory of elementary particles," Rev. Mod. Phys. 21, 463 (1949). The round-3 audit (of #14, which cites the same two papers) reports that the "1938 abstract does not state the rule; primary text paywalled."
- **Born's self-dual program.** #13 §4: "This was Born's original hope in 1938: a physics with a fundamental mass and a fundamental length on equal footing." #13 claims its invariant-length shell "recovers Born's 1938–49 program of a self-dual physics with both m and λ fundamental [3†, 4†]" [D — structural] — see [the invariant-length shell](/wiki/conjugate-relativity/invariant-length-shell).
- **Names.** The program also calls ρ the Born rotation (#11 P3); Freidel, Leigh and Minic call the symmetry Born duality ([broken Born duality](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/broken-born-duality)).
- **What the program does with it.** "The program: ρ is exact and spontaneously broken" (#11 §3); P3 makes the action invariant under the Born rotation generated by I = ω⁻¹H [P] ([P3](/wiki/program/postulates/p3-reciprocity)). On the geometry, ρ preserves ω and H and flips the neutral metric η [D] (#1 §2; [the structures ω, η, H](/wiki/phase-space/born-geometry/born-structures)); on the modular field it acts as Z → −Z⁻¹ ([reciprocity as modular inversion](/wiki/phase-space/modular-field/modular-inversion)).
