*Curved momentum space is the idea, developed in a line of quantum-gravity research, that the space of momenta can carry a geometry of its own instead of being flat. Snyder used it in 1947 to build a quantized space-time; in quantum gravity with two space dimensions, which can be solved exactly, the momenta of a particle live on a curved space; and "relative locality" turned the idea into a principle. General relativity curves space-time but leaves momentum space flat, which the program counts as an assumption rather than a measurement; its own version of momentum-space geometry is the momentum graviton X. One known difficulty of the idea, the soccer-ball problem, was listed as open in the lab notebook and never resolved.*

**Status:** Established — literature [T] (#9 §1), cited from model memory [4†–6†] and not yet source-checked (the [† literature battery](/wiki/method/literature-battery)) — the soccer-ball problem folded in below is open [O] (#1 §11 item 6) and absent from #11's open list.

## Key facts

- **The program's framing.** #11 §3 counts among the dynamical sources of the x/p asymmetry "general relativity's flat momentum fibers — the last an architectural assumption, not a measurement, and false in the one exactly solved quantum gravity (2+1d, where momenta live on a curved group manifold [5†]; curved momentum space has an independent lineage from Snyder [4†] through relative locality [6†])." #9 §1 tags the 2+1 statement [T]. See [Born's question](/wiki/program/borns-question).
- **The references (#11).** [4]† H. S. Snyder, "Quantized space-time," Phys. Rev. 71, 38 (1947). [5]† H.-J. Matschull, M. Welling, "Quantum mechanics of a point particle in 2+1 dimensional gravity," Class. Quantum Grav. 15, 2981 (1998), gr-qc/9708054. [6]† G. Amelino-Camelia, L. Freidel, J. Kowalski-Glikman, L. Smolin, "The principle of relative locality," Phys. Rev. D 84, 084010 (2011), arXiv:1101.0931.
- **Open [O]: extensivity (the soccer-ball problem).** #1 §11 item 6: "why macroscopic momenta don't probe g̃ curvature; patched by standard coproduct rescaling, which is ad hoc here as everywhere." The item was dropped from later open-problem lists without a resolution; it is not among the items of #11 §14. See [open problems and the queue](/wiki/status/open-problems).
- **The program's momentum geometry.** In the vacuum the momentum metric is fixed by the position metric, g̃ = g⁻¹ ([the corollaries of the compatibility theorem](/wiki/phase-space/born-geometry/compatibility-corollaries)), and "any independent momentum-space geometry resides in the fluctuation X" of the modular field (#14 §1; [the modular field Z](/wiki/phase-space/modular-field/modular-field-z)). #11 §7's answer: "momentum-space geometry is dynamical — a reciprocity-odd massive spin-2" ([Born's answer](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/borns-answer)).
