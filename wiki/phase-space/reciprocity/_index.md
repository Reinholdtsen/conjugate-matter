*[Born reciprocity](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/born-reciprocity) is the exchange of position and momentum, ρ: (x, p) → (p, −x), a quarter-turn of phase space that leaves the rules of quantum kinematics unchanged. The Fourier transform carries it out: a state's position description and its momentum description are related as a waveform and its spectrum. The laws of motion that are observed do not share the symmetry — interactions are local in position, not in momentum — and Max Born asked in 1938 why. This section collects the established physics behind the symmetry and the literature that followed; the program's own claims about it are in other sections.*

**Stands:** all nine pages below are established physics, from [the canonical commutator](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/canonical-commutator) to [the Freidel–Leigh–Minic precedent](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/broken-born-duality) · **Died:** nothing yet · **Open:** no page of its own; [curved momentum space](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/curved-momentum-space) carries the open soccer-ball (extensivity) problem

## Map

[The canonical commutator and the uncertainty principle](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/canonical-commutator) comes first: [x, p] = iℏ defines quantum kinematics, keeps position and momentum from both being sharp, and is unchanged by ρ.

[The symplectic form and Sp(2d, ℝ)](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/symplectic-form) is the classical structure behind the commutator and the group of linear maps that preserve it; ρ is one of its elements, and the program's one rigid structure is this form.

[The Fourier transform as a phase-space rotation](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/fourier-transform) is the unitary map that implements ρ on wavefunctions, the literal version of the waveform–spectrum analogy.

[Born reciprocity ρ: (x, p) → (p, −x)](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/born-reciprocity) states the symmetry itself, with Born's 1938 and 1949 papers and his hope for a physics with a fundamental mass and a fundamental length.

[Unitary equivalence versus Noether symmetry](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/unitary-equivalence) explains why ρ is ordinarily only a change of description, and why the referees asked for an action in which it is a genuine symmetry.

[Lagrangian subspaces and polarizations](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/lagrangian-polarizations) defines what it means to choose which coordinates count as "position" — the choice the program assigns to the vacuum.

[The Wigner function and Weyl symbols](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/wigner-function) draws a quantum state on phase space; the toy models use it to build a matter coupling that tells position from momentum.

[Curved momentum space](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/curved-momentum-space) is the quantum-gravity literature in which momentum space has a geometry of its own, from Snyder through relative locality, with the soccer-ball problem still open.

[Broken Born duality: the Freidel–Leigh–Minic precedent](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/broken-born-duality) comes last because it is the bridge to the program: Freidel, Leigh and Minic wrote that a preferred polarization breaks Born duality, and the program's addition is to read that breaking as spontaneous.
