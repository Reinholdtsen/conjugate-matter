*[Born reciprocity](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/born-reciprocity) is the exchange of position and momentum, ρ: (x, p) → (p, −x), a quarter-turn of phase space that leaves the rules of quantum kinematics unchanged. The Fourier transform carries it out: a state's position description and its momentum description are related as a waveform and its spectrum. The laws of motion that are observed do not share the symmetry — interactions are local in position, not in momentum — and Max Born asked in 1938 why. This section collects the established physics behind the symmetry and the literature that followed; the program's own claims about it are in other sections.*

**Literature only:** all nine pages are established physics or literature, so the section ranks no claims of the program's own; those are tracked in the registers of [claims that stand](/wiki/status/alive), [the graveyard](/wiki/status/graveyard), [open problems and the queue](/wiki/status/open-problems) and [superseded statements](/wiki/status/superseded). ## Map

[The canonical commutator and the uncertainty principle](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/canonical-commutator) comes first: [x, p] = iℏ, the bound on Δx · Δp that follows from it, and why the relation is unchanged by ρ. The program uses it twice, as the premise of mandatory breaking and to read the rigidity of ω as the constancy of ℏ.

[The symplectic form and Sp(2d, ℝ)](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/symplectic-form) is the classical structure behind the commutator and the group of linear maps that preserve it: rotations, of which ρ is the quarter-turn, and the non-compact boosts and squeezes along which model I's first ρ-preserving potential failed. The program's one rigid structure is this form.

[The Fourier transform as a phase-space rotation](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/fourier-transform) is the unitary map that implements ρ on wavefunctions: a quarter-period of harmonic-oscillator motion, with F² the parity. It is the literal version of the waveform–spectrum analogy, time–bandwidth product included.

[Born reciprocity ρ: (x, p) → (p, −x)](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/born-reciprocity) states the symmetry itself: why it carries a sign, why it is a symmetry of kinematics and not of observed dynamics, Born's 1938 and 1949 papers and their partial source check, his hope for a fundamental mass and a fundamental length, and what the program does with ρ.

[Unitary equivalence versus Noether symmetry](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/unitary-equivalence) explains why, by the Stone–von Neumann theorem, ρ is ordinarily only a change of description, why the referees asked for an action in which it is a genuine symmetry, and how far the toy models answer them.

[Lagrangian subspaces and polarizations](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/lagrangian-polarizations) defines what it means to choose which coordinates count as "position", the choice the program assigns to the vacuum. The toy models build the choice from a real structure K, and the space of all choices is the Lagrangian Grassmannian.

[The Wigner function and Weyl symbols](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/wigner-function) draws a quantum state on phase space. The toy models use it to build a matter coupling that tells position from momentum, with ∫W² = 1/2π as the control that cannot, and read the duality of model II through Royer's parity form.

[Curved momentum space](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/curved-momentum-space) is the quantum-gravity literature in which momentum space has a geometry of its own, from Snyder through relative locality, set beside the program's own dynamical momentum metric. Its folded open item is the soccer-ball problem.

[Broken Born duality: the Freidel–Leigh–Minic precedent](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/broken-born-duality) comes last because it is the bridge to the program. Freidel, Leigh and Minic wrote that a preferred polarization breaks Born duality; the program's addition is to read that breaking as spontaneous, with η as the order parameter. The page also records how the credit entered the checkpoint.
