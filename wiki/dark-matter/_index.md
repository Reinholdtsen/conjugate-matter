*Dark matter is the mass that astronomers detect only through its gravity — in the orbital speeds of stars and gas in galaxies, in the motions of galaxies inside clusters, and in the bending of light — and that emits and absorbs no light of its own. The Broken-Born program offers two candidates for it: particles of conjugate matter, the mirror-polarized counterpart of ordinary matter, whose own force acts only between particles moving at nearly the same velocity; or a coherent oscillation of the program's momentum graviton X. This section holds the established astrophysics those candidates are tested against, then the program's own claims in the order they were derived: the velocity gate, the test that killed the program's founding image of dark matter clumping by velocity, and the candidates that remain.*

## Map

The first two subsections are established literature; the last three are the program's own claims.

[Halo astrophysics](/wiki/dark-matter/halo-astrophysics) comes first because every dark-matter claim of the program is tested against it. It covers what galaxies and clusters show about whether dark matter scatters off itself: self-interacting dark matter, the cores of dwarf galaxies and the ultra-faint dwarfs, and the stellar streams and dynamical friction that probe a halo's small-scale structure.

[Wave dark matter](/wiki/dark-matter/wave-dark-matter) is the physics of dark matter light enough to behave as a classical wave: when that happens, how such waves condense into Bose stars, when attractive condensates collapse, how such a field is produced, and how light it may be. It is the background for the wave branch of the momentum-clumping test.

[Conjugate matter and the velocity gate](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter) begins the program's claims. It is the subject of [Debt 2](/wiki/history/debts): the mirror-polarized matter field χ, the gate that lets χ particles interact only at small relative velocity, the mechanism that delivers the gate through exchange of the momentum graviton, the resulting knee in the scattering cross-section, the benchmark that fixes its strength, and two open problems.

[Momentum clumping (Debt 3)](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping) is the test of the founding image — dark matter clumping by velocity rather than by position — and its obituary: impossible for particles, fatal where it would have been mandatory for waves, optional elsewhere. It also records what survived the test and the one loophole left open.

[Dark-sector candidates](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates) states the program's answer to what dark matter is — χ particles or an X-condensate — together with the abundance question both share, and the retired third candidate, superheavy solitons, with its obituary.
