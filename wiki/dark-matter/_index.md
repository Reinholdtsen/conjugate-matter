*Dark matter is the mass that astronomers detect only through its gravity — in the orbital speeds of stars and gas in galaxies, in the motions of galaxies inside clusters, and in the bending of light — and that emits and absorbs no light of its own. The Broken-Born program offers two candidates for it: particles of [conjugate matter](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter), the counterpart of ordinary matter in which the roles of position and momentum are exchanged, whose own force acts only between particles moving at nearly the same velocity; or a coherent oscillation of the [momentum graviton X](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton), a massive second gravitational field that the program's [broken symmetry](/wiki/symmetry-breaking) between position and momentum adds to gravity. This section holds the established astrophysics those candidates are tested against, then the program's own claims in the order they were derived: the [velocity gate](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/velocity-gate), the test that killed the program's [founding image](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/founding-image) of dark matter clumping by velocity, and the candidates that remain. Both candidates are open, and neither explains the observed ratio of dark to ordinary matter.*

## Map

The first two subsections are established literature; the last three are the program's own claims, in the order the program derived them. No `verify/` script covers the Debt-2 or Debt-3 numbers; the machine-checked numbers of this section belong to the soliton review ([the soliton-obituary script](/wiki/method/verification/soliton-obituary-script)).

[Halo astrophysics](/wiki/dark-matter/halo-astrophysics) comes first because every dark-matter claim of the program is tested against it. It covers what galaxies and clusters show about whether dark matter scatters off itself: [self-interacting dark matter](/wiki/dark-matter/halo-astrophysics/self-interacting-dark-matter) and its [transfer cross-section](/wiki/dark-matter/halo-astrophysics/transfer-cross-section), the cores of [dwarf galaxies](/wiki/dark-matter/halo-astrophysics/dwarf-galaxies) and the ultra-faint dwarfs, Segue 1 among them, the cluster collision called [the Bullet Cluster](/wiki/dark-matter/halo-astrophysics/bullet-cluster), and the [stellar streams and dynamical friction](/wiki/dark-matter/halo-astrophysics/halo-substructure) that probe a halo's small-scale structure.

[Wave dark matter](/wiki/dark-matter/wave-dark-matter) is the physics of dark matter light enough to behave as a classical wave: when that happens (the [occupation number](/wiki/dark-matter/wave-dark-matter/occupation-number)), how such waves condense into [Bose stars](/wiki/dark-matter/wave-dark-matter/wave-kinetics), when [attractive condensates](/wiki/dark-matter/wave-dark-matter/attractive-condensates) collapse, how such a field is [produced](/wiki/dark-matter/wave-dark-matter/misalignment-mechanism), and [how light it may be](/wiki/dark-matter/wave-dark-matter/fuzzy-dark-matter-bounds). It is the background for the wave branch of the momentum-clumping test.

[Conjugate matter and the velocity gate](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter) begins the program's claims. It is the subject of [Debt 2](/wiki/history/debts): the conjugate matter field χ, the gate that lets χ particles interact only at small relative velocity, the mechanism that delivers the gate through exchange of the momentum graviton, the resulting knee in the scattering cross-section, the benchmark that fixes its strength, and two open problems.

[Momentum clumping (Debt 3)](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping) is the test of the founding image — dark matter clumping by velocity rather than by position — and its obituary. For particles the mean-field attraction falls short by 28–31 orders of magnitude, and collective behavior requires a wave; on [the wave island](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/wave-island), clumping is fatal where it would have been mandatory and optional elsewhere. It also records what survived the test — the knee-shape [island discriminant](/wiki/predictions/island-discriminant) and relic abundance by misalignment — and the one loophole left open.

[Dark-sector candidates](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates) states the program's answer to what dark matter is — χ particles or an X-condensate — together with the abundance question both share, with [relic abundance mechanisms](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/relic-abundance) and [thermal freeze-out](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/thermal-freeze-out) as its established background, and the retired third candidate, superheavy solitons, with its obituary and the review results that killed it.
