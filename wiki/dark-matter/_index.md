*Dark matter is the mass that astronomers detect only through its gravity — in the orbital speeds of stars and gas in galaxies, in the motions of galaxies inside clusters, and in the bending of light — and that emits and absorbs no light of its own. The Comatter program offers three candidates for it: particles of [conjugate matter](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter), the counterpart of ordinary matter in which the roles of position and momentum are exchanged, whose own force acts only between particles moving at nearly the same velocity; a coherent oscillation of the [momentum graviton X](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton), a massive second gravitational field that the program's [broken symmetry](/wiki/symmetry-breaking) between position and momentum adds to gravity; or, P.R.'s conjecture restored in the checkpoint's version 2.4, [magnetic monopoles of the conjugate sector, abundant in momentum space](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/momentum-space-monopoles) and held there by topology. The first and the third are the two forms in which P.R.'s core conjecture, that dark matter is conjugate matter, stands. This section holds the established astrophysics those candidates are tested against, then the program's own claims in the order they were derived: the [velocity gate](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/velocity-gate), the test that killed the program's [founding image](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/founding-image) of dark matter clumping by velocity, and the candidates. All three candidates are open, and none explains the observed ratio of dark to ordinary matter. The third is untested: #14's model of it, which failed, described the monopoles with ordinary kinematics.*

## Map

The first two subsections are established literature; the last three are the program's own claims, in the order the program derived them. The machine-checked numbers of this section belong to the soliton review ([the soliton-obituary script](/wiki/method/verification/soliton-obituary-script)) and to the two notes of 2026-10-28, which recompute the benchmark's σ₀/m, calc note #2's shortfall ratios and their own results ([the velocity-gated-gravity script](/wiki/method/verification/velocity-gated-gravity-script); [the gate-mechanism script](/wiki/method/verification/gate-mechanism-script)); the record's other gate and clumping numbers have no script.

[Halo astrophysics](/wiki/dark-matter/halo-astrophysics) comes first because every dark-matter claim of the program is tested against it. It covers how fast a halo's matter moves, [the velocity dispersion σ](/wiki/dark-matter/halo-astrophysics/velocity-dispersion) against which the program's gate is measured, and what galaxies and clusters show about whether dark matter scatters off itself: [self-interacting dark matter](/wiki/dark-matter/halo-astrophysics/self-interacting-dark-matter) and its [transfer cross-section](/wiki/dark-matter/halo-astrophysics/transfer-cross-section), the cores of [dwarf galaxies](/wiki/dark-matter/halo-astrophysics/dwarf-galaxies) and the ultra-faint dwarfs, Segue 1 among them, the cluster collision called [the Bullet Cluster](/wiki/dark-matter/halo-astrophysics/bullet-cluster), and the [stellar streams and dynamical friction](/wiki/dark-matter/halo-astrophysics/halo-substructure) that probe a halo's small-scale structure.

[Wave dark matter](/wiki/dark-matter/wave-dark-matter) is the physics of dark matter light enough to behave as a classical wave: when that happens (the [occupation number](/wiki/dark-matter/wave-dark-matter/occupation-number)), how such waves condense into [Bose stars](/wiki/dark-matter/wave-dark-matter/wave-kinetics), when [attractive condensates](/wiki/dark-matter/wave-dark-matter/attractive-condensates) collapse, how such a field is [produced](/wiki/dark-matter/wave-dark-matter/misalignment-mechanism), and [how light it may be](/wiki/dark-matter/wave-dark-matter/fuzzy-dark-matter-bounds). It is the background for the wave branch of the momentum-clumping test.

[Conjugate matter and the velocity gate](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter) begins the program's claims. It is the subject of [the gate problem](/wiki/history/main-problems): the conjugate matter field χ, the gate that lets χ particles interact only at small relative velocity, the mechanism that, in the record, delivers the gate through exchange of the momentum graviton, which a note of 2026-10-28 finds cannot produce it (flag 55, awaiting Paul's ruling), what kind of field could carry such a gate at all, the resulting knee in the scattering cross-section, the benchmark that fixes its strength, and two open problems.

[Momentum clumping](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping) is the test of the founding image — dark matter clumping by velocity rather than by position — and its obituary. For particles the mean-field attraction falls short by 28–31 orders of magnitude, and collective behavior requires a wave; on [the wave island](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/wave-island), clumping is fatal where it would have been mandatory and optional elsewhere. It also records what survived the test — the knee-shape [island discriminant](/wiki/predictions/island-discriminant) and relic abundance by misalignment — the one loophole left open, whose candidate since v2.4 is P.R.'s conjecture of monopoles abundant in momentum space, and the scope of the obituary: it covers the program's contact-range interaction, while a long-range attraction gated in relative velocity, P.R.'s model of 2026-10-26, is a conjecture outside the record that would be decorative at the program's gate width.

[Dark-sector candidates](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates) states the program's three answers to what dark matter is — χ particles, an X-condensate, or [magnetic monopoles abundant in momentum space](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/momentum-space-monopoles), P.R.'s conjecture restored in v2.4 — together with the abundance question all three share, with [relic abundance mechanisms](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/relic-abundance) and [thermal freeze-out](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/thermal-freeze-out) as its established background, and [#14's model of the third candidate](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate), superheavy solitons described with ordinary kinematics, with the obituary of its failure and the review results behind it.
