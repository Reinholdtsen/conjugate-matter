*Halo substructure is the small-scale lumpiness of a galaxy's dark-matter halo, and two kinds of observation probe it. Cold stellar streams — long, thin trails of stars pulled off globular clusters or small galaxies by the Milky Way's tides — are disturbed by any lump of dark matter that passes through them, which leaves gaps and kinks along the stream. Dynamical friction — the drag a massive body feels from the gravitational wake it raises in a sea of lighter particles — makes globular clusters sink toward the center of a dwarf galaxy, at a rate set by the dark matter's distribution. The program's [lab notebook](/wiki/history/documents/lab-notebook) used both as tests of its [founding image](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/founding-image) of dark matter clumping by velocity; both tests are withdrawn.*

**Status:** Established — [T], explained here from general knowledge — the program used these probes for old rows 3 and 4 of the lab notebook's predictions table, which are "permanently withdrawn, on both islands" (`notes/debt3c-ufd-endpoint.md`).

## Key facts

- **Streams as detectors [T].** A dynamically cold stream keeps the imprint of small perturbations for a long time. In cold dark matter (CDM), compact subhalos crossing a stream leave localized gaps; a halo with a different small-scale structure leaves a different pattern of disturbance along the stream. The lab notebook named Palomar 5 among its target streams, and Gaia's astrometry and the Rubin Observatory's imaging as the data.
- **Dynamical friction [T].** A massive body moving through lighter particles pulls them into a wake behind it, and the wake's gravity decelerates the body, which spirals inward. In a dwarf galaxy with a cuspy halo, globular clusters should sink to the center within the galaxy's lifetime; the Fornax dwarf still has globular clusters orbiting away from its center, a long-standing puzzle. The lab notebook predicted "**suppressed dynamical friction**" in condensed cores, adding that "the Fornax globular-cluster timing problem is a feature, not a bug" (#1 §5.2).
- **The two predictions, verbatim.** The lab notebook's old rows 3 and 4 (#1 §9; columns: observable, standard expectation, requirement, kill condition):

```text

    correlated perturbations of cold stellar streams (GD-1, Pal 5) vs. ΛCDM's localized subhalo gaps
  | Gaia DR4/Rubin stream power spectra consistent with smooth-halo + point-subhalo model only
4 | Dynamical friction in dwarf cores | GCs sink (cuspy CDM)
  | Suppressed in condensed cores; Fornax GCs stay put
  | Demonstrated normal friction in cored dwarfs
```
