*A dark-matter halo is the extended cloud of dark matter in which a galaxy or a galaxy cluster sits; it outweighs the visible stars and gas and sets their orbits. How dense a halo is at its center, how fast its matter moves, and how lumpy it is on small scales all depend on whether dark matter scatters off itself, and the observations of dwarf galaxies, galaxy clusters and stellar streams constrain each of these. This section is established literature: the observations and models against which the Broken-Born program's dark-matter claims are tested.*

**Literature only:** all three pages are established physics, so the section ranks no claims of the program's own; those are tracked in the registers of [claims that stand](/wiki/status/alive), [the graveyard](/wiki/status/graveyard), [open problems and the queue](/wiki/status/open-problems), [conjectures](/wiki/status/conjectures) and [superseded statements](/wiki/status/superseded). Where a page says how the program uses a result, it gives the program's tags; no `verify/` script covers any number in this section, and the program's observational inputs (the dwarf-core value σ₀/m ≈ 30 cm²/g, the Segue 1 data) are uncited in every source. One superseded input is folded into a page here: [Debt 3b's ultra-faint-dwarf row](/wiki/dark-matter/halo-astrophysics/dwarf-galaxies#superseded-debt-3bs-ultra-faint-dwarf-row), replaced by Segue 1's values.

## Map

The three pages come first in [Dark matter](/wiki/dark-matter) because the program's claims are stated against them. They run from the quantity the program's gate modifies, through the galaxies that fixed and then tested its numbers, to the probes of the program's withdrawn predictions.

[Self-interacting dark matter](/wiki/dark-matter/halo-astrophysics/self-interacting-dark-matter) comes first. It explains how self-scattering turns a halo's central cusp into a core, why the observable is the momentum-transfer cross-section per unit mass σ_T/m, and how its speed dependence follows from the mediator: constant and isotropic for a contact force, a v⁻⁴ fall with a Coulomb logarithm and forward peaking for a light Yukawa mediator. Those are the standard expectations of [row 1](/wiki/predictions/self-interaction-knee) and [row 2](/wiki/predictions/scattering-isotropy). It covers the Born regime, gravothermal evolution, whose elastic relaxation keeps a halo's mass, and the Bullet Cluster. It then shows where the program departs: its amplitude keeps the Yukawa propagator in the momentum transfer and adds a [velocity gate](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/velocity-gate) in the incoming relative momentum, which gives a knee in σ_T(v) and isotropy below it.

[Dwarf galaxies: cores and ultra-faint dwarfs](/wiki/dark-matter/halo-astrophysics/dwarf-galaxies) follows with the observations the program used. It sets out the cusp–core problem and its two families of explanation, dark-matter physics and baryonic feedback, and shows where each of the program's dark-matter options, alive or dead, gets its cores. The classical dwarfs fix the program's [scattering benchmark](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/benchmark) at σ₀/m ≈ 30 cm²/g. The ultra-faint dwarfs, with Segue 1 as the test case, falsified [Scenario A](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/wave-island/scenario-a) of the wave island; their inputs are uncited and are to be source-checked before external use.

[Halo-substructure probes: streams and dynamical friction](/wiki/dark-matter/halo-astrophysics/halo-substructure) closes the section with cold stellar streams such as GD-1 and Palomar 5, which record the passage of dark subhalos, and with dynamical friction on the globular clusters of the Fornax dwarf. These were the probes of the lab notebook's rows 3 and 4, the velocity granularity and suppressed friction of the [founding image](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/founding-image). The page records how [Debt 3](/wiki/history/debts) withdrew both rows; the obituaries are [old row 3](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/velocity-granularity) and [old row 4](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/fornax-friction).
