*The topological loophole is the route that [the clumping problem](/wiki/history/main-problems) leaves open for localization in momentum space: not a collective clumping of many particles, which [the net theorem](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/net-theorem) rules out as a requirement, but a single object, such as a [topological soliton](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/topological-defects#topological-and-non-topological-solitons-t), whose own structure would localize it in momentum. The clumping problem's calculations cover collective dynamics only, so they do not test this case, and no other calculation has. In [the checkpoint](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) the loophole's candidate is P.R.'s conjecture that [magnetic monopoles of the conjugate sector are abundant in momentum space](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/momentum-space-monopoles), held there by topology. The withdrawn manuscript #14, which modeled that candidate, described the objects with ordinary kinematics and [failed on its abundance arithmetic](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/obituary); it never treated them as localized in momentum, so the loophole stays untested. One form of P.R.'s [magnetic-mirror conjecture](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification#shared-photon), in which magnetically charged partners of our charged particles carry magnetic charge of our own photon, needs localization in momentum too.*

**Status:** Open — untested; its candidate is P.R.'s conjecture [O] of [conjugate monopoles abundant in momentum space](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/momentum-space-monopoles). #11 §12 says that it "remains untested; its candidate is P.R.'s conjecture of conjugate monopoles (table above)"; #11 §14 item 8 carries p-localization as open and tags it [O]: "p-local solitons are p-localized". No calculation has been made, and no item of the current work queue addresses it. Listed among [open problems](/wiki/status/open-problems); it qualifies the negative results on [velocity clumping](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/founding-image), the founding image, which is P.R.'s open conjecture.

## The statement

#11 App. A audits P.R.'s [founding suppositions](/wiki/program/founding-suppositions). Item 3, "Matter clumps in momentum space", is open, P.R.'s conjecture [O] ([Conjecture: velocity clumping, the founding image](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/founding-image)): as collective dynamics it "fails for particle dark matter by 10²⁸–10³¹ (theory) and, where mandatory for wave dark matter (Scenario A, under review), against the faintest dwarfs (data), and it is optional for a free-coupling wave" (§9). The item's last clause is the loophole: "topological p-localization of individual solitons untested; its candidate, p-localized conjugate monopoles, is P.R.'s open conjecture" (§12). Scenario A is the case in which clumping would be mandatory for wave dark matter ([Under review: Scenario A and the ultra-faint-dwarf endpoint](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/wave-island/scenario-a)).

#11 §12 lists the loophole among what survives the failure of #14's model: "The clumping loophole — topological momentum-space localization of individual objects — remains untested; its candidate is P.R.'s conjecture of conjugate monopoles (table above)." [The handoff](/wiki/history/documents/handoff) states it as "Loophole: *topological* p-localization of individual solitons untouched" (`HANDOFF.md` §2). The first consolidation, #9, states the clumping problem's three results without mentioning a loophole (`notes/broken-born-program-v1.md` §7).

"p-localization" means localization in momentum p: an object whose momentum-space profile is narrow, as an ordinary particle's position-space profile is narrow.

## Why the clumping problem leaves it open

The clumping problem asked whether many dark-matter particles, or a dark-matter wave field, would gather collectively into narrow ranges of velocity. Its three calculations are a mean-field stability analysis for classical particles ([the mean-field criterion](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/instability-criterion)) and a wave-kinetic [condensation rate](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/wave-island/condensation-rate) with its endpoints ([the wave island](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/wave-island)). Each treats the dark matter as a gas or a field whose structure comes from the interactions among its parts. [The net theorem](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/net-theorem) is correspondingly a statement about collective dynamics.

A topological soliton is a different kind of object. It is a localized configuration of a field that cannot decay to the vacuum, because it carries a conserved topological charge, an integer that no continuous change of the field can alter [T]. Its shape is set by its own field equations, not by an instability of a surrounding gas. If such an object lived in a sector polarized toward momentum, its localization would be in momentum. The candidate's claim, in the checkpoint's version 2.0, was of this kind: its solitons "slip through the clumping loophole (§9), since topology, not collective dynamics, would localize them in momentum" (#11 §12). Nothing in the clumping problem addresses that case. #11 §9's opening passage records the outcome of the clumping problem as leaving "one loophole for individual knots, whose candidate, conjugate monopoles localized in momentum, is P.R.'s open conjecture (§12)".

## The candidate

#11 §12 lists three [dark-sector candidates](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates): [χ particles](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/chi-particles), a condensate of the [momentum graviton](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton), and a third that version 2.0 of the checkpoint introduced as "topological solitons of a p-local gauge sector (§11)", "stable by topological charge".

The third candidate is P.R.'s conjecture [O]. #11 §12's table lists "**Conjugate monopoles**" as "magnetically charged topological solitons of the p-polarized sector, localized in momentum (§11, §11.4)", stable by "topological charge [O]", with the distinctive handle "untested: p-localization open (§14 item 8); #14's model, with ordinary kinematics, failed (below)". #11 §11.1, on such monopoles as ontology, adds that "P.R. conjectures that they are abundant in momentum space". The candidate has its own page, [Conjecture: magnetic monopoles abundant in momentum space](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/momentum-space-monopoles). It is also the second form in which #11 App. A item 4 keeps P.R.'s core conjecture, "Dark matter is conjugate matter", alive: as χ particles (x-clumping, gated self-force) or as conjugate monopoles localized in momentum, "two of the three candidates of §12".

## #14's model, and why it did not test the loophole

The candidate was developed into a standalone proposal, #14: superheavy ['t Hooft–Polyakov monopoles](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/thooft-polyakov-monopole) of a hidden SU(2) → U(1) sector ([the hidden sector](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/hidden-sector)). It described them with ordinary kinematics: "Throughout, the hidden sector is described by ordinary relativistic quantum field theory. Whether the parent framework requires a different kinematics for momentum-polarized matter is an open question that this proposal does not use." (#14 §1). #11 §12: "It did not model the localization in momentum that v2.0 claimed."

The model's abundance followed from the [Kibble estimate](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/kibble-mechanism) of how many defects a cosmological phase transition leaves. [The round-3 blind review](/wiki/method/review/round-3) recomputed it with code: [the normalization](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/kibble-normalization) had been set too low, at minimum production the v3 benchmark is [overproduced](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/overproduction) by 2.1×10³–1.2×10⁴ [V] (gated in `verify/soliton_obituary.py`; [the soliton-obituary script](/wiki/method/verification/soliton-obituary-script)), and viable abundances need either lower masses, where the scenario is prior art, or a free [dilution factor](/wiki/cosmology/early-universe/relic-yield-and-dilution#entropy-dilution-t) that removes the mass prediction (#11 §12; [the obituary of #14's model](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/obituary)). #11 §12's verdict: "#14's model fails as a program-native proposal: with ordinary kinematics it is either overproduced or the prior-art PeV-scale scenario, to which the framework adds nothing testable." The root `CLAUDE.md` records the framework as "merely compatible with known PeV-scale topological dark matter (prior art)" ([prior art](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/prior-art)). [Round-3 Decision 2](/wiki/method/review/round-3/decisions#decision-2-14s-model-retired), "retired as a program-native proposal", covers #14's model, which used ordinary kinematics; the candidate it modeled, conjugate monopoles localized in momentum, is P.R.'s open conjecture (`review/round3-adjudication.md`, 'Decisions'; #11 §12).

The failure did not test the loophole: #14's monopoles failed on their abundance, as objects described with ordinary kinematics, not on whether topology localizes them in momentum. #11 §12's plain-language passage: "The model never treated the defects as localized in momentum, so the candidate itself has not been tested."

## p-localization and the shared-photon form

#11 §14 item 8 reads: "Charge-lattice polarization: formalize §11.3's unification, and test P.R.'s magnetic-mirror conjecture (§11.4) [O]". It carries the soliton-spectrum and p-localization items as open, since the third candidate of §12 and §11.4's shared-photon form need p-localization: "p-local solitons are p-localized" [O].

The shared-photon form of P.R.'s magnetic-mirror conjecture is the one in which the momentum-polarized analogs of our electrically charged particles carry magnetic charge of our photon ([shared photon](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification#shared-photon)). #11 §11.4: "Localized in position at dark-matter density, such objects would exceed the Parker bound at #14's benchmark masses (#14 v2 and v3 §4; not gated). Something must therefore hide them, and neither option has been computed": p-localization ("p-local solitons are p-localized" [O]), "with the spread in the objects' field profile rather than their quantum state, wider than detectors and than the coherence length of galactic magnetic fields, and decoupled from the photon–baryon plasma before recombination (†)"; or "binding into magnetically neutral composites ("heavy and bound", §11.2)". The Parker bound caps the flux of free magnetic charges by the survival of the Galaxy's magnetic field [T] ([monopole flux bounds](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-searches)).

The same form is the setting of App. A item 5's explanation of the monopole null result, P.R.'s open conjecture ([the conjecture that conjugate monopoles explain the null result](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopole-explanation)): magnetic charges of our photon in the p-polarization, "unseen because they are not localized in position" (flag 62 in `review/wiki-build-flags.md`). It requires [P4](/wiki/program/postulates/p4-polarized-matter) restated and the hiding conditions above, "none of them yet computed" (#11 §11.4).

No current queue item addresses the loophole: the root `CLAUDE.md`'s queue lists [the partially-massless comparison](/wiki/gravity/palindromic-branch/pm-comparison), [the † literature battery](/wiki/method/literature-battery) and an optional round-6 review of [the toy-model note](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/toy-models). Its list of items open from the monopole discussion and not queued includes, for the candidate and the explanation, "p-localization, and the kinematics of momentum-polarized matter, never modeled (#14 §1 assumed ordinary kinematics)".

## What remains open

- **Whether topology localizes any program-native object in momentum.** The loophole has a candidate, P.R.'s conjecture of conjugate monopoles abundant in momentum space, and no calculation has been made: neither p-localization nor the kinematics of momentum-polarized matter has been modeled (root `CLAUDE.md`, 'Since the handoff'). #11 §14 item 8 carries p-localization as "p-local solitons are p-localized" [O].
- **p-localization for the shared-photon form.** That form of the magnetic-mirror conjecture needs its analogs hidden, by p-localization, with the spread in the objects' field profile rather than their quantum state and decoupled from the photon–baryon plasma before recombination (†), or by binding into magnetically neutral composites; neither option has been computed (#11 §11.4; [what it needs](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification#what-it-needs)).
- **The other dark-sector candidates.** χ particles and [the X-condensate](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/x-condensate) are not topological objects, and the sources do not connect either one to the loophole.
