*The topological loophole is the route that [the clumping problem](/wiki/history/main-problems) leaves open for localization in momentum space: not a collective clumping of many particles, which [the net theorem](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/net-theorem) rules out as a requirement, but a single object, such as a [topological soliton](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/topological-defects#topological-and-non-topological-solitons-t), whose own structure would localize it in momentum. The clumping problem's calculations cover collective dynamics only, so they do not test this case, and no other calculation has. Since [the checkpoint](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint)'s version 2.4 the loophole has a candidate again: P.R.'s conjecture that [magnetic monopoles of the conjugate sector are abundant in momentum space](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/momentum-space-monopoles), held there by topology. The withdrawn manuscript #14, which modeled that candidate, described the objects with ordinary kinematics and [failed on its abundance arithmetic](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/obituary); it never treated them as localized in momentum, so the loophole stays untested. One form of P.R.'s [magnetic-mirror conjecture](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification#shared-photon), in which magnetically charged partners of our charged particles carry magnetic charge of our own photon, needs localization in momentum too.*

**Status:** Open — untested, and since v2.4 its candidate is P.R.'s conjecture [O] of [conjugate monopoles abundant in momentum space](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/momentum-space-monopoles). #11 §12 says that it "remains untested; its candidate is P.R.'s conjecture of conjugate monopoles (table above)"; #11 §14 item 8 reopens p-localization, which v2.1 had closed, and tags it [O]: "p-local solitons are p-localized". No calculation has been made, and no item of the current work queue addresses it. Listed among [open problems](/wiki/status/open-problems); it qualifies the death of [velocity clumping](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/founding-image).

## The statement

#11 App. A audits P.R.'s [founding suppositions](/wiki/program/founding-suppositions). Item 3, "Matter clumps in momentum space", is "**dead as dynamics** (§9); **loophole**: topological p-localization of individual solitons untested; its candidate, p-localized conjugate monopoles, is P.R.'s open conjecture (§12; v2.4)".

The current wording, in #11 §12's list of what survives the failure of #14's model: "The clumping loophole — topological momentum-space localization of individual objects — remains untested; its candidate is P.R.'s conjecture of conjugate monopoles (table above)."

"p-localization" means localization in momentum p: an object whose momentum-space profile is narrow, as an ordinary particle's position-space profile is narrow.

## Why the clumping problem leaves it open

The clumping problem asked whether many dark-matter particles, or a dark-matter wave field, would gather collectively into narrow ranges of velocity. Its three calculations are a mean-field stability analysis for classical particles ([the mean-field criterion](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/instability-criterion)) and a wave-kinetic [condensation rate](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/wave-island/condensation-rate) with its endpoints ([the wave island](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/wave-island)). Each treats the dark matter as a gas or a field whose structure comes from the interactions among its parts. [The net theorem](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/net-theorem) is correspondingly a statement about collective dynamics.

A topological soliton is a different kind of object. It is a localized configuration of a field that cannot decay to the vacuum, because it carries a conserved topological charge, an integer that no continuous change of the field can alter [T]. Its shape is set by its own field equations, not by an instability of a surrounding gas. If such an object lived in a sector polarized toward momentum, its localization would be in momentum. The v2.0 candidate's claim was of this kind: its solitons "slip through the clumping loophole (§9), since topology, not collective dynamics, would localize them in momentum" (#11 §12). Nothing in the clumping problem addresses that case. #11 §9's opening passage records the outcome of the clumping problem as leaving "one loophole for individual knots, whose candidate, conjugate monopoles localized in momentum, is P.R.'s open conjecture (§12)".

## The candidate

In version 2.0 of the checkpoint, the program listed three [dark-sector candidates](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates): [χ particles](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/chi-particles), a condensate of the [momentum graviton](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton), and "topological solitons of a p-local gauge sector (§11) as a third candidate, stable by topological charge" (#11 §12).

Version 2.4 restores the third candidate as P.R.'s conjecture [O]. #11 §12's table lists "**Conjugate monopoles** (P.R.'s conjecture; v2.4)" as "magnetically charged topological solitons of the p-polarized sector, localized in momentum (§11, §11.4)", stable by "topological charge [O]", with the distinctive handle "untested: p-localization open (§14 item 8); #14's model, with ordinary kinematics, failed (below)". #11 §11.1, on such monopoles as ontology, adds that "P.R. conjectures that they are abundant in momentum space". The candidate has its own page, [Conjecture: magnetic monopoles abundant in momentum space](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/momentum-space-monopoles). It is also the second form in which #11 App. A item 4 keeps P.R.'s core conjecture, "Dark matter is conjugate matter", alive: "as χ particles (x-clumping, gated self-force) or, since v2.4, as conjugate monopoles localized in momentum, two of the three candidates of §12".

## #14's model, and why it did not test the loophole

The candidate was developed into a standalone proposal, #14: superheavy ['t Hooft–Polyakov monopoles](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/thooft-polyakov-monopole) of a hidden SU(2) → U(1) sector ([the hidden sector](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/hidden-sector)). It described them with ordinary kinematics: "Throughout, the hidden sector is described by ordinary relativistic quantum field theory. Whether the parent framework requires a different kinematics for momentum-polarized matter is an open question that this proposal does not use." (#14 §1). #11 §12: "It did not model the localization in momentum that v2.0 claimed."

The model's abundance followed from the [Kibble estimate](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/kibble-mechanism) of how many defects a cosmological phase transition leaves. [The round-3 blind review](/wiki/method/review/round-3) recomputed it with code: [the normalization](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/kibble-normalization) had been set too low, at minimum production the v3 benchmark is [overproduced](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/overproduction) by 2.1×10³–1.2×10⁴ [V] (gated in `verify/soliton_obituary.py`; [the soliton-obituary script](/wiki/method/verification/soliton-obituary-script)), and viable abundances need either lower masses, where the scenario is prior art, or a free [dilution factor](/wiki/cosmology/early-universe/relic-yield-and-dilution#entropy-dilution-t) that removes the mass prediction (#11 §12; [the obituary of #14's model](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/obituary)). #11 §12's verdict: "#14's model fails as a program-native proposal: with ordinary kinematics it is either overproduced or the prior-art PeV-scale scenario, to which the framework adds nothing testable. Its retirement was a decision P.R. delegated (round-3 Decision 2); the model stays recorded dead until he answers the concurrence question put to him on 2026-10-29." From v2.1 to v2.3 the verdict read "Retired as a program-native candidate." The root `CLAUDE.md` records the superheavy soliton candidate of `HANDOFF.md` §2 as "**dead** as a program-native proposal", the framework being "merely compatible with known PeV-scale topological dark matter (prior art)" ([prior art](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/prior-art)), and narrowed that record on 2026-10-29: "this covers #14's model, which used ordinary kinematics; the candidate it modeled, conjugate monopoles localized in momentum, is P.R.'s open conjecture, #11 v2.4 §12". The round-3 adjudication cited the clumping obituary as a precedent for the retirement (`review/round3-adjudication.md`, 'Decisions').

The failure did not test the loophole: #14's monopoles failed on their abundance, as objects described with ordinary kinematics, not on whether topology localizes them in momentum. #11 §12's plain-language passage: "The model never treated the defects as localized in momentum, so the candidate itself has not been tested."

## Two wordings, reconciled in v2.4

From v2.1 to v2.3 the checkpoint described the loophole in two ways that it did not reconcile:

- #11 §12: "The clumping loophole — topological momentum-space localization of individual objects — remains untested, but has no candidate."
- #11 §14 item 8, on the charge-lattice open problem, ended in v2.1 and v2.2: "(The soliton-spectrum and p-localization items are closed by the §12 obituary.)" In v2.3 it ended: "(v2.1 closed the soliton-spectrum and p-localization items with the §12 obituary; §11.4's shared-photon form needs p-localization again.)"

Read together on the v2.1 wording, they said that the logical loophole was untested and that there was no candidate and no work item for it. That reading followed the conflict register's ruling ([conflicts between sources](/wiki/history/documents/source-conflicts)). Version 2.4 removes the conflict: §12 gives the loophole a candidate, and §14 item 8 now reads "8. Charge-lattice polarization: formalize §11.3's unification, and test P.R.'s magnetic-mirror conjecture (§11.4) [O]. (v2.1 closed the soliton-spectrum and p-localization items with the §12 obituary; v2.4 reopens both, since the third candidate of §12 and §11.4's shared-photon form need p-localization: "p-local solitons are p-localized" [O].)"

## p-localization and the shared-photon form

Since v2.3 the checkpoint has tied p-localization to the shared-photon form of P.R.'s magnetic-mirror conjecture, in which the momentum-polarized analogs of our electrically charged particles carry magnetic charge of our photon ([shared photon](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification#shared-photon)). #11 §11.4: "Localized in position at dark-matter density, such objects would exceed the Parker bound at #14's benchmark masses (#14 v2 and v3 §4; not gated). Something must therefore hide them, and neither option has been computed: p-localization, open since v2.0 ("p-local solitons are p-localized" [O]), with the spread in the objects' field profile rather than their quantum state, wider than detectors and than the coherence length of galactic magnetic fields, and decoupled from the photon–baryon plasma before recombination (†); or binding into magnetically neutral composites ("heavy and bound", §11.2)." The Parker bound caps the flux of free magnetic charges by the survival of the Galaxy's magnetic field [T] ([monopole flux bounds](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-searches)).

The same form is the setting of App. A item 5's explanation of the monopole null result, open since v2.4 as P.R.'s conjecture ([the conjecture that conjugate monopoles explain the null result](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopole-explanation)): magnetic charges of our photon in the p-polarization, "unseen because they are not localized in position" (flag 62 in `review/wiki-build-flags.md`). #11 §11.4: "This form is the setting of App. A item 5's explanation of the null result, open since v2.4 (flag 62): it requires P4 restated and the hiding conditions above, none of them yet computed."

No current queue item addresses the loophole: the root `CLAUDE.md`'s queue lists [the partially-massless comparison](/wiki/gravity/palindromic-branch/pm-comparison), [the † literature battery](/wiki/method/literature-battery) and an optional round-6 review of [the toy-model note](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/toy-models). Its list of items open from the monopole discussion and not queued includes, "for the restored candidate and the explanation: p-localization, and the kinematics of momentum-polarized matter, never modeled (#14 §1 assumed ordinary kinematics)".

## What remains open

- **Whether topology localizes any program-native object in momentum.** The loophole has a candidate, P.R.'s conjecture of conjugate monopoles abundant in momentum space, and no calculation has been made: neither p-localization nor the kinematics of momentum-polarized matter has been modeled (root `CLAUDE.md`, 'Since the handoff'). #11 §14 item 8 carries p-localization as "p-local solitons are p-localized" [O].
- **p-localization for the shared-photon form.** That form of the magnetic-mirror conjecture needs its analogs hidden, by p-localization, with the spread in the objects' field profile rather than their quantum state, or by binding into magnetically neutral composites; neither option has been computed (#11 §11.4; [what it needs](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification#what-it-needs)).
- **The other dark-sector candidates.** χ particles and [the X-condensate](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/x-condensate) are not topological objects, and the sources do not connect either one to the loophole.

## History

- **#9.** The first consolidation states the clumping problem's three results without mentioning a loophole (`notes/broken-born-program-v1.md` §7).
- **v2.0 and [the handoff](/wiki/history/documents/handoff).** The third candidate was added, and v2.0 also claimed that it slips through the loophole. The handoff, written while the candidate stood, lists among the dead: "Loophole: *topological* p-localization of individual solitons untouched." (`HANDOFF.md` §2).
- **v2.1, 2026-10-19.** After round 3 the candidate was retired with #14's model, a decision P.R. had delegated (`review/round3-adjudication.md`, Decision 2). #11 §12 then read "remains untested, but has no candidate", and App. A item 3 "its candidate is retired (§12)". #11 outranks the handoff, so "untouched" was superseded by "untested, but has no candidate".
- **v2.3, 2026-10-29.** §14 item 8's parenthetical became "v2.1 closed the soliton-spectrum and p-localization items with the §12 obituary; §11.4's shared-photon form needs p-localization again", when #11 recorded P.R.'s magnetic-mirror conjecture on Paul's ruling on flag 60 (`review/wiki-build-flags.md`). §12 and App. A item 3 were unchanged.
- **v2.4, 2026-10-29.** Paul directed that a claim is dead only when theory and data kill it and he concurs (root `CLAUDE.md`, 'Method'). Decision 2 was narrowed to "#14's model, which used ordinary kinematics" (`review/round3-adjudication.md`, Decision 2, "Narrowed by Paul (2026-10-29)"), and the candidate returned as P.R.'s conjecture. #11 §12, §9's opening passage, App. A item 3 and §14 item 8 took their present wording: untested, with a candidate, and p-localization reopened.
