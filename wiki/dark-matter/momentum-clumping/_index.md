*Momentum clumping is the program's founding picture of [dark matter](/wiki/dark-matter): instead of gathering into lumps in space, dark matter would gather into a few streams whose members share nearly the same velocity. Whether that happens was [the clumping problem](/wiki/history/main-problems), the third of the program's three main problems, and three short notes settled it with standard kinetic theory. The answer was no. For particle dark matter the attraction falls short by 28–31 orders of magnitude. For wave dark matter, the one scenario that made clumping mandatory predicts that the smallest galaxies lose their dark matter, and they have not; with the coupling left free, clumping is merely optional. This section holds the founding claim's obituary, the calculation that killed it, and what survived. It also holds a later question: on 2026-10-26 P.R. asked whether the obituary was premature, and a note of 2026-10-28 found that the obituary covers the program's own contact-range interaction, that it shows clumping is required nowhere rather than impossible, and that a long-range attraction gated in relative velocity, a different model, would be decorative at the program's gate width.*

## Map

The clumping problem began as item 3 of the [lab notebook](/wiki/history/documents/lab-notebook)'s open problems: the growth rate of the stream instability against orbital phase mixing, which "determines whether Prediction 3's granularity survives in Milky-Way-like halos" (`notes/broken-born-theory.md` §11). Three calc notes carried it out: `notes/stream-stability.md` ("negative on the particle island, with a structural corollary"), `notes/wave-island.md` ("rate derived, environments evaluated, verdict reached") and `notes/ufd-endpoint.md` ("Scenario A falsified; the clumping problem closed"). [Checkpoint #11](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) §9 is the current summary, tagged [D] on the particle side and [D + T] on the wave side. Since 2026-10-28 `verify/velocity_gated_gravity.py` (section D) reproduces calc note #2's two shortfall ratios, which the handoff had marked machine-verified with no script behind them; the record's tag stays [D], and no script covers the other clumping numbers (flag 16 in `review/wiki-build-flags.md`, addendum; [the velocity-gated-gravity script](/wiki/method/verification/velocity-gated-gravity-script)). The [history of the three main problems](/wiki/history/main-problems) places the clumping problem among the others. One page below is established physics and one is method; every other page is a claim of the program, alive, dead or open.

[Obituary: velocity clumping, the founding image](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/founding-image) comes first, because the death is the subject of the section. It records the lab notebook's picture of a halo breaking into discrete velocity streams, "the Jeans problem with the roles of x and v exchanged", with its analogies to superconductivity and to Berry-curvature transport; the two predictions it carried; how the clumping problem tested it; and the three verdicts that killed it.

[Vlasov stability and Landau damping](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/vlasov-stability) is the established kinetic theory the test used: the collisionless Boltzmann equation, linear response about a Maxwellian, Landau damping, the static threshold of an attractive gas and its best-known case, the Jeans instability, and Boltzmann's H-theorem. [The gated Vlasov model](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/gated-vlasov-model) is the method built on it: a Gaussian [velocity gate](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/velocity-gate) on a contact attraction, whose linear response becomes an eigenvalue problem on momentum space, bracketed by a broad-gate and a narrow-gate limit. It also holds the superseded plan to race stream growth against orbital stirring, which the threshold made moot.

[The mean-field criterion and its 10²⁸–10³¹ shortfall](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/instability-criterion) is the first result: the threshold for streams in both limits, g·n_gate ≥ m·min(σ, v\*)², and its failure by 28–31 orders of magnitude once the coupling is fixed by [dwarf-galaxy cores](/wiki/dark-matter/halo-astrophysics/dwarf-galaxies), with collisions excluded by the H-theorem. It also records the handoff's machine-check mark on these numbers, which had no script behind it until the 2026-10-28 port (flag 16 in `review/wiki-build-flags.md`), and calc note #2's narrow-gate form of the threshold, which keeps the gate width where a smooth halo keeps its dispersion and overstates the Milky Way shortfall about 3× without changing the verdict (flag 58). [The classicality theorem and the particle island](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/classicality-theorem) follows from it by a scaling argument: meeting the criterion requires masses below about 0.1 meV, and at that mass about 10²⁰ quanta share each quantum state (on the regime in which calc note #2 evaluates these numbers, see flag 19 in `review/wiki-build-flags.md`), so classical particle dark matter scatters but cannot clump, and collective behaviour belongs to waves.

[The wave island](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/wave-island) is the branch the theorem leaves open, with its own pages: the gated condensation rate, Scenario A (dead), Scenario B (quarantined) and the misalignment route to the relic density. [The net theorem](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/net-theorem) then states the outcome: velocity clumping is nowhere mandatory, "forbidden (particles), fatal (committed wave), decorative (free wave)". Its salvage, in #11 §9, is the island discriminant, now [row 3 of the predictions table](/wiki/predictions/island-discriminant), and misalignment production on the wave island.

[Conjecture: a long-range velocity-gated attraction among χ](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/long-range-gated-attraction) follows the net theorem because it asks where the theorem's scope ends. P.R.'s question of 2026-10-26, whether the obituary is premature, has an answer in `notes/velocity-gated-gravity.md` (2026-10-28, computed, not reviewed): the obituary covers the program's contact-range interaction ([what the obituary covers](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/founding-image#what-the-obituary-covers)), and an attraction among χ that acts at long range only between particles of similar velocity is a model the record does not contain. No field in the record carries it. At the record's gate width the Lyman-α forest allows ε < 0.057–0.106 of gravity's strength in the record's reading, while velocity-selected clumping needs ε > 1 at every gate width; the force could not make dwarf cores, which would need G̃/G ≈ 3.6×10³¹, so it would be decorative. Gates below about 30 km/s are a corner where N-body runs by one agent found no velocity-selected structure up to ε = 10, with one statistic not adjudicated, and estimates rerun by one agent exclude ε ≈ 2 from 10 km/s up.

[Obituary: velocity-space granularity in halos (old row 3)](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/velocity-granularity) and [Obituary: Fornax friction suppression (old row 4)](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/fornax-friction) record the two predictions withdrawn with the founding image: macro-streams perturbing cold [stellar streams](/wiki/dark-matter/halo-astrophysics/halo-substructure) in the Milky Way, and superfluid-like dwarf cores in which globular clusters do not sink. [Open: the topological loophole](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/topological-loophole) comes last: a single topological object localized in momentum, which the clumping problem did not test and which no longer has a candidate. #11 §12 calls it untested, while §14 item 8 records that v2.1 closed the p-localization items and, since v2.3, that the shared-photon form of P.R.'s [magnetic-mirror conjecture](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification#shared-photon) needs p-localization again; the checkpoint does not reconcile the two.

## Boundaries

- [Vlasov stability](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/vlasov-stability) is the one piece of established physics kept here, because only the clumping problem uses it. The rest is elsewhere: halo observations (dwarf galaxies, ultra-faint dwarfs and Segue 1, stellar streams, dynamical friction) are in [halo astrophysics](/wiki/dark-matter/halo-astrophysics), and ultralight-dark-matter physics ([occupation number](/wiki/dark-matter/wave-dark-matter/occupation-number), [wave kinetics](/wiki/dark-matter/wave-dark-matter/wave-kinetics), [attractive condensates](/wiki/dark-matter/wave-dark-matter/attractive-condensates), [misalignment](/wiki/dark-matter/wave-dark-matter/misalignment-mechanism), [the bounds on fuzzy dark matter](/wiki/dark-matter/wave-dark-matter/fuzzy-dark-matter-bounds)) is in [wave dark matter](/wiki/dark-matter/wave-dark-matter).
- The gate itself, its mechanism and [the scattering benchmark](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/benchmark) whose coupling the clumping problem used are in [conjugate matter and the velocity gate](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter).
- The island discriminant salvaged from the clumping problem is a test, [row 3 of the predictions table](/wiki/predictions/island-discriminant); the renumbering that turned the old rows 3 and 4 into withdrawn rows is in [the history of the predictions table](/wiki/predictions/table-history).
- [The soliton candidate](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate) that once occupied the topological loophole belongs to [the dark-sector candidates](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates), with its own [obituary](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/obituary).
- The three notes as documents are described in [the clumping calc notes](/wiki/history/documents/clumping-notes), and every dead claim in this section is listed in [the graveyard](/wiki/status/graveyard).
