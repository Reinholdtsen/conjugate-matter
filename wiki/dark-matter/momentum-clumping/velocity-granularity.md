*Velocity-space granularity was the lab notebook's prediction that the dark matter of a relaxed galaxy halo, such as the Milky Way's, is split into of order ten broad streams, each with its own velocity and coherent over kiloparsec distances, and that their gravity leaves correlated ripples along thin streams of stars. It was row 3 of the notebook's prediction table and one of its three flagship discriminants. It rested on the [founding image](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/founding-image) of velocity clumping, so when Debt 3 showed that clumping is impossible for particle dark matter and never mandatory for wave dark matter, the row was withdrawn permanently, on both islands. The current table's row 3 is a different prediction, the island discriminant.*

**Status:** Dead — "Rows 3 and 4 of the predictions table are permanently withdrawn, on both islands" (`notes/debt3c-ufd-endpoint.md`); #11 §13 lists "velocity-space granularity in halos" under "Withdrawn with cause". Killed by [the net theorem](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/net-theorem). Home section: [momentum clumping](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping); listed in [the graveyard](/wiki/status/graveyard).

## Key facts

- **The row as written** (`notes/broken-born-theory.md` §9, row 3). Observable: the dark matter's "velocity-space structure in relaxed halos", where the standard expectation is a "Smooth f(v) + sub-percent accretion streams". Requirement: "Fragmentation into O(10) macro-streams of width ~v\*, coherent over kpc; long-wavelength correlated perturbations of cold stellar streams", in contrast with the localized subhalo gaps of the standard cold-dark-matter picture ([halo-substructure probes](/wiki/dark-matter/halo-astrophysics/halo-substructure)). Kill condition: stream power spectra from Gaia's fourth data release and the Rubin Observatory "consistent with smooth-halo + point-subhalo model only".
- **Its standing** (#1 §9). "Rows 1, 3, and 7 are the flagship discriminants: no mainstream framework predicts an exponential scattering cliff, gravitationally-detectable macro-granularity of the local velocity distribution, and a guaranteed-null direct-detection program."
- **Its basis** (#1 §5.2). The fragmentation of a Maxwellian "into discrete velocity streams of width Δv ~ v\*", in a section tagged "[D at mean-field level; O beyond]".
- **Withdrawn on the particle island** (3a). "Rows 3 (velocity granularity) and 4 (condensed cores / Fornax friction suppression) are withdrawn, and with them the mechanism-level linkage claimed between them" ([the mean-field criterion](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/instability-criterion)).
- **Withdrawn on both islands.** 3b: row 3 as written "is dead on both islands as a mandatory signature". 3b's "surviving descendant of old rows 3–4", an ultra-faint-dwarf signature, died with [Scenario A](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/wave-island/scenario-a), and 3c withdrew both rows permanently. Under [Scenario B](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/wave-island/scenario-b) the granularity survives only as "an option — a degeneration flag" (3b).
- **In the later tables.** #9 §9: "Withdrawn with cause, permanently: velocity-space granularity in halos; Fornax friction suppression (rows 3–4 of earlier versions; obituary in §7)." #11 §13 keeps the line. In #9 and #11, row 3 is [the island discriminant](/wiki/predictions/island-discriminant), so this page calls the notebook's row old row 3 ([history of the predictions table](/wiki/predictions/table-history)). Its companion is [old row 4](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/fornax-friction).
