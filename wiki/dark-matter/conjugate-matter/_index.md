*Conjugate matter, written χ, is the program's mirror-polarized matter: the counterpart of ordinary matter in which the roles of position and momentum are exchanged. It gravitates exactly like ordinary matter, so it falls, orbits and bends light as dark matter does, but its own self-interaction acts only between particles moving at nearly the same velocity. In signal terms, an ordinary contact force acts where two waveforms overlap; conjugate matter's force acts only where their spectra overlap as well. That restriction is the velocity gate, the subject of the program's [Debt 2](/wiki/history/debts), and this section holds its microphysics: what the gate is, what carries it, the scattering law it produces, the benchmark that fixes its strength, and two open questions.*

## Map

All seven pages are the program's own claims, tagged [D] or [O], derived in [the lab notebook](/wiki/history/documents/lab-notebook) and [the Debt-1 calc notes](/wiki/history/documents/debt-1-notes) and summarized in [the checkpoint](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) (#11 §8); none is machine-checked. The established physics they rest on is [self-interacting dark matter](/wiki/dark-matter/halo-astrophysics/self-interacting-dark-matter) and [the momentum graviton X](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton). The postulate that gives χ its own polarization is [P4](/wiki/program/postulates/p4-polarized-matter).

[The velocity gate](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/velocity-gate) comes first: the self-interaction amplitude with its gate F(k²/μ²), which depends on the incoming relative momentum k, traced from the v0.1 Gaussian postulate to the current form.

[Dual stress T̃](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/dual-stress) defines the quantity through which matter couples to X, the momentum-side counterpart of stress-energy. It comes before the mechanism because the mechanism is exchange of X between two dual stresses.

[The gate mechanism: X exchange over fiber overlap](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/gate-mechanism) derives the gate: integrating out the massive X leaves a contact interaction that acts only over the overlap of two χ quanta's momentum-space wavepackets, with μ the width of a χ quantum in momentum.

[The gated scattering law σ_T(v)](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/scattering-law) is the observable consequence: a knee at the gate velocity v\*, isotropic scattering below it, and a tail whose shape diagnoses the vertex. It records the downgrade from the lab notebook's exponential cliff to the knee.

[The scattering benchmark](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/benchmark) fixes the gate's strength from dwarf-galaxy cores, giving m_χ ≈ 70 MeV·α̃^{2/3}(σ₀/m ÷ 30 cm²g⁻¹)^{−1/3}; its other numbers exist only in the lab notebook and are unverified.

[Open: the gate shape](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/gate-shape) and [Open: the dual-stress enhancement](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/dual-stress-enhancement) close the section. The first asks whether the gate's tail is Gaussian or a power law; the second asks why χ scatters off itself far more strongly than ordinary matter couples to X.
