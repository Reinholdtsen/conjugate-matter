*The X-condensate candidate is the proposal that [dark matter](/wiki/dark-matter) is a coherent, spatially uniform oscillation of X, the program's [momentum graviton](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton): the massive spin-2 field that the broken [reciprocity](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/born-reciprocity) adds to gravity. If it holds, dark matter and the hidden half of the geometry are one field rather than two separate things. The idea was logged during the [Debt-1](/wiki/history/debts) work as a direction deliberately left unopened, became one of three dark-matter candidates in version 2.0 of the checkpoint, and has been one of two since [the third](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/obituary) was retired. The record holds its profile and its history, not a calculation; like [the χ-particle candidate](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/chi-particles), its abundance is unexplained.*

**Status:** Alive — one of two candidates; #11 §12 tags the pair "[D — framework; each candidate O]". The field it is made of is derived ([D + T], #11 §7.1); that the dark matter is made of it is open. Its lineage, [bigravity dark matter](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/bigravity-dark-matter), is cited from model memory [23†]; flag 20 in `review/wiki-build-flags.md` suspects that #11's entry for it pairs one paper's title with another's arXiv number. Nothing on this page is machine-checked.

## The candidate

[Checkpoint #11](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) §12 lists two candidates for dark matter, each with its origin, what makes it stable, and the observable that would single it out. The second row:

| Candidate | Origin | Stability | Distinctive handle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **X-condensate** | oscillating massive spin-2 (bigravity-DM lineage [23†]) | m_X-protected | unifies dark sector with geometry; coherent-field signatures; knee shape wave-like |

DM there is dark matter. The tag "[D — framework; each candidate O]" says that the framework producing the candidates is derived and each candidate is open. The other row is [χ particles](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/chi-particles), and "The 5.4 : 1 abundance question (polarization-genesis) applies to both" (#11 §12).

## What X is

The program's geometry lives in one field on phase space, the [modular field](/wiki/phase-space/modular-field/modular-field-z) Z = X + iY. Its imaginary part is the spacetime metric, Im Z = Y = g. Its real part is "Re Z = X, a *new symmetric tensor*: the momentum graviton. It is exactly the fluctuation that breaks η-compatibility — i.e., the local wobble of the emergent polarization" (#5 §C). X is the field that measures how the split of phase space into position and momentum varies from place to place.

Debt 1 established what kind of field X is ([why X must be massive](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/forcing-chain)). In #11's summary [D + T]: X is [shift-symmetric](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/shift-symmetry) and so "never auxiliary"; its local symmetry makes it "a second linearized-diff gauge field", that is, a second graviton; a second massless graviton is excluded by [the multi-graviton no-go](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/multi-graviton-no-go) [8†]; and "The polarization choice spontaneously breaks the dual diffs; X eats the four Goldstones and emerges massive, M_X = the polarization-breaking scale" (#11 §7.1; [the gravitational Higgs mechanism](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/gravitational-higgs)). X is therefore a massive spin-2 field with five polarizations, governed by [Hassan–Rosen bimetric gravity](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/hassan-rosen-bimetric) restricted to the [palindromic branch](/wiki/gravity/palindromic-branch/palindrome).

The properties that matter for a dark-matter candidate are in #11 §7.5 [D]:

- **Mass.** "Five βₙ → two parameters (M_X ∝ 2(β₁+β₂); one cubic shape)". No document gives a value for M_X. [Calc note #8](/wiki/history/documents/debt-1-notes) §3, the source of the count, writes the proportionality for the squared [Fierz–Pauli](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/fierz-pauli) mass, "m_FP² ∝ β₁ + 2β₂ + β₃ = 2(β₁+β₂), i.e. **M_X**"; the difference is recorded in `review/wiki-build-flags.md` item 5, and this page quotes both as written ([spectrum, parameters and reciprocity parity](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/spectrum-and-parity)).
- **Parity.** The massless graviton is "reciprocity-even" and X is "reciprocity-odd"; "Reciprocity is a selection rule on vertices" (#8 §3).
- **Couplings to ordinary matter.** "SM couplings gravitational-strength and Yukawa-cut at 1/M_X" (SM is the Standard Model). With equal effective Planck masses, "SM matter — coupled to g — sees both eigenstates with comparable strength; safety is by **range**, not decoupling" (#8 §3; [safety by range](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/fifth-force-safety)).

## What "condensate" means here

Calc note #6 states the idea in one sentence: "homogeneous oscillations of a massive spin-2 X are themselves a dark-matter candidate (cf. bigravity dark matter)" (#6 §4). A scalar field that oscillates uniformly through space at a frequency set by its mass behaves, averaged over the oscillation, as pressureless matter, its energy diluting with the cube of the scale factor like cold dark matter [T]; for a light scalar this is [the misalignment mechanism](/wiki/dark-matter/wave-dark-matter/misalignment-mechanism). The uniform mode of a spin-2 field is a tensor and singles out directions in space; that case and its literature are on [bigravity dark matter](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/bigravity-dark-matter). Such a state is coherent: the field moves in phase everywhere, like a single standing wave, rather than as a gas of independent quanta [T].

The program's record does not go further. No document computes how the oscillation would be produced in the early universe, its amplitude, or its energy density.

## The three handles

The last column of the table names three distinguishing features. #11 states them and does not derive them; what the record says about each:

- **"unifies dark sector with geometry".** Calc note #6: "BBT may not need χ as a separate field — X-condensate dark matter would unify the geometric sector with the dark sector" (#6 §4; BBT is the Broken-Born theory). #9 phrased it as "potentially eliminating χ and unifying the dark sector with the geometry" (#9 §10 item 7). If the candidate holds, the dark matter is a state of the geometric field X, not a separate matter field.
- **"coherent-field signatures".** Not specified further in any document. [Row 11](/wiki/predictions/dark-sector-microstructure) relies on "coherence signatures", with rows 1–3, to separate particles from a condensate.
- **"knee shape wave-like".** #11 gives no derivation for this entry. In [the predictions table](/wiki/predictions) the wave-like tail of [the self-interaction knee](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/scattering-law) is "(v\*/σ)⁴", derived for wave χ on [the wave island](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/wave-island) (#11 §9; [row 3](/wiki/predictions/island-discriminant)). No document derives a knee for a condensate of X or relates it to wave χ.

The stability entry, "m_X-protected", is likewise stated without an argument. No document computes a lifetime for the oscillation.

## Its tests

- **[Row 7](/wiki/predictions/direct-detection-null): a permanent null.** "**Permanent null** (both dark-sector candidates SM-decoupled; P4 strict)", killed by "Any confirmed non-gravitational DM–SM coupling" (#11 §13). X does couple to ordinary matter, at gravitational strength and with a range cut off at 1/M_X (#11 §7.5); the null concerns couplings other than gravity.
- **[Row 11](/wiki/predictions/dark-sector-microstructure): particles or condensate.** "Particles vs condensate, separable via rows 1–3 + coherence signatures", marked "Diagnostic" (#11 §13). How to make the separation is open: "Discriminating the two remaining dark-sector candidates (§12) [O]" (#11 §14 item 9).

## What is open

- **Abundance.** "The 5.4 : 1 abundance question (polarization-genesis) applies to both" (#11 §12; [Open: polarization-genesis and the 5.4 : 1 ratio](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/polarization-genesis)).
- **Telling it from χ.** #11 §14 item 9, above.
- **Cosmological stability of X.** Reciprocity forces equal effective Planck masses, which "rules out the M_f ≪ M_g escape" in which a heavy spin-2 field has been shown linearly stable at late times ([bimetric cosmology and its instabilities](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/bimetric-cosmology)); whether "a large mass alone is enough at M_f = M_g is open" (`review/v3-worklog.md`, item R7; [Open: stability at equal Planck masses](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/equal-planck-mass-stability)). The question concerns X itself, and through it this candidate.
- **No calculation yet.** Production, amplitude, lifetime and the coherence signatures are all uncomputed.

## Not the same condensate

The word "condensate" appears twice in the dark-matter record. On the wave island of Debt 3, very light χ can condense into Bose stars ([wave kinetics and Bose-star formation](/wiki/dark-matter/wave-dark-matter/wave-kinetics)). Those are condensates of the matter field χ. The X-condensate is a state of the geometric field X. No document relates the two.
