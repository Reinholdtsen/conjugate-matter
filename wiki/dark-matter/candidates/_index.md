*The dark-sector question asks what dark matter is made of, within the program's framework. The program gives two answers, each still open: particles of conjugate matter χ, the mirror-polarized counterpart of ordinary matter, or a coherent oscillation of X, the massive momentum graviton that the broken symmetry adds to gravity. Version 2.0 of the checkpoint listed a third answer, superheavy topological solitons of a hidden gauge sector; a review that re-ran every number in code found its abundance estimate misnormalized, and it was retired on 2026-10-19. Both remaining answers share one unsolved question: what sets the observed ratio of dark to ordinary matter, 5.4 : 1.*

**Stands:** [the χ-particle candidate](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/chi-particles), [the X-condensate candidate](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/x-condensate), and two results that outlived the retired candidate, [overproduction at the benchmark](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/overproduction) and [W′ overclosure](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/w-prime-overclosure) · **Died:** [superheavy conjugate solitons](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/obituary), with its components [the hidden SU(2) → U(1) proposal](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/hidden-sector), [the coupling α_d ≈ 34](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/alpha-d-coupling), [the Kibble normalization](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/kibble-normalization), [the √N topological floor (chat-only)](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/sqrt-n-floor), [the ψ relic window](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/psi-relic-window) and [Planck-suppressed adjoint mixing](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/dimension-five-operator) · **Open:** [polarization-genesis and the 5.4 : 1 ratio](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/polarization-genesis)

## Map

Checkpoint #11 §12 is the current statement of the question. It turns version 2.0's three-way choice into "two program-native candidates", tagged "[D — framework; each candidate O]": the framework that produces them is derived, and each candidate is open. Two pages below are established literature, and the last child is a retired candidate; every other page is a claim of the program.

[Relic abundance mechanisms](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/relic-abundance) comes first because every candidate has to meet it. It is the established physics of how a relic density is set: thermal freeze-out, cannibal and asymmetric dark matter, freeze-in, and the Sakharov conditions for baryogenesis.

[The χ-particle candidate](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/chi-particles) is the program's first answer: particles of the momentum-polarized matter field χ, with assumed stability, that fall, orbit and lens like ordinary dark matter. Its distinctive handles are the knee in the scattering cross-section at v\* with a sharp tail, isotropic scattering below the gate, and a permanent null in direct detection. Its microphysics belongs to the next section up.

[Bigravity dark matter](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/bigravity-dark-matter) is established literature placed before the second candidate because it is that candidate's lineage: the massive spin-2 field of bimetric gravity proposed as the dark matter by Babichev et al. [23†].

[The X-condensate candidate](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/x-condensate) is the program's second answer: a coherent oscillation of the massive momentum graviton X, "m_X-protected", which would make dark matter part of the geometry. The Debt-1 notes logged it as a new door "deliberately left closed"; it became a candidate in #11 v2.0.

[Open: polarization-genesis and the 5.4 : 1 ratio](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/polarization-genesis) follows the two candidates because it qualifies both. Thermal freeze-out fails for a light, strongly self-interacting sector connected to ordinary matter only by gravity, so the ratio Ω_χ : Ω_b = 5.4 : 1 needs a mechanism, the program's analog of baryogenesis; it is #11 §14 item 7.

[The soliton candidate (retired)](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate) comes last. It is version 2.0's third candidate, superheavy 't Hooft–Polyakov monopoles of a hidden SU(2) → U(1) sector, which was written up as the standalone manuscript #14, reviewed blind three times and retired after the third review. The subsection opens with the obituary and records each failed component, the results that survive, and the published low-mass scenario the corrected candidate duplicates.
