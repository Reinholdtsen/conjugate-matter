*The dark-sector question asks what [dark matter](/wiki/dark-matter) is made of, within the program's framework. The program gives two answers, each still open: particles of [conjugate matter χ](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter), the mirror-polarized counterpart of ordinary matter, or a coherent oscillation of X, [the massive momentum graviton](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton) that the broken symmetry adds to gravity. Version 2.0 of the checkpoint listed a third answer, [superheavy topological solitons](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/obituary) of a hidden gauge sector; a review that re-ran every number in code found its abundance estimate misnormalized, and it was retired on 2026-10-19. Both remaining answers share one unsolved question: what sets the observed ratio of dark to ordinary matter, 5.4 : 1.*

**Stands:** [the χ-particle candidate](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/chi-particles), [the X-condensate candidate](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/x-condensate), and two results that outlived the retired candidate, [overproduction at the benchmark](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/overproduction) and [W′ overclosure](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/w-prime-overclosure) · **Died:** [superheavy conjugate solitons](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/obituary), with its components [the hidden SU(2) → U(1) proposal](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/hidden-sector), [the coupling α_d ≈ 34](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/alpha-d-coupling), [the Kibble normalization](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/kibble-normalization), [the √N topological floor (chat-only)](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/sqrt-n-floor), [the ψ relic window](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/psi-relic-window) and [Planck-suppressed adjoint mixing](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/dimension-five-operator) · **Open:** [polarization-genesis and the 5.4 : 1 ratio (OP7)](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/polarization-genesis)

## Map

[Checkpoint #11](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) §12 is the current statement of the question. It lists two candidates in a table, each with its origin, what makes it stable and the observable that would single it out, and tags the table "[D — framework; each candidate O]": the framework that produces the candidates is derived, and each candidate is open. Three pages below are established literature, one is an open problem, and the last child is a retired candidate; the two candidate pages are claims of the program.

[Relic abundance mechanisms](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/relic-abundance) comes first because every candidate has to meet it. It is the established physics of how a relic density is set, mechanism by mechanism: the [yield](/wiki/cosmology/early-universe/relic-yield-and-dilution#the-yield-t), a summary of [thermal freeze-out](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/thermal-freeze-out), freeze-out in a [sector at its own temperature](/wiki/cosmology/early-universe/hidden-sector-temperature), cannibal and asymmetric dark matter and the Sakharov conditions for baryogenesis, and freeze-in. It ends with the program's uses: the argument that χ cannot freeze out, misalignment on the wave island, and the relics of the retired candidate's hidden sector.

[Thermal freeze-out](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/thermal-freeze-out) works through the standard mechanism: the Boltzmann equation, why freeze-out comes near T ~ m/20, the s-wave relic formula exactly as `verify/soliton_obituary.py` codes it and the standard relic it is checked against, and freeze-out in a sector at its own temperature. Its program uses are the lab notebook's argument that particle χ cannot freeze out, which leaves the ratio of dark to ordinary matter open; the freeze-out relic coupled to ordinary matter against which [row 7](/wiki/predictions/direct-detection-null) predicts a permanent null; and the W′ and ψ relics of the retired candidate, the first of which survives it.

[The χ-particle candidate](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/chi-particles) is the program's first answer: particles of the momentum-polarized matter field χ, which no Standard Model force reaches under the strict form of postulate [P4](/wiki/program/postulates/p4-polarized-matter). The page sets out why χ falls, orbits and lenses like ordinary dark matter [D], summarizes [the velocity gate](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/velocity-gate) and [the knee in the scattering cross-section](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/scattering-law), and sets out its tests, rows 1, 2, 3 and 7 of [the predictions table](/wiki/predictions), with their kill conditions. It explains why the candidate is particle χ rather than [the wave branch of the clumping problem](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/wave-island), what it no longer claims, and what is open: its stability is assumed and its abundance unexplained. The gate's microphysics belongs to [conjugate matter and the velocity gate](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter).

[Bigravity dark matter](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/bigravity-dark-matter) is established literature placed before the second candidate because it is that candidate's lineage: the massive spin-2 eigenstate of a two-metric theory proposed as the dark matter by Babichev et al. [23†]. The page explains the two mass eigenstates and how the ratio of the [Planck masses](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/planck-units#two-metrics-two-planck-masses) sets their coupling to matter, why a uniform oscillation of a massive field behaves as cold matter, the check status of the single citation, and how the program's X differs: forced massive by the broken symmetry, restricted to the [palindromic branch](/wiki/gravity/palindromic-branch/palindrome), and with equal Planck masses.

[The X-condensate candidate](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/x-condensate) is the program's second answer: a coherent, spatially uniform oscillation of X, which would make dark matter a state of the geometry rather than a separate field. The page sets out what X is (the real part of [the modular field](/wiki/phase-space/modular-field/modular-field-z), massive, [reciprocity-odd](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/spectrum-and-parity), coupled to ordinary matter at gravitational strength with a finite range), what a condensate means here, and the three handles of #11's table, which are stated without derivation. It records the candidate's path from a door "deliberately left closed" in [the field-equation notes](/wiki/history/documents/field-equation-notes) to one of three candidates in #11 v2.0 and one of two since v2.1.

[Open: polarization-genesis and the 5.4 : 1 ratio (OP7)](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/polarization-genesis) follows the two candidates because it qualifies both. [The lab notebook](/wiki/history/documents/lab-notebook) argues that a light, strongly self-interacting sector connected to ordinary matter only by gravity cannot get its abundance from thermal freeze-out, so the ratio Ω_χ : Ω_b = 5.4 : 1 needs an asymmetric mechanism, the program's analog of baryogenesis. The page goes through the notebook's sketch, heavy [Grassmannian](/wiki/phase-space/modular-field/lagrangian-grassmannian) moduli decaying with a branching set by the vacuum's tilt angle, and records that a later calc note expects most of those moduli to be eliminated; it is #11 §14 item 7.

[The soliton candidate (retired)](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate) comes last. It is version 2.0's third candidate, superheavy ['t Hooft–Polyakov monopoles](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/thooft-polyakov-monopole) of a hidden SU(2) → U(1) sector, which was written up as the standalone manuscript #14, [reviewed blind](/wiki/method/review) three times and retired after the third review. The subsection opens with the obituary and records each failed component, the results that survive, and the published low-mass scenario the corrected candidate duplicates.

**How the question changed.** [The handoff](/wiki/history/documents/handoff) of 2026-10-19 still called superheavy solitons the "Current best DM candidate" (DM: dark matter) and listed "Dark-sector trilemma discrimination: particles vs X-condensate vs solitons" as open item 8 (`HANDOFF.md` §2). Both [popular articles](/wiki/history/documents/popular-articles) describe the same three-way question. After [round 3](/wiki/method/review/round-3), [Decision 2](/wiki/method/review/round-3/decisions#decision-2-the-soliton-candidate-retired) retired the solitons: "the trilemma becomes two program-native candidates (gated χ particles; the X-condensate), plus the remark that the framework is compatible with known PeV-scale topological dark matter" (`review/round3-adjudication.md`). #11 v2.1 adopted that form, and the root `CLAUDE.md` records the soliton candidate as "dead as a program-native proposal".

## Boundaries

- The microphysics of χ — the velocity gate, its mechanism, the scattering law and the benchmark — is in [conjugate matter and the velocity gate](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter); the particle and wave regimes of χ are in [momentum clumping](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping).
- Telling the two candidates apart is a test, [row 11: particles or condensate](/wiki/predictions/dark-sector-microstructure), which is also #11 §14 item 9. The null that both candidates predict for direct detection is [row 7](/wiki/predictions/direct-detection-null).
- X itself — why it is massive, its spectrum, how it couples to ordinary matter, and the open question of its stability at equal Planck masses — is in [the momentum graviton X](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton); the bimetric theory it lands on is [Hassan–Rosen bimetric gravity](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/hassan-rosen-bimetric).
- Relic production for wave χ is [relic abundance by misalignment on the wave island](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/wave-island/misalignment-relic), and the established mechanism is [the misalignment mechanism](/wiki/dark-matter/wave-dark-matter/misalignment-mechanism). The heavy moduli of the polarization-genesis sketch belong to [the field content of the broken vacuum](/wiki/phase-space/modular-field/field-content).
- Monopole physics in general is in [magnetic monopoles](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles), and the review that retired the soliton candidate is [review round 3](/wiki/method/review/round-3). The program-wide registers are [the graveyard](/wiki/status/graveyard) and [open problems](/wiki/status/open-problems).
