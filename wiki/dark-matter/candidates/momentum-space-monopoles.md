*Magnetic monopoles abundant in momentum space are P.R.'s conjecture about what [dark matter](/wiki/dark-matter) is: magnetically charged [topological solitons](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/topological-defects#topological-and-non-topological-solitons-t) of the program's momentum-polarized matter, present in abundance and localized in momentum rather than in position. A topological soliton is a localized configuration of a field held together by an integer winding that no smooth change of the field can undo, so it cannot decay; a [magnetic monopole](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles) is the standard example. Version 2.0 of [the checkpoint](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) listed such objects as its third dark-matter candidate. The withdrawn manuscript [#14](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate) then modeled the candidate as superheavy monopoles of a hidden gauge force moving in ordinary space, and [that model failed](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/obituary) in review, on its count of how many monopoles the early universe makes. The model never treated the objects as localized in momentum, so the conjecture itself was not tested. Since version 2.4 (2026-10-29) the checkpoint lists it again as the third candidate, beside [χ particles](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/chi-particles) and [the X-condensate](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/x-condensate). Nothing derives it yet: whether solitons of the momentum-polarized sector are localized in momentum at all is open.*

**Status:** Conjecture — [O] — P.R.'s; the third candidate of #11 §12 since v2.4, with its stability entered as "topological charge [O]", in a table tagged "[D — framework; each candidate O]". No derivation, calculation or script stands behind it. #14's model of it failed in [round 3](/wiki/method/review/round-3) of [blind review](/wiki/method/review) (2026-10-19) and is recorded dead; Paul's concurrence was asked on 2026-10-29, and until he answers, the death stands as recorded (root `CLAUDE.md`; [Obituary: #14's superheavy-soliton model](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/obituary)). Home: [dark-sector candidates](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates); listed with the [conjectures](/wiki/status/conjectures).

## The conjecture

Paul's statement of 2026-10-29, which restored it:

> I think you prematurely killed off the most interesting parts of the comatter conjecture: that dark matter is conjugate (momentum) matter; and that magnetic monopoles exist in abundance in momentum space. Yes, I know electric/magnetic and space/momentum are different dualities. Also, conjugate gravitational collapse drives positional expansion acceleration. Don't kill them off unless theory and data kill them off and you ask me explicitly that I concur. Do not hide the killing in a massive change.

The root `CLAUDE.md` records the statement in full ('Since the handoff', the entry "2026-10-29, later the same day"), and its 'Method' section quotes the last two sentences with the rule they set: a claim is dead only when theory and data kill it and Paul explicitly concurs, and "A failed model or version of an idea is not the death of the idea." The two other ideas the statement names are [dark matter as conjugate matter](/wiki/program/founding-suppositions#4-dark-matter-is-conjugate-matter) and [conjugate collapse powering our expansion](/wiki/cosmology/scale-factor-duality/collapse-powered-expansion).

#11 v2.4 enters the conjecture as the third row of its table of dark-matter candidates (§12):

| Candidate | Origin | Stability | Distinctive handle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Conjugate monopoles** (P.R.'s conjecture; v2.4) | magnetically charged topological solitons of the p-polarized sector, localized in momentum (§11, §11.4) | topological charge [O] | untested: p-localization open (§14 item 8); #14's model, with ordinary kinematics, failed (below) |

Its monopole audit adds the abundance and the candidacy: "P.R. conjectures that they are abundant in momentum space. Their candidacy as the dark matter is open (§12): #14's model of it failed in v2.1, and the candidate itself was never modeled" (§11.1).

The terms of the row:

- **The p-polarized sector** is the program's [conjugate matter](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter), introduced by postulate [P4](/wiki/program/postulates/p4-polarized-matter): "Matter comes in x-polarized (Standard Model) and p-polarized (χ) representations; both couple universally to H; gauge forces do not cross polarizations" [P] (#11 §4). A polarization is the choice of which half of the phase-space coordinates counts as position ([Lagrangian polarizations](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/lagrangian-polarizations)). Ordinary matter is local in position; p-polarized matter is organized the other way round.
- **Localized in momentum** means that the object's profile is narrow in momentum p, as an ordinary particle's profile is narrow in position. The two profiles are related as a waveform and its spectrum, so an object narrow in momentum, with width Δp, is spread in position by about ħ/Δp, as a narrowband signal is long in time [T]. The record writes this property as p-localization.
- **Topological charge** is the integer that makes such a soliton stable: the field far from its core wraps the space of the theory's vacuum states a whole number of times, and no smooth change of the field alters that number [T] ([the 't Hooft–Polyakov monopole](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/thooft-polyakov-monopole)). #11 tags this entry [O].
- **Abundant.** The record gives no number and no mechanism for how many such objects there are, and the abundance question is shared: "The 5.4 : 1 abundance question (polarization-genesis) applies to all three" (§12).

## Where it comes from

- **Founding supposition 5.** #11's audit of P.R.'s [founding suppositions](/wiki/program/founding-suppositions) lists "Monopoles live in conjugate space, explaining non-detection" (App. A item 5). Since v2.4 it is "**open in both halves**". As an explanation of why no monopole of ours has been found, it is P.R.'s conjecture [O] ([the explanation of the null result](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopole-explanation)). As ontology it is "alive and consequence-bearing (P.R.'s "true-but-irrelevant is measure zero"), with P.R.'s conjecture that such monopoles are abundant in momentum space (§12)". The ontology half states that conjugate-sector solitons, if they exist, gravitate, "by P4" (§11.1; [conjugate monopoles as ontology](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/conjugate-monopoles)). This conjecture adds that they exist in abundance and that they are a dark-matter candidate.
- **Founding supposition 4.** "Dark matter is conjugate matter — **alive**, P.R.'s core conjecture: as χ particles (x-clumping, gated self-force) or, since v2.4, as conjugate monopoles localized in momentum, two of the three candidates of §12" (App. A item 4). From v2.1 to v2.3 the item read "now one of two remaining candidates (§12)".
- **Founding supposition 3 and its loophole.** "Matter clumps in momentum space — **dead as dynamics** (§9); **loophole**: topological p-localization of individual solitons untested; its candidate, p-localized conjugate monopoles, is P.R.'s open conjecture (§12; v2.4)" (App. A item 3). [The clumping problem](/wiki/history/main-problems)'s calculations treat a gas of particles or a wave field, and [its result](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/net-theorem) concerns collective dynamics. A single soliton localized in momentum by its own topology is a case they do not reach ([Open: the topological loophole](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/topological-loophole)). #11 §9 ends its account of the clumping problem with "one loophole for individual knots, whose candidate, conjugate monopoles localized in momentum, is P.R.'s open conjecture (§12)". The conjecture concerns single objects. It does not reopen collective clumping in momentum space, whose recorded death stands, with Paul's concurrence asked on 2026-10-29 ([Obituary: velocity clumping, the founding image](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/founding-image)).
- **Version 2.0.** The checkpoint's version 2.0 (in git history at commit 008c3ee) listed the third candidate as "Conjugate knots": "topological solitons of a p-local gauge sector (§11)", with stability "**free** (topological charge)". Its handles were "superheavy relics; essentially collisionless; compact-object probes (lensing) if heavy" and "p-localization by topology", the clumping problem's loophole, which it tagged "[O: "solitons of p-local gauge theories are p-localized" is duality-plausible, underived]". v2.4 summarizes the claim: "v2.0 listed topological solitons of a p-local gauge sector (§11) as a third candidate, stable by topological charge. It also claimed they slip through the clumping loophole (§9), since topology, not collective dynamics, would localize them in momentum" (§12). v2.0's plain-language passage stated the second claim in a sentence: the no-clumping theorem "never touched a single object localized in momentum space because it is a knot, not a flock".
- **The magnetic analogs.** The row's origin cites §11.4, P.R.'s magnetic-mirror conjecture [O]: "momentum matter is similar to position matter, each electrically charged x-polarized particle having a magnetically charged p-polarized analog" ([Conjecture: the momentum sector as the magnetic mirror](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification)). §11.4 gives it two forms. In the [separate-photon form](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification#separate-photon) the analogs are magnetic under the p-polarized sector's own gauge field; the form "is consistent with P4 and leaves the absence of our own monopoles to §11.1". In the [shared-photon form](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification#shared-photon) they carry magnetic charge of our photon, which "P4 as written excludes"; that form is "the setting of App. A item 5's explanation of the null result". #11 does not say which form the third candidate takes.
- **A laboratory analog.** §11.1: "Their laboratory shadow is real: Weyl semimetals host catalogued families of momentum-space Berry-curvature monopoles [16†] — right geometry, different flux." In those crystals the monopoles are sources of a curvature of the electron states at points in momentum space, not magnetic charges [T] ([the laboratory shadow](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/conjugate-monopoles#the-laboratory-shadow-t)). It is an analog, not a test.
- **Its standing over time.** [The handoff](/wiki/history/documents/handoff), written before round 3, called the candidate the "Current best DM candidate" (DM: dark matter), in #14's superheavy form (`HANDOFF.md` §2). After round 3, round-3 Decision 2 retired "the soliton candidate" as a program-native proposal, a decision Paul had delegated ([round-3 decisions](/wiki/method/review/round-3/decisions)). From v2.1 to v2.3 #11 listed two candidates, §11.1 read "Their candidacy as the dark matter itself is retired in v2.1 (§12)", and §12 said the loophole "remains untested, but has no candidate". On 2026-10-29 the adjudication recorded the decision's narrowing in a note headed "Narrowed by Paul (2026-10-29)", which quotes his direction, "Don't kill them off unless theory and data kill them off and you ask me explicitly that I concur", and continues: "This decision covers #14's model, which used ordinary kinematics. The candidate #14 modeled, conjugate monopoles localized in momentum, was never tested and is P.R.'s open conjecture again (#11 v2.4 §12). #14's model stays recorded dead until Paul answers the concurrence question put to him on 2026-10-29" (`review/round3-adjudication.md`, Decision 2).

## What tested it and what did not

### #14's model, which failed

The candidate was developed into the standalone manuscript #14: "superheavy 't Hooft–Polyakov monopoles of a hidden SU(2) → U(1) sector" (#11 §12; [the hidden SU(2) → U(1) proposal](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/hidden-sector)). Its kinematics were those of ordinary particles moving in space (#14 §1):

> Kinematics. Throughout, the hidden sector is described by ordinary relativistic quantum field theory. Whether the parent framework requires a different kinematics for momentum-polarized matter is an open question that this proposal does not use.

#11 §12 draws the consequence for the claim of v2.0: "It did not model the localization in momentum that v2.0 claimed."

The model failed in round 3, a blind review with code execution, with verdict C. Its count of monopoles formed in the early universe had been normalized too low: one defect per Hubble volume gives p ≈ 104 [V], the causal minimum, where #14 v3 had used p ≈ 0.023 ([Obituary: the Kibble normalization](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/kibble-normalization)). At that minimum the benchmark of 3×10¹³ GeV is overproduced by 2.1×10³–1.2×10⁴ [V] ([overproduction at the benchmark](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/overproduction)). Hidden-sector monopoles avoid overclosing the universe only for symmetry-breaking scales ≲ 100 PeV, or with a period of early matter domination [29S], and below that scale the model is the established topological-dark-matter scenario [28S, 29S] ([topological dark matter in the literature](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/prior-art)). The numbers are gated in `verify/soliton_obituary.py`. #11 §12's verdict: "#14's model fails as a program-native proposal: with ordinary kinematics it is either overproduced or the prior-art PeV-scale scenario, to which the framework adds nothing testable." Its plain-language passage: "The model never treated the defects as localized in momentum, so the candidate itself has not been tested."

Three results outlive the model (#11 §12): "A weakly coupled hidden sector always has strongly coupled monopoles, by Dirac quantization [T]" ([Dirac quantization](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/dirac-quantization)); the minimal adjoint-only SU(2) → U(1) sector is excluded by its stable W′± ([W′ overclosure](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/w-prime-overclosure)) [V]; and "The clumping loophole — topological momentum-space localization of individual objects — remains untested; its candidate is P.R.'s conjecture of conjugate monopoles (table above)."

### What did not reach it

- **The clumping problem.** Its calculations cover collective dynamics only, so they leave the loophole the conjecture occupies untested (#11 §9, §12).
- **A calculation of p-localization.** None exists. v2.0 called the statement "duality-plausible, underived", and #11 §11.4 still calls it "open since v2.0 ("p-local solitons are p-localized" [O])".
- **Observation.** The record compares no observation with the candidate as conjectured, localized in momentum. The null result of monopole searches concerns magnetic charges of our own photon, which the objects would carry only in the shared-photon form. In that form the null result is the subject of App. A item 5's explanation, and because it is expected under several standard histories it "lends no support either way" (App. A item 5; flags 59 and 62 in `review/wiki-build-flags.md`; [row 8](/wiki/predictions/monopole-null)).

### The evidence against it

- **The one model built of it failed.** With ordinary kinematics the monopoles are overproduced at superheavy masses, they survive at those masses only with a free dilution factor that removes the mass prediction, and at low masses they are prior art to which the framework adds nothing testable (#11 §12).
- **Its central property is underived.** That solitons of the momentum-polarized sector are localized in momentum is tagged [O] and has been since v2.0.
- **The objects are unspecified.** The record does not specify the gauge content of the p-polarized sector, so it does not say which solitons the sector has: conjugate-sector solitons are objects "whose existence depends on gauge content the record does not specify" (`notes/closed-universe.md` §9 item 4; [what the objects would be](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/conjugate-monopoles#what-the-objects-would-be)).
- **In the shared-photon form,** the objects would carry magnetic charge of our photon, which P4 as written excludes. "Localized in position at dark-matter density, such objects would exceed the Parker bound at #14's benchmark masses (#14 v2 and v3 §4; not gated)" (#11 §11.4), the bound that the survival of galactic magnetic fields sets on free magnetic charges [T] ([the Parker bound](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-searches#the-parker-bound-t)). The form also conflicts outright with the stated basis of [row 7](/wiki/predictions/direct-detection-null), "both dark-sector candidates SM-decoupled; P4 strict" (#11 §13; SM is the Standard Model), whose observable null would survive only if the objects are spread in position, which no document analyzes (flag 60). In v2.0 the row's basis read "all three trilemma candidates SM-decoupled"; v2.4 left the v2.1 wording, "both", unchanged. Flag 63 in `review/wiki-build-flags.md` proposes wording for rows 7 and 11 that counts all three candidates; the rows stay as they are until Paul rules.

## What it needs

- **p-localization.** A derivation that the sector's solitons are localized in momentum: "p-local solitons are p-localized" [O], reopened in v2.4 as part of #11 §14 item 8.
- **The kinematics of momentum-polarized matter.** The root `CLAUDE.md` lists, for the restored candidate and the explanation, "p-localization, and the kinematics of momentum-polarized matter, never modeled (#14 §1 assumed ordinary kinematics)" ('Open from the monopole discussion'). [Conjugate special relativity](/wiki/conjugate-relativity), #13, treats the kinematics of the momentum polarization but not localization (flag 60).
- **The sector's gauge content,** which decides which solitons it has.
- **An abundance.** A mechanism for how many form and survive, and the 5.4 : 1 ratio of dark to ordinary matter that every candidate has to meet (#11 §12, §14 item 7).
- **A distinctive observable.** The table's handle names none: "untested". Telling the three candidates apart is #11 §14 item 9 [O].
- **For the shared-photon form,** P4 restated and something to hide the objects, of which "neither option has been computed: p-localization, open since v2.0 ("p-local solitons are p-localized" [O]), with the spread in the objects' field profile rather than their quantum state, wider than detectors and than the coherence length of galactic magnetic fields, and decoupled from the photon–baryon plasma before recombination (†); or binding into magnetically neutral composites ("heavy and bound", §11.2)" (#11 §11.4).

## Related open problems

- [Open: the topological loophole](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/topological-loophole) — the gap in the clumping result for single objects localized in momentum by topology; since v2.4 this conjecture is its candidate (#11 §9, §12, App. A item 3).
- [Open: one modular structure for both polarizations (OP8)](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/unified-modular-structure) — #11 §14 item 8: "Charge-lattice polarization: formalize §11.3's unification, and test P.R.'s magnetic-mirror conjecture (§11.4) [O]. (v2.1 closed the soliton-spectrum and p-localization items with the §12 obituary; v2.4 reopens both, since the third candidate of §12 and §11.4's shared-photon form need p-localization: "p-local solitons are p-localized" [O].)"
- [Row 11: particles or condensate (OP9)](/wiki/predictions/dark-sector-microstructure) — "Discriminating the three dark-sector candidates (§12) [O]" (#11 §14 item 9); the row itself names particles and a condensate (§13), and flag 63, pending, proposes adding the conjugate monopoles, "the third untested".
- [Open: polarization-genesis and the 5.4 : 1 ratio (OP7)](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/polarization-genesis) — the abundance question shared by all three candidates.
- [The explanation of the monopole null result](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopole-explanation) — App. A item 5's other half, open since v2.4 and set in §11.4's shared-photon form (flag 62, ruled option B by Paul's direction).
- [Open: point defects of the polarization (OP12)](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/topological-defects#open-point-defects-of-the-polarization-op12) — a different object. #11 §14 item 12 records that selecting the polarization can leave "point defects of the monopole type" of the program's own order parameter, where it ranges over the whole polarization manifold [Λ(4)](/wiki/phase-space/modular-field/lagrangian-grassmannian). The closed-universe note separates the two: "They are defects of the program's own order parameter, unlike conjugate-sector solitons, whose existence depends on gauge content the record does not specify" (`notes/closed-universe.md` §9 item 4). No document relates them to this conjecture.
