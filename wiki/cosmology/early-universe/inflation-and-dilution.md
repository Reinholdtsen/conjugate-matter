*Inflation is a proposed early period of extremely rapid, accelerating expansion, which thins any relics produced before it until essentially none remain in the observable universe; reheating is its end, when the energy that drove inflation turns into hot particles and sets the starting temperature of the radiation era. Relics made after reheating can still be thinned later, if something releases a large amount of entropy into the plasma, for example the decay of heavy particles that dominated the universe for a time. The program met this physics twice. Inflation is the standard reason no magnetic monopoles of our own sector are seen, which disposed of an early claim that the conjugate sector explained their absence. And dilution was the only way the retired soliton dark-matter candidate could have kept its heavy mass, a route the program rejected because it removes the mass prediction.*

**Status:** Established — [T] — textbook early-universe physics, written here from general knowledge; how the program used it is recorded in #11 §11.1 and §12 and in round-3 decision 2.

## Key facts

- **Inflation dilutes relics [T].** Relics formed before or during inflation are thinned by it. #11 §11.1: as an explanation of the null result, conjugate monopoles are "dead — the relevant duality is electric–magnetic (E↔B, g → 1/g), not x↔p, and inflationary dilution already accounts for absence [T]". See [the monopole problem](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-problem) and [Obituary: conjugate monopoles explain the null result](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopole-explanation).
- **Ordering and reheating [T].** Defects formed at a transition after inflation are not diluted by it, and a transition happens after inflation only if reheating brings the plasma above the transition temperature. The withdrawn #14 §3 required: "Inflation must precede the hidden transition, and v must lie below the reheating temperature."
- **The referee's reheating requirement.** The round-3 referee found that the candidate "requires visible T_RH >~ v/xi = 1.06e12 - 1.90e12 GeV (step4c.out) and a two-field (or asymmetric) reheating mechanism for xi; a separately reheated hidden sector can carry dark-radiation isocurvature (conditional; not computed)" (`review/round3/referee/audit-notes.md` Step 4(c) item 9). The adjudication took the referee's list of omitted constraints "as a to-do; not individually re-checked". Isocurvature means perturbations in the relative amounts of the components rather than in the total density [T].
- **Entropy dilution and early matter domination [T].** A late release of entropy divides every relic yield Y = n/s by the same factor, and a period of early matter domination ended by decays is one way to produce it. #11 §12: "Hidden-sector monopoles avoid overclosing the universe only for symmetry-breaking scales ≲ 100 PeV, or with a period of early matter domination [29S]." The scenario below that scale is prior art: [topological dark matter in the literature](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/prior-art).
- **The route the program rejected.** Round-3 decision 2 rejected "route (b): superheavy plus a named dilution", which "restores the mass only by adding a free dilution factor of at least 10³–10⁴ (ruling 2), probably far more (ruling 3). The mass would then no longer be a prediction, and the program does not trade falsifiability for survival." #11 §12: "Superheavy masses survive only with a free dilution factor. That removes the mass prediction, which was the candidate's reason to exist." See [Obituary: superheavy conjugate solitons](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/obituary) and [round-3 decisions and lessons](/wiki/method/review/round-3/decisions).
