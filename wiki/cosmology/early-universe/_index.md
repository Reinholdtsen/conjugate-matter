*Early-universe cosmology is the physics of the hot, dense first stages of cosmic history, when the universe was filled with radiation whose temperature fell as it expanded. This section holds established physics only: the thermal history of that radiation era and the count of light particle species it fixes, inflation and the later processes that thin out relics, and the gravitational waves that a phase transition would leave. The program uses this physics in two places: its optional prediction of an early change in the strength of gravity and in the effective number of light species, and the review that retired its soliton dark-matter candidate, which turned on how many relics the early universe makes and how far later processes could dilute them.*

## Map

All three pages are literature, written from general knowledge [T]; each says where the program used it.

[The radiation era: thermal history and N_eff](/wiki/cosmology/early-universe/radiation-era) comes first, because the other two pages assume it: the expansion rate as a function of temperature, entropy density and relic yields, and N_eff, the effective number of light species, with its bounds. Its standard coefficients are anchors of the script that checks the soliton obituary.

[Inflation, reheating and entropy dilution](/wiki/cosmology/early-universe/inflation-and-dilution) covers how relics are thinned: by inflation if they form before it, and by a later release of entropy if they form after reheating. Inflation is why monopoles of our own sector are not seen, and dilution was the route by which the retired soliton candidate could have kept its mass, a route the program rejected.

[Gravitational waves from phase transitions](/wiki/cosmology/early-universe/phase-transition-gravitational-waves) closes the section: the random background a first-order transition leaves, its redshifted peak frequency, the band the retired candidate's hidden transition would have produced, and the primordial-background step that an early form of row 10 contained.
