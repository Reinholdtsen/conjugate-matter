*Mandatory breaking is the program's argument that Born reciprocity, the exchange of position and momentum, cannot remain unbroken in any world that contains local physics. The uncertainty principle rules out anything being sharp in both position and momentum at once, so a world whose interactions are local must be local in one variable at the cost of the other; in #11's phrase, "the magnet must point". The argument is why the program treats the breaking as forced rather than optional, while leaving open which of the two variables ends up as position. Since v2.2 it carries qualifications from the toy models: the polarization is a continuous angle, a definite polarization needs an added potential, and the breaking has physical effect only once matter couples to the sign of the order parameter.*

**Status:** Alive — untagged in #11 §3 (part of the hard core, whose postulates are [P]) — a qualitative argument, not machine-checked; its v2.2 qualifications come from the toy models and carry [D][V] where #11 §4 tags them.

## Key facts

- **The symmetry.** "Quantum kinematics is exactly invariant under ρ: (x,p) → (p,−x); the Fourier transform implements it unitarily [1†, 2†]." (#11 §3; see [Born reciprocity](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/born-reciprocity) and [the Fourier transform as a phase-space rotation](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/fourier-transform)).
- **The argument.** "The program: ρ is exact and spontaneously broken. The breaking is mandatory: [x,p] = iℏ forbids joint locality, so any world with local physics must select a Lagrangian polarization. The magnet must point." (#11 §3, untagged; see [the canonical commutator](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/canonical-commutator), [Lagrangian polarizations](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/lagrangian-polarizations) and [spontaneous symmetry breaking](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/spontaneous-symmetry-breaking)).
- **What it leaves free.** The argument requires some polarization, not a particular one: "P5 The vacuum selects a polarization; ours is x-tilted; the mirror vacuum is equally lawful. [P]" (#11 §4; [P5](/wiki/program/postulates/p5-spontaneous-selection)). It is part of [the hard core](/wiki/program/hard-core).
- **Attribution.** "the idea that a preferred polarization breaks Born duality is Freidel, Leigh and Minic's", who "treat the breaking as kinematical and call the choice 'a dynamical question'. This program's addition is to read it as spontaneous breaking, with η as the order parameter" (#11 §3, v2.2; [Broken Born duality](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/broken-born-duality); [the order parameter](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/order-parameter)).
- **The v2.2 qualifications.** "with only ω rigid, ρ sits inside a continuous U(1), and the polarization is a Goldstone angle rather than a discrete choice [D][V]. Two things follow. A definite polarization needs a ρ-preserving potential on that angle […] And the breaking of ρ is physical only once matter couples to the sign of K (P4)" (#11 §4, P2′ refinement; see [the Goldstone angle](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/goldstone-angle), [Open: the origin of the ρ-preserving potential](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/rho-preserving-potential), [the twisted element](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/twisted-element) and [P4](/wiki/program/postulates/p4-polarized-matter)).
- **A further limit from the toys.** In the complex Grosse–Wulkenhaar model, "the breaking does not survive quantization: every condensate phase is an exact flat direction, and the condensate is a localized lump with no volume factor" (toy-model note, Abstract; [Quantum restoration in the complex model](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/toy-models/grosse-wulkenhaar/quantum-restoration)).
- **Earlier and popular wordings.** #9 §1 (superseded): "nothing can be local in both variables at once, so any universe with local physics must pick a polarization. The choice is the magnet's direction." #12 calls the settling "compulsory, since the uncertainty principle allows no symmetric option"; that article presents the framework as if confirmed and omits the v2.2 qualifications, so it is not a source for the claim's status ([The popular articles](/wiki/history/documents/popular-articles)).
