*A toy model is a small, fully specified field theory built to test one idea in isolation. The idea tested here is the program's [hard core](/wiki/program/hard-core): that [Born reciprocity](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/born-reciprocity), the exchange of position and momentum, is an exact symmetry of the equations that the lowest-energy state does not share. Two models were built and tested on 19–20 October 2026: a sigma model whose fields are a phase-space metric and a real structure ([model I](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/toy-models/coset-model)), and a scalar field theory on a noncommutative plane ([model II](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/toy-models/grosse-wulkenhaar)). Model I, with a bounded potential added, answers the referees' [acceptance criterion 1](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/toy-models/acceptance-criterion), which [checkpoint #11](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) now records as met in the referees' wording, with stated open ends. Model II's complex-field version breaks the exchange only classically, and its real-field version shows a one-loop preference for the position-sharp vacuum that is recorded as a lead.*

## Map

**The two documents.** The work is recorded in two documents, and this section covers both. `papers/reciprocity-toy-models.md`, version 2 (commit 6606734, 2026-10-20), is the current reference. It is written as an anonymous manuscript for blind review, so it carries no [provenance tags](/wiki/method/provenance-tags), and its † marks are Hermitian adjoints (φ†φ), not citation marks. `notes/toy-lagrangian.md` is the dated worklog: the criteria and forecasts, four chunks of results, the assessment against the forecasts, and the write-up. Where the two differ, version 2 governs; several of the worklog's numbers and wordings predate the [round-4 review](/wiki/method/review/round-4), and the pages here mark each such case. The sequence ran over two days: the criteria were fixed and the four chunks computed on 19 October 2026, the first version of the note was [pre-registered](/wiki/method/review/pre-registration) and sent to blind review the same evening, and the adjudication and version 2 followed on 20 October ([timeline](/wiki/history/timeline)).

The pages run in the order a reader needs them: the demand, the tests, then the two models.

- [Acceptance criterion 1: the toy Lagrangian](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/toy-models/acceptance-criterion) comes first because it is the demand the models answer: an action in which ρ is a genuine Noether symmetry, [spontaneously broken](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/spontaneous-symmetry-breaking), with the [neutral metric](/wiki/phase-space/born-geometry/born-structures) η as [order parameter](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/order-parameter). The page traces the demand from the round-1 and round-2 referees through [#14](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate) and [the handoff](/wiki/history/documents/handoff) to [round 3](/wiki/method/review/round-3)'s decision to build it, gives #11's verdict and what each part of it rests on, and records what remains open, the worklog's superseded first verdict, and a clause of an older wording, the electric sublattice, that nothing addresses.
- [Success criteria S0–S5 and the pre-registered predictions](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/toy-models/success-criteria) are the pass-or-fail tests written down before either model was built, with the forecasts made alongside them. The page explains each criterion, the prior-art survey S0 required, the scorecards, the worklog's self-scored assessment and the three findings nobody forecast. It is method, not physics, and it defines what the later pages mean by "pass".
- [Model I: the coset sigma model](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/toy-models/coset-model) is a field theory of a phase-space metric H and a real structure K, and it is the model that meets the criterion. Its subsection runs from the sigma-model background through the action, the vacua and the Goldstone spectrum, the failed and the repaired potentials, the frozen-H reduction and its lattice simulation, to the open question about ρ's SO(2).
- [Model II: the self-dual Grosse–Wulkenhaar model](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/toy-models/grosse-wulkenhaar) was tested first, as the route in which a field's own vacuum might select position or momentum with no K put in by hand. Its subsection runs from the noncommutative-field-theory background through the matrix base, the duality lemma and the distinction between complex and real fields, to the complex model's vacua, their quantum restoration, and the real model's one-loop lead. The route fails the quantum-stability test, so the coset model's fallback, a ρ-preserving potential, became the result for that test.

Each model's subsection opens with one page of established physics, [coset and hyperbolic sigma models](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/toy-models/coset-model/sigma-models) and [noncommutative field theory and the Langmann–Szabo duality](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/toy-models/grosse-wulkenhaar/noncommutative-field-theory), both literature [T]. Every other page in this section is the program's own work. The numbers are checked by the four gated scripts described at [the toy-model scripts](/wiki/method/verification/toy-model-scripts), and the note was graded B in [review round 4](/wiki/method/review/round-4) and revised to version 2. A round-5 review of the note is optional, "but only before any external use" (root `CLAUDE.md`).

## Boundaries

- The general lessons drawn from the toys sit at the root of [Spontaneous breaking of reciprocity](/wiki/symmetry-breaking): [the polarization as a Goldstone angle](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/goldstone-angle), [the twisted element ρ∘(K → −K)](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/twisted-element), [the origin of the ρ-preserving potential](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/rho-preserving-potential) (open), [the section condition](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/section-condition), [locality from an (H, K)-built coupling](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/matter-coupling) and [forced locality and the P4 lock](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/strong-locality) (open). This section holds the calculations behind them.
- The theorem that limits ordering in two dimensions is [the Mermin–Wagner–Coleman theorem](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/mermin-wagner-coleman).
- The objection the models were built to answer is [the 'framework does no work' critique](/wiki/program/framework-does-no-work), and the postulates they test and refine are [P3](/wiki/program/postulates/p3-reciprocity), [P2′](/wiki/program/postulates/p2-prime) and [P5](/wiki/program/postulates/p5-spontaneous-selection).
- The blind review that graded the toy-model note is [review round 4](/wiki/method/review/round-4), and the scripts that check it are described at [the toy-model scripts](/wiki/method/verification/toy-model-scripts).
- The status registers that list this section's items are [the graveyard](/wiki/status/graveyard), [open problems](/wiki/status/open-problems) and [superseded statements](/wiki/status/superseded).
