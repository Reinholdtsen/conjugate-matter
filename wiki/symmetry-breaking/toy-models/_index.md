*A toy model is a small, fully specified field theory built to test one idea in isolation. The idea tested here is the program's [hard core](../../program/hard-core.md): that [Born reciprocity](../../phase-space/reciprocity/born-reciprocity.md), the exchange of position and momentum, is an exact symmetry of the equations that the lowest-energy state does not share. Two models were built and tested on 19–20 October 2026. [Model I](coset-model/_index.md) is a [sigma model](coset-model/sigma-models.md), a field theory whose fields take values in a curved space; its two fields are a metric on the phase plane, the plane of a particle's position and momentum, and a real structure, which picks out two lines of that plane as the candidate position and momentum axes. [Model II](grosse-wulkenhaar/_index.md) is a field theory on a [noncommutative plane](grosse-wulkenhaar/noncommutative-field-theory.md), a plane whose two coordinates fail to commute, as position and momentum do in quantum mechanics. Model I, with a bounded potential added (one that cannot fall without limit), answers the referees' [acceptance criterion 1](acceptance-criterion.md), their demand for an explicit model in which the exchange is a genuine symmetry that the vacuum breaks; [checkpoint #11](../../history/documents/checkpoint.md) records it as met in the referees' wording, with stated open ends. With a complex field, model II's vacua break the exchange only before quantum effects are included, and quantum fluctuations restore it. With a real field the exchange is not a symmetry of the model, and the first quantum correction (the one-loop term) favours the vacuum that is sharper in position over its mirror image, a preference recorded as a lead.*

## Map

**The two documents.** The work is recorded in two documents, and this section covers both. [`wiki/papers/reciprocity-toy-models.md`](../../papers/reciprocity-toy-models.md), titled "Spontaneous Breaking of Born Reciprocity: Two Toy Models", version 2 (commit 6606734, 2026-10-20), is the current reference. It is written as an anonymous manuscript for blind review, so it carries no [provenance tags](../../method/provenance-tags.md), and its † marks are Hermitian adjoints (φ†φ), not citation marks. The version sent to [review round 4](../../method/review/round-4.md) (commit d9a06ac, 2026-10-19) was titled "Spontaneous Breaking of Born Reciprocity in Two Toy Models: the Polarization as a Goldstone Angle"; version 2 does not carry that subtitle. Round 4's revision 3, among its changes to the note's section on model II, reads "Restrict 'Goldstone angle' to the complex model." ([`review/round4-adjudication.md`](../../../review/round4-adjudication.md), 'Revisions' 3), and version 2's abstract reads "Where ρ sits inside a continuous U(1) — model I, and model II with complex fields — the polarization is a Goldstone angle." ([the polarization as a Goldstone angle](../goldstone-angle.md)). [`notes/toy-lagrangian.md`](../../../notes/toy-lagrangian.md) is the dated worklog: the criteria and forecasts, four chunks of results, the assessment against the forecasts, and the write-up. Where the two differ, version 2 governs; several of the worklog's numbers and wordings predate the round-4 review, and the pages here mark each such case. The sequence ran over two days: the criteria were fixed and the four chunks computed on 19 October 2026, the first version of the note was [pre-registered](../../method/review/pre-registration.md) and sent to blind review the same evening, and the adjudication and version 2 followed on 20 October ([timeline](../../history/timeline.md)).

The pages run in the order a reader needs them: the demand, the tests, then the two models.

- [Acceptance criterion 1: the toy Lagrangian](acceptance-criterion.md) comes first because it is the demand the models answer: an action in which ρ is a genuine Noether symmetry, [spontaneously broken](../spontaneous-symmetry-breaking.md), with the [neutral metric](../../phase-space/born-geometry/born-structures.md) η as [order parameter](../order-parameter.md). The page traces the demand from the round-1 and round-2 referees through [a withdrawn paper on monopole dark matter](../../../notes/knot-dark-matter-standalone.md) and [the handoff](../../history/documents/handoff.md) to [round 3](../../method/review/round-3/_index.md)'s decision to build it, gives [#11](../../papers/broken-born-checkpoint-v2.md)'s verdict and what each part of it rests on, and records what remains open, the worklog's superseded first verdict, and a clause of an older wording, the electric sublattice, that nothing addresses and that [#11 §11.4](../../papers/broken-born-checkpoint-v2.md#114-the-magnetic-mirror-conjecture-o-pr-v23) lists first among what P.R.'s [magnetic-mirror conjecture](../../electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification.md) needs.
- [Success criteria S0–S5 and the pre-registered predictions](success-criteria.md) are the pass-or-fail tests written down before either model was built, with the forecasts made alongside them. The page explains each criterion, the prior-art survey S0 required, the scorecards, the worklog's self-scored assessment and the three findings nobody forecast. It is method, not physics, and it defines what the later pages mean by "pass".
- [Model I: the coset sigma model](coset-model/_index.md) is a field theory of a phase-space metric H and a real structure K, and it is the model that meets the criterion. Its subsection runs from the sigma-model background through the action, the vacua and the Goldstone spectrum, the failed and the repaired potentials, the frozen-H reduction and its lattice simulation, to the two open questions: [a Monte Carlo with H dynamical](coset-model/dynamical-h-monte-carlo.md) and ρ's SO(2) in the coupled model.
- [Model II: the self-dual Grosse–Wulkenhaar model](grosse-wulkenhaar/_index.md) was tested first, as the route in which a field's own vacuum might select position or momentum with no K put in by hand. Its subsection runs from the noncommutative-field-theory background through the matrix base, the duality lemma and the distinction between complex and real fields, to the complex model's vacua, their quantum restoration, and the real model's one-loop lead. The route fails the quantum-stability test, so the coset model's fallback, a ρ-preserving potential, became the result for that test.

Each model's subsection opens with one page of established physics, [coset and hyperbolic sigma models](coset-model/sigma-models.md) and [noncommutative field theory and the Langmann–Szabo duality](grosse-wulkenhaar/noncommutative-field-theory.md), both literature [T]. Every other page in this section is the program's own work. The numbers are checked by the four gated scripts described at [the toy-model scripts](../../method/verification/toy-model-scripts.md), and the note was graded B in [review round 4](../../method/review/round-4.md) and revised to version 2. The adjudication and the worklog record every accepted revision as applied, though version 2 says that the comparison with an exactly solved related model, which revision 4 asked for, was not carried out ([quantum restoration](grosse-wulkenhaar/quantum-restoration.md#open-the-comparison-with-the-exact-solution); flag 42 in [`review/wiki-build-flags.md`](../../../review/wiki-build-flags.md)). A round-6 review of the note is optional, "but only before any external use" (root [`CLAUDE.md`](../../../CLAUDE.md)).
