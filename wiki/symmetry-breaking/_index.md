*Spontaneous symmetry breaking is the situation in which the laws of a system have a symmetry that its lowest-energy state lacks: the laws governing a magnet treat all directions alike, yet a cooled magnet is magnetized along one of them. The Broken-Born program proposes that Born reciprocity, the exchange of position and momentum under which quantum kinematics is exactly symmetric, is broken in this way, so that the vacuum, not the laws, fixes which variable acts as position. This section holds the textbook theory of such breaking, the program's account of the mechanism, and the two toy field theories built to test it.*

## Map

The section opens with three pages of established physics, which the program's claims rely on. [Spontaneous symmetry breaking](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/spontaneous-symmetry-breaking) defines the pattern: a symmetric law with an asymmetric ground state, the vacuum manifold and stabilizer, the order parameter, why breaking needs infinitely many degrees of freedom, and spontaneous against explicit breaking. It also gives the program's operational definition, the toy-model [criteria S1–S3](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/toy-models/success-criteria), with model I as the worked case. [Goldstone modes and the Higgs mechanism](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/goldstone-and-higgs) covers the massless modes that a broken continuous symmetry leaves behind and their count from the Hessian, pseudo-Goldstone modes, and how a gauge field absorbs the modes and becomes massive; the program meets these in model I's three flat directions, in its polarization angle, and in the mass of its momentum graviton. [The Mermin–Wagner–Coleman theorem](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/mermin-wagner-coleman) states which symmetries can break in two dimensions and why tunnelling rules out breaking in 0+1, the limits that determined how the toy models were built; in model I the theorem applies only to the compact rotation group containing ρ.

The program's own claims follow in the order of its argument. [Mandatory breaking](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/mandatory-breaking) is the uncertainty-principle argument that some polarization must be selected in any world with local physics, given step by step with the qualifications v2.2 added. [The order parameter](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/order-parameter) names the quantity that carries the selection, the [neutral metric](/wiki/phase-space/born-geometry/born-structures) η, written in terms of J, η or K in different documents, and records the superseded discrete pattern ℤ₄ → ℤ₂. [The polarization as a Goldstone angle](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/goldstone-angle) records the v2.2 finding from the toy models: the selection is a continuous angle rather than a discrete choice, so a definite polarization needs a potential, and in the coset model the angle is the circle the program assigns to its dark-energy field τ, which shows consistency, not a test. [Open: the origin of the ρ-preserving potential](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/rho-preserving-potential) asks where the potential that pins that angle comes from.

[The twisted element ρ∘(K → −K)](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/twisted-element) shows that the breaking has no physical effect until matter can tell K from −K, which leads to the three pages on matter. [The section condition](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/section-condition) confines matter to one of the two lines that η singles out, without saying which. [Locality from an (H, K)-built coupling](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/matter-coupling) makes matter local in the selected line, the weak form of the locality criterion. [Open: forced locality and the P4 lock](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/strong-locality) records that nothing yet forces matter to couple that way.

Last, [Toy models of broken reciprocity](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/toy-models) holds the two explicit field theories behind these results: [model I, a coset sigma model](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/toy-models/coset-model), and [model II, the self-dual Grosse–Wulkenhaar model](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/toy-models/grosse-wulkenhaar), with the criteria fixed before they were built. The pages above draw the general lessons; the toy-model section holds the calculations.
