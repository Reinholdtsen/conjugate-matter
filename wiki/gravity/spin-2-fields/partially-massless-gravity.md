*Partially massless gravity is a massive spin-2 field on de Sitter space whose mass takes the one special value, exactly at the Higuchi bound, at which an extra gauge symmetry appears and removes the field's helicity-0 polarization [T]. Bimetric gravity has a parameter point with this property: there the potential is symmetric under exchange of the two metrics, and the theory has de Sitter solutions in which the two metrics are proportional with an undetermined ratio. The point matters to the program because it lies inside the [palindromic branch](/wiki/gravity/palindromic-branch) that reciprocity selects. That raises the open question whether reciprocity is the origin of the partially massless symmetry, and it means the program's [vacuum-energy identity](/wiki/gravity/palindromic-branch/vacuum-energy-identity) must be compared with this literature before it can be called new.*

**Status:** Established — [T] — literature, cited in the [checkpoint](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) as [24S] (retrieved by live search). Its relation to the program's branch is open, as open problem 2 of #11 §14 ([the partially massless comparison](/wiki/gravity/palindromic-branch/pm-comparison)).

## Key facts

- The parameter point (#11 §7.4): "the PM point β₀ = 3α⁻²β₂, β₁ = β₃ = 0, β₄ = 3α²β₂ makes the potential g↔f symmetric, with proportional de Sitter solutions of undetermined ratio [24S]." PM is partially massless; the βₙ are the coefficients of the [Hassan–Rosen potential](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/hassan-rosen-bimetric).
- #11 does not define α or c. In the bimetric literature's usual notation, α is the ratio of the two metrics' Planck masses and c is the constant of proportionality in f = c²g [T].
- Inside the palindromic branch (#11 §7.4): "At α = 1 the PM point lies **inside** our palindromic branch", which raises "the open question of whether reciprocity is the origin of the PM symmetry [O]". See [the palindrome βₙ = β₄₋ₙ](/wiki/gravity/palindromic-branch/palindrome).
- A different point of the same branch (#11 §7.4): "the PM point sits at Λ ≠ 0 with c undetermined; our flat-aligned-vacuum identity selects a *different* point of the same branch (β₁ ≠ 0 generically, β₀ = −4β₁ − 3β₂)". See [the vacuum-energy identity](/wiki/gravity/palindromic-branch/vacuum-energy-identity).
- Reference [24][S] (#11): "Partially massless bimetric gravity and its exchange-symmetric parameter point, arXiv:1609.09515"; #11 gives no authors.
- The [handoff](/wiki/history/documents/handoff) records the same finding (HANDOFF §2): the partially massless bimetric point "is g↔f symmetric and sits inside the palindromic branch". The literature check that found it, together with Hossenfelder's exchange-symmetric bimetric theory, downgraded the novelty of the vacuum-energy identity.
- The mass value relates to the [Higuchi bound](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/bimetric-cosmology) m² ≥ 2H², the unitarity bound for a massive spin-2 field in de Sitter space; the partially massless field sits at its edge [T].
