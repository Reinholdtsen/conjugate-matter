*A spin-2 field is a symmetric tensor field, h_μν, whose quanta carry two units of angular momentum; the graviton of [general relativity](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/general-relativity) is the massless case. Such a field can be given a mass, or joined by a second one, only in narrow ways, because a generic mass term or coupling adds a ghost, a mode whose energy has the wrong sign. This section collects the established results that define those narrow ways, from the [Fierz–Pauli mass term](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/fierz-pauli) to the ghost-free [de Rham–Gabadadze–Tolley (dRGT)](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/drgt-massive-gravity) and [Hassan–Rosen](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/hassan-rosen-bimetric) theories, because the program's [momentum graviton X](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton) is held to them. Everything here is literature; the program's own claims about X are in the neighbouring sections.*

**Literature only:** all ten pages are established physics or literature, so the section ranks no claims of the program's own; those are tracked in the registers of [claims that stand](/wiki/status/alive), [the graveyard](/wiki/status/graveyard), [open problems and the queue](/wiki/status/open-problems), [conjectures](/wiki/status/conjectures) and [superseded statements](/wiki/status/superseded). Where a page says how the program uses a result, it gives the program's tags and its machine checks ([battery tests](/wiki/method/verification/battery) T4, T6 and T7). One superseded statement is folded into a page here: the vacuum-energy cancellation framed as a cosmological-constant result, on [the cosmological-constant problem](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/cosmological-constant-problem).

## Map

The section runs from the massless graviton, through the conditions under which a spin-2 field may carry a mass, to the ghost-free theories and their algebra, and ends with the literature that bears on the program's later results.

[General relativity and the graviton](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/general-relativity) comes first: the metric, the Einstein–Hilbert action, minimal coupling and the equivalence principle, then the linearized theory, whose gauge symmetry δh_μν = ∂_μξ_ν + ∂_νξ_μ makes the graviton a massless spin-2 field, and the result that one consistently interacting massless graviton is general relativity. X is defined by analogy with this graviton: its local shifts are a second copy of the same linearized diffeomorphisms.

[The Fierz–Pauli mass term and the Boulware–Deser ghost](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/fierz-pauli) gives the only healthy quadratic mass term, Tr(h²) − (Tr h)², and shows why its relative weight is special. It holds the signature obstruction, which excludes a symmetric tensor without gauge symmetry, and the Boulware–Deser ghost that generic nonlinear completions revive. It also records how the program's metric-built potential misses the Fierz–Pauli weight, the comparison checked by battery test T4. [The multi-graviton no-go theorem](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/multi-graviton-no-go) shows that several massless gravitons cannot interact with one another. Since X must couple to the metric and to [conjugate matter](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter), it cannot stay massless; with the signature obstruction, this forces a mass.

[dRGT massive gravity](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/drgt-massive-gravity) is the ghost-free nonlinear completion of the Fierz–Pauli term with one dynamical metric, a potential linear in the elementary symmetric polynomials of √(g⁻¹f). The program took its class as the health criterion for X, and the page follows the criterion through [the tuning test](/wiki/gravity/tuning-test). [Hassan–Rosen bimetric gravity](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/hassan-rosen-bimetric) makes the second metric dynamical. Its potential, V = 2m²M² Σ βₙ eₙ(√(g⁻¹f)), and the exchange identity that reads the list of coefficients backwards when the two metrics trade places, are what [reciprocity](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/born-reciprocity) later acts on; its spectrum is one massless and one massive spin-2 field. [Elementary symmetric polynomials](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/elementary-symmetric-polynomials) gives the algebra of that potential, with battery tests T6 and T7. [The vielbein formulation and wedge potentials](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/vielbein-formulation) gives its frame form, ghost-free by construction, in which the program's tuning test passed.

The last three pages are context for the program's results. [Partially massless gravity](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/partially-massless-gravity) is a massive spin-2 field on de Sitter space at the mass where a gauge symmetry removes its helicity-0 mode, together with the bimetric parameter point studied as partially massless bimetric gravity, which lies inside the program's [palindromic branch](/wiki/gravity/palindromic-branch) at α = 1. [Bimetric cosmology and its instabilities](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/bimetric-cosmology) records the known instabilities of bimetric cosmologies, the heavy spin-2 field shown to be linearly stable at late times when the second metric's Planck mass is much smaller than the first, and the Higuchi bound; they bear on X's [open stability at equal Planck masses](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/equal-planck-mass-stability). [The cosmological-constant problem](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/cosmological-constant-problem) records the vacuum-energy problem that the program's [vacuum-energy identity](/wiki/gravity/palindromic-branch/vacuum-energy-identity) does not by itself address.
