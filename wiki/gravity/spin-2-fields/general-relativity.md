*General relativity is Einstein's theory of gravity, in which gravity is the curvature of spacetime and a single field, the metric g_μν, fixes distances, durations and the paths of freely falling bodies. Small ripples of the metric are the graviton, a massless spin-2 field whose gauge symmetry is the linearized form of coordinate changes (diffeomorphisms). The program leaves general relativity unchanged for ordinary matter and uses the graviton as the template for its second field X, whose local symmetry is a second copy of the same linearized diffeomorphisms.*

**Status:** Established — [T] — textbook physics, written here from general knowledge; the program's uses of it, listed below, carry their own tags.

## Key facts

- The metric g_μν fixes the geometry; the Einstein–Hilbert action, proportional to ∫√−g R with R the curvature scalar, governs it; and minimal coupling means that matter feels gravity only through g [T].
- For a small perturbation h_μν of a flat metric, infinitesimal coordinate changes act as δh_μν = ∂₍μξ_ν₎, and the massless spin-2 graviton is the gauge field of this symmetry. A single consistently self-interacting massless spin-2 field reproduces general relativity [T]; [the multi-graviton no-go](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/multi-graviton-no-go) rules out several interacting ones.
- The program's [momentum graviton X](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton) has local shifts δX_μν = ∂₍μλ_ν₎, which form "a second copy of linearized diffeomorphisms" because X is symmetric ([#6](/wiki/history/documents/debt-1-notes) §2; #11 §7.1, [D + T]); see [dual diffeomorphisms](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/dual-diffeomorphisms).
- Ordinary matter is left alone. In the [lab notebook](/wiki/history/documents/lab-notebook)'s v0.1 action, the g-block of the phase-space metric H "reproduces the Einstein–Hilbert action with M_P fixed by M_B and the section volume" [D, schematic], and the Standard Model is "minimally coupled to g. Nothing is modified." [P4] (#1 §3, superseded as reference; see [the v0.1 action](/wiki/gravity/tuning-test/geometric-action)). In the current postulate [P4](/wiki/program/postulates/p4-polarized-matter), x-polarized and p-polarized matter "both couple universally to H" [P] (#11 §4).
- The equivalence principle: free fall is force-free (geodesic) motion, and a uniformly accelerated (Rindler) frame is locally equivalent to a uniform gravitational field [T]. [#13](/wiki/conjugate-relativity) §5 reads free fall and Rindler frames as the rest state and the uniform motion of the momentum polarization, and states that "the dual principle of relativity — equivalence of all force-free frames — is the equivalence principle" [D — structural]; see [the equivalence principle as dual relativity](/wiki/conjugate-relativity/equivalence-principle).
- General relativity leaves momentum space flat. The [checkpoint](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) counts "general relativity's flat momentum fibers" among the sources of the position–momentum asymmetry and calls them "an architectural assumption, not a measurement" (#11 §3); see [Born's question](/wiki/program/borns-question).
- For gravitational waves the standard expectation is m_g = 0; the program requires the reciprocity-even eigenstate to be exactly massless, and a nonzero mass attributed to it would kill the claim (#11 §13, [row 6](/wiki/predictions/massless-graviton)).
