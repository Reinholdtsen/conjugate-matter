*Bimetric cosmology studies expanding-universe solutions of Hassan–Rosen bimetric gravity. The published analyses of its cosmological branches find gradient or ghost instabilities when the spin-2 mass is of order today's Hubble rate and the two metrics have comparable Planck masses; a heavy spin-2 field has been shown to be linearly stable at late times when the second metric's Planck mass is much smaller than the first. Separately, a massive spin-2 field on de Sitter space must satisfy the Higuchi bound m² ≥ 2H², which a heavy field meets trivially. For the program the stable regime is out of reach: reciprocity sets the two Planck masses equal, so the cosmological stability of the heavy [momentum graviton X](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton) is an open question.*

**Status:** Established — [T] — literature as summarized in the withdrawn manuscript [#14](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate) (§1) and the v3 worklog's [round-2 pending list](/wiki/method/review/rounds-one-and-two) (item R7). The round-3 audit confirmed that the cited papers exist and support the statements, and ruling 12 accepted its finding that #14's reference [4] was over-generalized ([round-3 rulings](/wiki/method/review/round-3/rulings)).

## Key facts

- The instabilities (#14 §1): "The published analyses of bimetric cosmological branches find gradient or ghost instabilities, but they assume a spin-2 mass of order the present Hubble rate and comparable Planck masses for the two metrics [4]." Reference [4] is D. Comelli, M. Crisostomi, L. Pilo (arXiv:1202.1986); F. Könnig, Y. Akrami, L. Amendola, M. Motta, A. R. Solomon (arXiv:1407.4331); and M. Lagos, P. G. Ferreira (arXiv:1410.0207).
- The over-generalization (round-3 audit, Step 1): Könnig et al. identify a stable model (infinite-branch bigravity), and Lagos–Ferreira find stable scalar perturbations for a particular parameter choice. Ruling 12 accepted this as a minor citation issue (`review/round3-adjudication.md`).
- The heavy-spin-2 escape (#14 §1): "A heavy spin-2 field has been shown to be linearly stable at late times in the regime where the second metric's Planck mass is much smaller than the first [5]", with [5] Y. Akrami, S. F. Hassan, F. Könnig, A. Schmidt-May, A. R. Solomon, Phys. Lett. B 748, 37 (2015), arXiv:1503.07521. The worklog lists three limits: the viable regime takes M_f ≪ M_g; the result holds at linear order only, with tuned βₙ; and the strong-coupling era H > H⋆ is unresolved (`review/v3-worklog.md`, R7).
- The Higuchi bound m² ≥ 2H² [6] (A. Higuchi, Nucl. Phys. B 282, 397 (1987)). The audit read the abstract as "M^2 cannot be between 0 and 2/3 x (cosmological constant)", which with Λ = 3H² is m² ≥ 2H². #14 §1: "satisfied trivially by the heavy field". The [partially massless](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/partially-massless-gravity) field sits at the bound's edge [T].
- The program's tension (worklog R7): [#8](/wiki/history/documents/debt-1-notes) §3 says reciprocity forces equal effective Planck masses, M_f = M_g, "which rules out the M_f ≪ M_g escape. Whether a large mass alone is enough at M_f = M_g is open (De Felice et al. 2014, cited in Akrami et al.: 'the instability becomes negligible for large values of m'; not yet read)." See [open: stability at equal Planck masses](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/equal-planck-mass-stability).
- Where the item lives: only in #14 (§1, and §5 item 7 among its exclusion conditions) and the worklog. #11 §14 does not list it, so the wiki keeps it as a separate open item. #14 itself was withdrawn for its abundance arithmetic; see [the soliton candidate (retired)](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate).
