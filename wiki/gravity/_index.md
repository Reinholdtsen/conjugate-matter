*Gravity in the Broken-Born program involves two spin-2 fields, symmetric tensor fields of the kind that carry gravity: the spacetime metric of [general relativity](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/general-relativity), and a second tensor X, the [momentum graviton](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton), which carries the geometry of momentum space. A symmetry of the program's phase-space geometry gives X the gauge symmetry of a second graviton. Established results exclude both a second interacting massless graviton and a symmetric tensor without gauge symmetry, which leaves X one option, a mass. Whether the potential that supplies the mass is free of ghosts was the program's [tuning test](/wiki/gravity/tuning-test), passed in frame variables; reciprocity then constrains that potential to a [palindromic branch](/wiki/gravity/palindromic-branch), in which a flat aligned vacuum forces the vacuum energy of the spin-2 interaction potential to zero. This section sets out the established theory of massless and massive spin-2 fields first, then the program's results on X, worked out as [Debt 1](/wiki/history/debts), the program's name for the missing field equation of the phase-space metric. Those results are structural derivations, partly machine-checked, and the [checkpoint](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) states that the word "theory" still waits on its first three open problems.*

## Map

[Spin-2 fields](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields) comes first because it is the established literature that X is held to. It runs from [general relativity's graviton](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/general-relativity) and its gauge symmetry, through [the Fierz–Pauli mass term](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/fierz-pauli), whose special weight keeps a massive spin-2 field free of ghosts and whose page also holds the signature obstruction, and [the multi-graviton no-go theorem](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/multi-graviton-no-go), to the ghost-free nonlinear theories, [dRGT massive gravity](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/drgt-massive-gravity) (de Rham–Gabadadze–Tolley) and [Hassan–Rosen bimetric gravity](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/hassan-rosen-bimetric), with their algebra and frame form. It ends with the literature behind the program's later results: [the partially massless point](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/partially-massless-gravity), [bimetric cosmology](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/bimetric-cosmology) and the cosmological-constant problem. Its pages are literature; where a page states how the program uses a result, it gives the program's tags.

[The momentum graviton X](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton) holds the program's chain of results on X = Re Z: its shift symmetry and dual diffeomorphisms, why it must be massive, how the choice of polarization gives it a mass, why [τ is not eaten](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/tau-not-eaten), its spectrum and its safety for ordinary matter, the open questions, the chat-only results, and the program's answer to Born's question. These are program claims; the checkpoint tags the chain [D + T] (§7.1) and the spectrum [D] (§7.5).

[The tuning test](/wiki/gravity/tuning-test) asks whether the potential that gives X its mass avoids the Boulware–Deser ghost. Potentials built from metrics fail twice, and each failure has a numerical check in [the verification battery](/wiki/method/verification/battery): their mass term lacks the Fierz–Pauli trace term (test T4), and their nonlinear structure lies outside the Hassan–Rosen family (test T7, which checks the power sum p₄). Potentials built from frames pass, which calc note [#7](/wiki/history/documents/debt-1-notes) calls a conditional pass; the checkpoint tags the test [D][V] (§7.2). The section also keeps the superseded v0.1 action and the open derivation of the passing potential from the geometric action.

[The palindromic branch](/wiki/gravity/palindromic-branch) comes last because it acts on the potential that passed: reciprocity forces βₙ = β₄₋ₙ, a flat aligned vacuum then forces the wedge sector's vacuum energy to zero, and a comparison with the partially massless literature is owed before that identity can be called new.
