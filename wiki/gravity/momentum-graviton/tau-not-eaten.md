*This result says that the dark-energy field τ is not one of the modes the momentum graviton X absorbs when it becomes massive. τ is an angle on [the Lagrangian Grassmannian](/wiki/phase-space/modular-field/lagrangian-grassmannian) Λ(d), the compact space of all the ways of splitting phase space into position and momentum: it is the [Maslov phase](/wiki/phase-space/modular-field/maslov-phase) of the vacuum's polarization. The modes X absorbs belong to a different sector, the gauge directions of the broken [dual diffeomorphisms](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/dual-diffeomorphisms), which shift X; constant shifts of X do not generate the compact directions in which τ lies. So τ survives the [gravitational Higgs mechanism](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/gravitational-higgs) as the light field the program's dark-energy sector needs. Calc note #6 flagged the point as open; calc note #8 derived it at leading order [D — leading order]. How strongly τ still mixes with the absorbed modes through the potential has not been computed and is an open problem.*

**Status:** Alive at leading order — [D — leading order] in calc note #8 (`notes/debt1a-iv-beta-coefficients.md` §3), carried by [checkpoint #11](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) §7.5, a section tagged [D] — an argument from where each mode comes from, not a computation of the mixing; not machine-checked. The residual mixing is [Open: τ–Stückelberg mixing (OP6)](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/tau-stuckelberg-mixing), item 6 of #11 §14.

## The statement

Calc note #8 ([the Debt-1 calc notes](/wiki/history/documents/debt-1-notes)), §3:

> **τ is not eaten** [D — leading order]: the Higgsing of the dual diffeomorphisms eats the vector π_μ and the *longitudinal scalar of the λ-gradient sector*. τ is a J-modulus on the compact Λ(d), a different sector with different origin; it survives as the light field. (Residual τ–Stückelberg mixing through the potential: logged, expected small by compactness.)

## The two kinds of mode

### What X absorbs

When the vacuum selects a polarization, it breaks the local shifts of X, δX_μν = ∂₍μλ_ν₎, and X absorbs the would-be Goldstone modes of that broken gauge symmetry, becoming a massive spin-2 field ([the gravitational Higgs mechanism](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/gravitational-higgs)). These modes belong to the gauge parameter λ_μ. Calc note #6 calls them "the four would-be Goldstones π_μ of the broken λ-gauge" (§3); calc note #8 calls them "the vector π_μ and the *longitudinal scalar of the λ-gradient sector*" (§3), and no document reconciles the two wordings. Written as explicit fields, would-be Goldstone modes of this kind are Stückelberg fields [T] ([Goldstone modes and the Higgs mechanism](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/goldstone-and-higgs)), and #11 calls them "the Stückelberg sector".

### What τ is

Under the corrected postulate [P2′](/wiki/program/postulates/p2-prime) the vacuum chooses a real structure J, which fixes which directions of phase space count as position, and the possible choices form the Lagrangian Grassmannian: "the vacuum spontaneously selects a real structure J (an involution anticommuting with I), and η ≡ HJ. The space of H-compatible real structures on (ℝ^{2d}, I) is {J} ≅ U(d)/O(d) = Λ(d)" (#5 §B). Λ(d) is compact, of dimension "d(d+1)/2 = 10 for d=4" (#1 §2).

τ is one coordinate on that space, the angle around its Maslov circle (#1 §2):

```text
τ ≡ f · arg det²(·)
```

#11 §6 places it on the boundary of the Siegel domain: "the dark-energy field τ is the Maslov phase of the boundary variable" ([τ as the Maslov phase of the boundary](/wiki/phase-space/modular-field/maslov-phase)). A coordinate on the space of J's is what #8 calls "a J-modulus", and the moduli of J together make up what #11 calls "the compact J-sector".

The two kinds of mode therefore have different origins. The absorbed modes come from the gauge parameter of the shifts of X, which move X = Re Z through the non-compact [Siegel domain](/wiki/phase-space/modular-field/siegel-domain). τ is a coordinate of the choice of J: changing it moves the polarization around the Maslov circle of the compact Λ(d).

## The argument

Calc note #6 stated the expectation and left it as a check: "(The Λ(d) moduli, τ included, are not the eaten modes — they carry the compact phase directions, which constant shifts do not generate; verifying τ survives un-eaten is flagged [O].)" (§3).

Calc note #8 settled it at leading order with the same distinction: the absorbed modes are those of "the λ-gradient sector", while "τ is a J-modulus on the compact Λ(d), a different sector with different origin; it survives as the light field" (§3). The argument rests on where each mode comes from. The modes a gauge field absorbs are the would-be Goldstone modes of its own broken gauge symmetry, here the dual diffeomorphisms [T]; a mode from another sector can enter only through mixing, and that is what "leading order" sets aside.

#8 states what is set aside in the same passage. Through the potential the two sectors can still mix, and #8 records that mixing only as "logged, expected small by compactness". No calculation backs the expectation: the τ–Stückelberg mixing matrix is open item 6 of #11 §14 ([Open: τ–Stückelberg mixing (OP6)](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/tau-stuckelberg-mixing)).

## Why it matters

τ is the program's dark-energy field: "τ is hilltop quintessence on the Maslov circle" (#11 §10; [τ: hilltop quintessence on the Maslov circle](/wiki/cosmology/dark-energy/tau-quintessence)). That role needs τ to be light. The lab notebook assumed it from the start: "that τ is the *lightest* modulus, with the other nine directions heavy, is assumed **[P]**" (#1 §2). The tuning test's picture builds the same split into its geometry, Hassan–Rosen bimetric gravity with the second frame confined to Λ(d), "with the light direction of the compact space being τ (the dark-energy field) and the massive spin-2 being X" (#7 §4; [constrained bimetric gravity](/wiki/gravity/tuning-test/constrained-bimetric)).

If τ had been among the modes X absorbs, it would have become part of the massive spin-2 field instead of a light field of its own [T]. The result shows, at leading order, that the mechanism giving X its mass leaves τ alone. It does not explain why τ is light: the lightness itself remains the postulate above, and the origin of τ's potential is open ([Open: the origin of the ρ-preserving potential](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/rho-preserving-potential)).

## What remains open

- **The residual mixing.** The τ–Stückelberg mixing matrix, open item 6 of #11 §14 ([OP6](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/tau-stuckelberg-mixing)).
- **The other nine moduli.** At d = 4, Λ(d) has ten directions: "Λ(d) σ-modes (10: τ plus nine, of which inverse-Higgs constraints likely eliminate most [O])" (#5 §D). #6 places all the Λ(d) moduli outside the absorbed modes; what happens to the nine is "(4) inverse-Higgs accounting of the nine heavy moduli" in calc note #5's open list (§F), a question no later document takes up ([Open: field content of the broken vacuum](/wiki/phase-space/modular-field/field-content)).
- **Lorentzian signature.** The mass mechanism belongs to what calc note #6 calls "the Euclidean-rigorous skeleton" (§3); the extension to spacetime signature is open item 3 ([Open: Lorentzian dressing of the Euclidean skeleton (OP3)](/wiki/phase-space/born-geometry/lorentzian-dressing)).

The calc notes write Λ(d), counting its dimension at d = 4; #11 writes Λ(d) in §6 and Λ(4) in §7. At d = 4 these are the same space.
