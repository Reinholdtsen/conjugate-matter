*This open problem asks whether a heavy massive spin-2 field such as the momentum graviton X is stable in an expanding universe when its metric and the ordinary metric have equal Planck masses, that is, equal gravitational coupling strengths [T]. Published analyses of bimetric cosmologies report instabilities for a light spin-2 field, and the work that finds a heavy spin-2 field linearly stable at late times assumes the second metric's Planck mass is much smaller than the first. Reciprocity forces the two Planck masses to be equal, which excludes that regime, and whether a large mass alone is enough has not been checked. The question came out of the worklog's answer to a finding of the second blind review of the soliton manuscript (#14), since withdrawn, and is recorded only in that worklog and the manuscript; [checkpoint #11](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) §14 does not list it.*

**Status:** Open — no tag in the sources — round-2 item R7 in `review/v3-worklog.md` ("state this as an open consistency item, not as evaded"), and #14 §1 and §5 item 7; absent from #11 §14; no calculation. Listed in [Open problems and the queue](/wiki/status/open-problems).

## Key facts

- Origin: the round-2 referee's valid findings included "bimetric cosmological-branch instability severity (mass hierarchy may evade — the analyses assume light second metric; early universe remains open; cite properly)" (`HANDOFF.md` §6; [Review rounds 1 and 2](/wiki/method/review/rounds-one-and-two)).
- Round-2 item R7 (`review/v3-worklog.md`, 'Round-2 pending list'): "The instability analyses assume a spin-2 mass m ~ H₀ and M_f ~ M_g". The works cited are Comelli–Crisostomi–Pilo (2012), Könnig et al. (2014) and Lagos–Ferreira (2014), #14's reference [4]. "A heavy spin-2 is linearly stable at late times", per Akrami–Hassan–Könnig–Schmidt-May–Solomon (2015), #14's reference [5]. "Three limits on that: their viable regime takes M_f ≪ M_g; it holds at linear order only, with tuned βₙ; and the strong-coupling era H > H⋆ is unresolved."
- The tension (R7): calc note #8 §3 "says reciprocity forces equal effective Planck masses (M_f = M_g), which rules out the M_f ≪ M_g escape. Whether a large mass alone is enough at M_f = M_g is open (De Felice et al. 2014, cited in Akrami et al.: 'the instability becomes negligible for large values of m'; not yet read)." The equality comes from the same symmetry that sorts the spin-2 eigenstates ([Spectrum, parameters and reciprocity parity](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/spectrum-and-parity)).
- #14 §1: "The framework's reciprocity, acting as exchange of the two metrics, instead sets the two Planck masses equal, so the stability of a heavy spin-2 field at equal Planck masses is an open consistency condition (Sec. 5, item 7)." The Higuchi bound m² ≥ 2H² is, per R7, "trivially satisfied by a heavy X".
- #14 §5, item 7, lists among its internal exclusion conditions "instability of a heavy spin-2 field at equal Planck masses".
- The instability literature is not uniform: round-3 ruling 12 accepted that #14's reference [4] is "over-generalized" (`review/round3-adjudication.md`, 'Rulings'), and the referee's audit notes report that the Könnig et al. abstract identifies a stable model ("infinite-branch bigravity") and that Lagos–Ferreira "find stable scalar perturbations for a particular parameter choice" (`review/round3/referee/audit-notes.md`, Step 1). The background is [Bimetric cosmology and its instabilities](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/bimetric-cosmology).
- Standing: #14 and its candidate are retired ([Obituary: superheavy conjugate solitons](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/obituary)), but the question concerns X, which stands ([The momentum graviton X](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton)). No later document closes it, and #11 §14 does not list it.
