*This result explains why ordinary matter shows no sign of the momentum graviton X. Standard Model matter couples to the metric g, and because reciprocity forces the two spin-2 frames to have equal Planck masses, it feels both spin-2 eigenstates, the massless graviton and X, with comparable, gravitational strength. X is massive, so the force it carries is a Yukawa force, cut off beyond a range of order 1/M_X [T], and gravitational-strength forces at such short ranges are not constrained by experiment; X is safe because of its range, not because it decouples. The argument changed across the Debt-1 calc notes; the current form is calc note #8's [D + T], carried by [checkpoint #11](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) §7.5 [D], and it cites no experimental bound for the short-range claim.*

**Status:** Alive — [D + T] in calc note #8 (`notes/debt1a-iv-beta-coefficients.md` §3); [D] in #11 §7.5 — not machine-checked, and no force measurement is cited. Its gravitational-wave side is [Row 6: the exactly massless graviton](/wiki/predictions/massless-graviton).

## Key facts

- Calc note #8 §3 [D + T] (see [The Debt-1 calc notes](/wiki/history/documents/debt-1-notes)): "With equal effective Planck masses (forced by the same symmetry), SM matter — coupled to g — sees both eigenstates with comparable strength; safety is by range, not decoupling: X-mediated forces on the SM are Yukawa-cut at 1/M_X, and gravitational-strength short-range forces are experimentally unconstrained down to far below any plausible M_X." SM is the Standard Model.
- #8 §3 on gravitational-wave bounds: graviton-mass bounds "apply to the even eigenstate, which is exactly massless" ([Spectrum, parameters and reciprocity parity](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/spectrum-and-parity)).
- #11 §7.5 [D]: "SM couplings gravitational-strength and Yukawa-cut at 1/M_X".
- Earlier forms of the argument, superseded by #8's (the one #11 §7.5 carries):
  - calc note #5 §E [O]: "SM safety: X is massive and couples to x-polarized matter only through its (suppressed) dual stress; short-range, fifth-force-safe — to be verified when the propagator exists [O]."
  - calc note #6 §4: "Massive X ⇒ short-ranged, safe; couplings to x-polarized (SM) matter run through the SM's dual stress, which is unenhanced. [Consistency preserved; quantify with the propagator later.]"
  - calc note #7 §4 [T]: "one massless combination (our graviton, coupling to SM stress) and one massive (X); safety requires the massless combination to dominate SM couplings", with graviton-mass bounds from gravitational-wave observations, "m_g ≲ 10⁻²³ eV territory", treated as a check on the mixing angle.
- The open quantitative item beside it (#8 §3): "Parametrics of the χ dual-stress enhancement — why σ₀ can be nuclear-scale while SM couplings stay gravitational — logged as the main open quantitative item." It is item 5 of #11 §14: [Open: the dual-stress enhancement](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/dual-stress-enhancement). The dual stress itself is on [Dual stress T̃](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/dual-stress).
- The dark-energy field τ has a separate fifth-force argument, as a derivatively coupled field: [Fifth-force safety of τ](/wiki/cosmology/dark-energy/tau-fifth-force).
