*The forcing chain is the sequence of arguments in [checkpoint #11](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) §7.1 that leaves the momentum graviton X one consistent option: to be a massive spin-2 field. [Shift symmetry](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/shift-symmetry) rules out X being auxiliary, the [dual diffeomorphisms](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/dual-diffeomorphisms) make it a second graviton, a theorem excludes a second interacting massless graviton, and Lorentz signature leaves a symmetric tensor without gauge symmetry no ghost-free kinetic term. Mass is what remains. The chain answered an earlier three-way question about X (healthy, auxiliary or ghost, the last of which would have killed the program's geometric sector) with "massive spin-2, conditionally healthy", the condition being [the tuning test](/wiki/gravity/tuning-test). It is derived and rests on literature theorems [D + T].*

**Status:** Alive — [D + T] in #11 §7.1 and calc note #6 (`notes/debt1a-ii-X-classification.md` §2) — a chain of symmetry and no-go arguments, not machine-checked; the three-way classification it resolved is superseded.

## Key facts

- #11 §7.1, "The chain [D + T]": "(i) The rigid Sp(2d,ℝ) contains Z → Z + B (B symmetric): X is shift-symmetric — axion-like, never auxiliary. (ii) Locally, dual diffeomorphisms act as δX_μν = ∂₍μλ_ν₎ — a second linearized-diff gauge field, because X is symmetric … Massless X is excluded by the multi-graviton no-go [8†]; ungauged X by Lorentz signature." The † marks a citation from model memory, not yet checked against the source.
- The massless exit, from calc note #6 §2 (see [The Debt-1 calc notes](/wiki/history/documents/debt-1-notes)): "With the gauge invariance, X is a second massless spin-2 — and massless multi-graviton cross-interactions are inconsistent (Boulanger–Damour–Gualtieri–Henneaux). X must couple (to g through the Siegel structure, to χ through the gate), so decoupling is not available. [T]" The theorem is described on [The multi-graviton no-go theorem](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/multi-graviton-no-go).
- The ungauged exit, the signature obstruction (#6 §2): "Without the gauge invariance, no ghost-free two-derivative kinetic term for a Lorentz-covariant symmetric tensor exists (mixed time–space components enter with the wrong sign under any invariant contraction; this is why the Fierz–Pauli structure and its accidental gauge symmetry exist at all). [T — standard]" See [The Fierz–Pauli mass term and the Boulware–Deser ghost](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/fierz-pauli).
- The conclusion (#6 §2): "Therefore X must be massive." The mass comes from [the gravitational Higgs mechanism](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/gravitational-higgs).
- The question the chain resolved (calc note #5, `notes/debt1a-geometric-core.md` §D, marked "[partial]"): X would be "1. healthy propagating → dynamical momentum-space gravity (Born's dream, linearized); 2. auxiliary (no derivatives at quadratic order) → integrate out → a contact, momentum-structured four-χ operator, i.e. the gate delivered directly; 3. ghost → fatal to the geometric sector. Outcomes 1 and 2 both serve the program; only 3 kills." #5 had defined the decisive computation as an expansion of the generalized curvature scalar to quadratic order in X; #6 settled the question by the symmetry and no-go arguments above instead.
- The answer (#6 §5): "Not healthy-by-default, not auxiliary, not necessarily ghost: massive spin-2, conditionally healthy". The condition (#6 §3): "The geometric sector is healthy iff the potential generated by the η-selection lands in the Hassan–Rosen class; a generic potential reintroduces the Boulware–Deser ghost." The class is [dRGT massive gravity](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/drgt-massive-gravity) and [Hassan–Rosen bimetric gravity](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/hassan-rosen-bimetric); the test is [the tuning test](/wiki/gravity/tuning-test).
- How many tests: calc note #7 §5 counts "three structural tests in a row (multi-graviton no-go, signature obstruction, tuning test)"; #11 §15, which governs, counts four consecutive consistency tests, each forcing the theory "onto the unique consistent structure available" ([Assessment: a live research program](/wiki/program/assessment)).
