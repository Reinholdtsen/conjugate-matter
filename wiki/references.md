*This page is the bibliography of the Broken-Born program and of this wiki: every work that the program's documents or the wiki's pages cite, grouped by subject. An entry gives the reference as the citing document writes it and the mark that document attaches to it: † for a reference recalled from model memory and not yet checked against the source, [S] for one retrieved by a live search during the project. It then says what the work is cited for, what checks have been made of it, and which wiki pages cite it. Works that the wiki itself added to explain established physics follow the documents' references in each subject, all marked †. A mark records where a citation came from, not whether the cited work says what it is used for.*

**Status:** Record — a register of citations; it adds no results and changes no mark. Every † reference must be checked against its source before any external use (#11 header); that check is the open [† literature battery](/wiki/method/literature-battery) (OP10).

## How to read this register

- **What is listed.** The reference lists of [checkpoint #11](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) ([1]–[29]), of [conjugate special relativity](/wiki/conjugate-relativity), #13 ([1]–[4]), of the withdrawn soliton manuscript [#14](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate) ([1]–[23]), of the [toy-model note](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/toy-models) ([1]–[17]) and of the phase-space-diameter note ([the phase-space diameter relation](/wiki/cosmology/scale-factor-duality/phase-space-diameter)), whose literature list is alphabetical and unnumbered; works cited only in the text of a document, in the v3 and toy-model worklogs, in the referees' audit notes and scripts, or in `review/wiki-build-flags.md`; and the works the wiki's own pages add. Literature the documents name without citing any work is collected under [named without a reference](#named-without-a-reference).
- **The marks.** #11's header defines [S] as "retrieved by live search during this project" and † as "cited from model memory — verify against the source before external use". #11 marks [1]–[18] and [23] with † and [19]–[22] and [24]–[29] with [S]; `HANDOFF.md` §3 describes version 2.0 as having "25 references flagged † (memory — verify) vs [S] (live-searched)". #13 marks all four of its references †. The phase-space-diameter note defines [S] as "retrieved by live search for this note" and marks every work in its literature list [S]. #14 and the toy-model note carry no marks; their worklogs say how the references were obtained: #14 v3's literature report pulled each record "with no citations from memory", and the toy-model worklog records its searches as "every citation retrieved [S], nothing from memory". The lab notebook (#1) and the v1 paper (#9) have no reference lists and tag cited literature [T], which in #1 means "theorem — established in the literature, cited". See [provenance tags and citation marks](/wiki/method/provenance-tags).
- **An entry** gives the reference as the citing document writes it, then labelled lines. *Marks* gives each document's mark and number, and says where the documents differ. *Cited for* says what each document uses the work for. *Checks* reports what the referees, the worklogs or the wiki build found. *Cited on* lists the wiki pages that cite the work.
- **The Cited on lists** were built mechanically. The finished pages were searched for each work's authors, identifiers and reference numbers, and every hit was read by hand to drop pages that only name a concept after its discoverers, such as the Higuchi bound, without citing the paper. The home page, the status registers, the glossary and the notation page are left out of the lists.
- **Added by the wiki.** Works that the wiki's pages cite from model memory to explain established physics are listed at the end of their subject. No program document cites them; each is marked †, and each line names the pages that cite it.
- **Numbering.** The toy-model note is cited in its version-2 numbering. The [round-4](/wiki/method/review/round-4) referee audited version 1, which had twelve references, and its numbers are given in parentheses where the audit is quoted.
- **The audits.** Round 3 audited #14's list and found all 23 references, "37 individual items", resolving on INSPIRE with matching metadata: "No fabricated or mis-numbered reference found". Round 4 found all of the toy-model note's first-version references resolving. [Round 5](/wiki/method/review/round-5) checked every reference of the phase-space-diameter note's version 1.0 against arXiv, INSPIRE or Crossref, and "every literature citation resolves"; the works added in version 1.1 at the referees' suggestion were retrieved for the note and marked [S] (`review/round5-adjudication.md`, Verdict and ruling R15). None of these rounds audited #11's or #13's list ([Open: the † literature battery (OP10)](/wiki/method/literature-battery)). `HANDOFF.md` §1 summarizes the record at the handoff as "Zero fabricated citations across all checks".
- **A mark is not a verdict.** A reference that exists can still be cited for more than it says: [round 3](/wiki/method/review/round-3/rulings) judged #14's [10] "cited for more than it says" and its [4] over-generalized, and round 5's adjudication counts among the citations of the diameter note's first version that were used for more than they say Egan and Lineweaver's table, Mignemi, the column of Lee's exclusion table, Bhattacharyya et al. and Gabor (rulings R4, R10, R12, R5 and R13), all corrected in version 1.1. A check made outside the † battery, by a referee or during the wiki build, does not change a mark, and #11's † entries stay † until the battery records them.

## Born reciprocity and curved momentum space

- **Born 1938 and 1949.** M. Born, "A suggestion for unifying quantum theory and relativity," Proc. R. Soc. Lond. A 165, 291 (1938); "Reciprocity theory of elementary particles," Rev. Mod. Phys. 21, 463 (1949).
  - Marks: #11 [1]† and [2]†; #13 [3]† and [4]†; #14 [1], unmarked, one entry for both papers; the phase-space-diameter note, [S] for each, retrieved by live search for its §4. #11's and #13's marks stay † until the † battery records the check; the root `CLAUDE.md` notes in the queue that the two papers "were retrieved for the diameter note".
  - Cited for: The exchange (x, p) → (p, −x) as an exact symmetry of quantum kinematics, implemented by the Fourier transform (#11 abstract and §3; #14 §1); Born's program of a physics with both a fundamental mass and a fundamental length (#13 §4, §7). The popular articles #10 and #12 date Born's observation to 1938 without a reference. In the diameter note (§4), Born 1938 is the first statement of the note's question: Einstein's static closed universe has "an upper limit for the distance between two points, given by the radius a of the universe"; "Let us transfer this consideration to the p-space", where an upper limit for momenta is "determined by the radius b of the hypersphere"; Born declines to connect the two radii ("Whether these nuclear constants are connected with the corresponding molar constants λ, κ cannot be decided yet"), fixes b from the classical electron radius, b c = 2ℏc/r₀ ≈ 1.4×10⁸ eV, and leaves open "whether the constant b is universal, or characteristic for each energetically closed system". Born 1949 splits ℏ = ab into a length and a momentum and fixes the length from particle masses, which the note reads as the choice of units of its reciprocity ρ_λ (§5). "Neither paper uses the Planck length."
  - Checks: The round-3 referee, auditing #14, confirmed the metadata of both papers, found that the 1938 abstract does not state the rule and that the primary text was paywalled, confirmed the rule from secondary sources (it names the Wikipedia article "Born reciprocity"), and graded the citation "partially verified (primary text not read)", capitals lowered here (`review/round3/referee/audit-notes.md`, Step 1). For the diameter note both papers were retrieved, Born 1938 as the publisher's own file archived in the Wayback Machine and Born 1949 as the American Physical Society's open copy, with the copies' hashes in the worklog; neither copy has a usable text layer, so the quotations were transcribed from the page images, and the worklog records Born 1938 "read in full" (`notes/phase-space-diameter-worklog.md`, Status; `review/round5-adjudication.md`, header). Of the round-5 referees, referee A could open neither paper; referee B verified the 1938 quotations in the Internet Archive's scan of the volume, could not open the 1949 paper, and noted Born's question whether b is universal, which version 1.1 adds (`review/round5-adjudication.md`, header; `review/round5/referee-A/audit-notes.md` and `review/round5/referee-B/audit-notes.md`, Step 1).
