*The literature battery is the planned check of every reference that the program's papers cite from model memory, marked †, against the published source. A † citation was recalled by Claude rather than retrieved, so its authors, title, journal reference, or the statement it is cited for may be wrong. [Checkpoint #11](../history/documents/checkpoint.md) lists the battery as open problem 10 of its §14 and calls itself "not for submission until the † audit (open item 10) completes". Some of the works involved have been checked in other contexts, Born's two papers have been read for a later note, and one suspected error in [#11](../papers/broken-born-checkpoint-v2.md)'s list has been flagged, but no complete battery is on record. It is part of the first item of the current work queue.*

**Status:** Open — [O] ([#11 §14 item 10](../papers/broken-born-checkpoint-v2.md#14-open-problems)) — no completed battery is recorded in the repository; it gates any submission (#11 footer) and sits in item 1 of the current queue (root [`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md)). It qualifies the † mark defined on [Provenance tags and citation marks](provenance-tags.md) and is listed in [Open problems and the queue](../status/open-problems.md).

## The open item

"Literature battery completion; every † reference source-checked [O]." ([#11 §14 item 10](../papers/broken-born-checkpoint-v2.md#14-open-problems)). In #11's header, [S] marks a reference "retrieved by live search during this project" and † one "cited from model memory — verify against the source before external use" (#11, header; the legend of its reference list says the same more briefly). The battery turns each † into a checked reference, or finds what is wrong with it.

It matters for Tier 2 of #11's [claim tiers](claim-tiers.md): literature results are "correct at the level of well-worn results; exact numbers, conventions, and attributions are flagged † pending source checks" ([#11 §2](../papers/broken-born-checkpoint-v2.md#2-methodological-preface-provenance-and-verification)). A memory citation can be right about the physics and wrong about where it was published, who proved it, or what exactly the paper says.

## Why it gates external use

#11's footer: "Working checkpoint — not for submission until the † audit (open item 10) completes." [`HANDOFF.md` §7](../../HANDOFF.md#7-working-conventions-and-user-preferences): "All †-flagged citations must be source-verified before anything goes external." The root [`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md) gives † the same condition: "from model memory (verify against the source before any external use)".

## What a check involves

The blind referees of rounds 3 to 5 used [a protocol](review/review-protocol.md) that shows what a source check is. Its first step: "Verify every citation against INSPIRE, arXiv or the publisher, including that each one supports what it is cited for." ([`review/round3-preregistration.md`](../../review/round3-preregistration.md), 'Protocol'). There are two parts. The reference must exist with the stated authors, title, journal, volume, page, year and preprint number; and the paper must say what the citing sentence uses it for. The round-3 referee did the second by searching the full text for each cited statement ([`review/round3/referee/audit-notes.md`](../../review/round3/referee/audit-notes.md), Step 1).

## Citation errors found so far

The program's reviews and audits have found errors of each kind the battery targets. All but the last were in other manuscripts; no review has audited #11's list.

- **A citation inverted.** Round 1, on [the soliton manuscript #14](../dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/_index.md): "Preskill citation inverted (fixed)" ([`HANDOFF.md` §6](../../HANDOFF.md#6-review-history-and-the-measured-failure-mode)).
- **Cited for more than it says.** Round 3 found [#14](../../notes/knot-dark-matter-standalone.md)'s reference [4] "over-generalized", Zwanziger's quantization stated with 4π where the manuscript wrote 2π, and Schwinger named but uncited (ruling 12). The referee also claimed that Baumgart et al. do not contain an operator #14 cited them for; the adjudicator overturned this from a footnote, quoted verbatim, that the referee had missed (ruling 8). The referee had searched the full text by keyword and quoted the same page ([`review/round3/referee/audit-notes.md`](../../review/round3/referee/audit-notes.md), Step 1); in the wiki's reading, a check has to read the cited passage, not only search it for terms ([Round-3 verdict, rulings and citation audit](review/round-3/rulings.md)).
- **Wrong details.** Round 4, on the [toy-model note](../symmetry-breaking/toy-models/_index.md): "A. Duncan, not N."; a missing erratum; and, short of an error, one reference's specific claim that "cannot be checked (paywalled)" ([`review/round4-adjudication.md`](../../review/round4-adjudication.md), 'Rulings', Citations; [Review round 4](review/round-4.md)).
- **Cited for more than it says, and a formula quoted without a parameter.** Round 5, on the diameter note: every reference resolves, but the event horizon's entropy is in Egan and Lineweaver's Table 2, not Table 1 (ruling R4); Bhattacharyya et al. never call circuit complexity a clock (R5); Mignemi writes no geometric-mean length (R10); Gabor's "self-reciprocal" refers to Gaussian pulses of any sharpness (R13); and the Yukawa strength of two bimetric papers was quoted without the background ratio both carry, the round's major finding (R1) ([Round 5](review/round-5.md)). All of these were [S] references, retrieved for the note.
- **One entry pairing two papers.** #11's [23]† gives the title and journal of "Bigravitational origin of dark matter" (Phys. Rev. D 94, 084055, which is [arXiv:1604.08564](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.08564)) with [arXiv:1607.03497](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.03497), the number of the companion paper "Heavy spin-2 Dark Matter". A verifier of the wiki build found this by live search; it is flag 20 in [`review/wiki-build-flags.md`](../../review/wiki-build-flags.md), a suspected error, pending, recorded "For the † battery (OP10)" ([bigravity dark matter](../dark-matter/candidates/bigravity-dark-matter.md)).

A related case concerned a convention rather than a citation. Working on the toy models, Claude found that its recalled Fourier eigenvalues contradicted [Grosse–Wulkenhaar](../symmetry-breaking/toy-models/grosse-wulkenhaar/_index.md)'s stated invariance: "So a recalled convention is wrong somewhere." A direct numerical integral settled it, "not a recalled eigenvalue" ([`notes/toy-lagrangian.md`](../../notes/toy-lagrangian.md), 'Chunk 1 results', 'Chunk 2 results').

[`HANDOFF.md` §1](../../HANDOFF.md#1-what-this-project-is) summarized the record at the handoff as "Zero fabricated citations across all checks", and the round-3 referee found all 23 of #14 v3's references, "37 individual items", resolving with matching metadata. The errors above are errors of detail and of support, not invented papers.

## Progress so far

The battery is open; what follows is partial progress, none of which closes it.

- **One † upgraded.** #11 v2.0 marked reference [21], the second data release of the [Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument](../cosmology/dark-energy/desi.md) (DESI), with †. v2.1 retrieved it and marked it [S], and added references [26]–[29], "all INSPIRE-checked" (repository history, commit e1c1f67, the v2.1 amendment).
- **Works checked through other manuscripts.** Several works that #11 cites with † were also cited by #14 v3 or by the toy-model note, and the referees of rounds 3 and 4 resolved them there: Born 1938 and 1949 (#11's [1], [2]; metadata confirmed, the primary text not read), Boulanger–Damour–Gualtieri–Henneaux ([8]), [Hassan–Rosen](../gravity/spin-2-fields/hassan-rosen-bimetric.md) ([10], [11]), Dirac 1931 and Zwanziger 1968 (two of the three works in [17]), Montonen–Olive (one of the two in [18]), and the two 2014 papers of Freidel, Leigh and Minic ([3]) ([`review/round3/referee/audit-notes.md`](../../review/round3/referee/audit-notes.md) and [`review/round4/referee/audit-notes.md`](../../review/round4/referee/audit-notes.md), Step 1, matched against [`notes/knot-dark-matter-standalone.md`](../../notes/knot-dark-matter-standalone.md) and [`wiki/papers/reciprocity-toy-models.md`](../papers/reciprocity-toy-models.md), 'References'). Those checks confirmed the bibliographic data and, except for Born's papers, the support for what the other manuscripts cited each work for. Whether each supports what #11 cites it for remains part of the battery.
- **Born 1938 and 1949, read for the diameter note.** For the note on [the phase-space diameter relation](../cosmology/scale-factor-duality/phase-space-diameter.md) (2026-10-26), both papers were retrieved and read, and the note's reference list marks them [S], "retrieved by live search for this note". The round-5 adjudication records that "Both papers were retrieved for the note", Born 1938 as the publisher's file archived in the Wayback Machine and Born 1949 as the American Physical Society's open copy, with the copies' hashes in the worklog. The worklog adds that both copies "have no usable text layer, so the quotations were transcribed from the page images", and records Born 1938 "read in full". The note's §4 records what they say. In 1938 Born pairs the radius of Einstein's static closed universe, "an upper limit for the distance between two points", with the radius b of a momentum-space hypersphere, "an upper limit for momenta", and declines to connect them: "Whether these nuclear constants are connected with the corresponding molar constants λ, κ cannot be decided yet." He fixes b from the classical electron radius, a particle scale, and leaves open "whether the constant b is universal, or characteristic for each energetically closed system". In 1949 "he splits ħ = ab into a length and a momentum and fixes the length from particle masses", and "Neither paper uses the Planck length." The note's §5 cites the 1949 split as the choice of units in the reciprocity map. The round-5 referees confirmed both papers' bibliographic data; referee A could open neither, and referee B read Born 1938 in the Internet Archive's scanned text of the volume, found the note's quotations there, and could not open Born 1949 ([Round 5](review/round-5.md)).

  #11 still marks the two papers [1]† and [2]†, citing them for reciprocity as "the (x,p) → (p,−x) symmetry of quantum kinematics" (abstract) and for the statement that "the Fourier transform implements it unitarily" ([§3](../papers/broken-born-checkpoint-v2.md#3-the-question)), a statement that needs a scope on a closed space (flag 47; [reciprocity on a closed space](../phase-space/reciprocity/closed-space-reciprocity.md)); #13 marks them [3]† and [4]† and cites them for "Born's 1938–49 program of a self-dual physics with both m and λ fundamental" (§4). The note does not compare its reading with those uses, so whether the papers support what #11 and #13 cite them for is still the battery's to check. The root [`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md) records the retrieval in the queue item itself (below).
- **Metadata checked by the literature check.** The [literature check](literature-check.md) of 2026-11-02 found that the bibliographic data of #11's [3]† (both papers), [13]† (its first paper, Levkov, Panin and Tkachev 2018) and [18]† (Montonen and Olive) match INSPIRE; whether they support what #11 cites them for was not checked ([`notes/literature-check.md` §9](../../notes/literature-check.md#9-what-follows-for-the-record)). Reference [3] and Montonen–Olive had already been checked through other manuscripts (above).
- **A lead for #13.** The diameter note's literature sweep found [Low 2006](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0506031), whose reciprocal relativity bounds forces by a constant b, as prior art for #13's [maximum force](../conjugate-relativity/maximum-force.md) (note §8.1). It is an [S] reference of the note, not one of #13's † entries, and the root [`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md) lists it among the leads that are "not queued". Beside it, §8.1 names Gibbons's maximum-tension conjecture and Caianiello's maximal acceleration as #13 cites them, keeping #13's † ("[P2, §1, †]"), so those two entries are still the battery's to check.
- **The wiki build.** The verifiers of this wiki checked pages against their sources, and at least one † reference was checked by live search, which produced flag 20. Two further flags concern [S] references, outside the battery: [24][S] has no authors and a descriptive title (flag 9), and a live search found that the release #11 cites as [21][S] already covers three years of operations, which bears on the handoff's label "three-year data" for the next release (flag 26).
