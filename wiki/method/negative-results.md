*The program's rule on negative results is that a failed calculation or a contrary datum is reported in full, where the claim is discussed; since 2026-10-29 it has also been that a claim is dead only when theory and data kill it and Paul explicitly concurs. A failed model or version of an idea is not the death of the idea. [Checkpoint #11](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) ranks negative results as the most reliable of its new structural results. Paul was asked on 2026-10-29 whether he concurred in each death the record then held, and answered the same day. He concurred in one, the death of [#14's model](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/obituary) of a dark-matter candidate, superheavy solitons of a hidden gauge sector. He did not concur in the death of [the founding image](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/founding-image) of the program, dark matter clumping in velocity rather than in space, which is his open conjecture again; the calculations that tested it stand. Twelve other claims recorded dead are [under review](/wiki/status/under-review), not dead, until he reviews each, and so is a thirteenth, found on 2026-10-30, that his answer also covers. Under the rule, four of his founding ideas that had been recorded dead or retired without his concurrence are open conjectures again. The wiki gives a dead claim its own obituary page and lists it in [the graveyard](/wiki/status/graveyard), which since that day has held #14's model alone.*

**Status:** Method, in force, revised on 2026-10-29 — untagged — root `CLAUDE.md` ('Method'), `HANDOFF.md` §7 and `wiki/CLAUDE.md`; applied in #11 §9 and §12 and in round-3 Decision 2 (`review/round3-adjudication.md`), and since 2026-10-29 in #11 v2.4's restorations (§10, §11.1, §12, Appendix A), in the narrowing of Decision 2, and in #11 v2.5's record of Paul's answers (§9, §10, §12, §13, §15, Appendix A items 3 and 9).

## The rule

The root `CLAUDE.md` states the rule in two bullets of its method, as revised on 2026-10-29. The first: "Report negative results prominently: a failed calculation or a contrary datum is stated in full, where the claim is discussed." The second: "A claim is dead only when theory and data kill it and Paul explicitly concurs. Paul, 2026-10-29: "Don't kill them off unless theory and data kill them off and you ask me explicitly that I concur. Do not hide the killing in a massive change."" It goes on:

- "Ask him for each death, separately, and record his answer with the date."
- "Make each death its own change, never part of a larger one."
- "Until he concurs, a claim against which theory or data weigh is recorded as open, or as his conjecture, with the evidence against it stated."
- "A failed model or version of an idea is not the death of the idea."
- "Paul answered for the deaths recorded before 2026-10-29 on the same day. He concurred only in #14's model. Every other one is under review, not dead, until he reviews it (wiki status `under-review`), or restored where he did not concur ('Since the handoff', 2026-10-29)."

Until his answers of that day the last bullet read "Deaths recorded before 2026-10-29 stand until he answers the concurrence question for them, except those he has contested."

The rule has two halves. The first is about visibility: a failure is reported in full where a reader will see it, not in a footnote, and `HANDOFF.md` §7 makes it part of Paul's [calibration request](/wiki/method/collaboration#the-calibration), "keep reporting negative results prominently. Honest obituaries are part of the deliverable." The second is about the threshold for a death: theory and data must kill the claim, and Paul must concur, asked about that death alone.

Until 2026-10-29 the bullet read "Report negative results prominently. Claims listed as dead in HANDOFF §2 stay dead.", and its second half was about permanence: once a claim was dead, later work did not bring it back under a new parameter or a new name. The direction came with Paul's statement, which the root `CLAUDE.md` records in full: "I think you prematurely killed off the most interesting parts of the comatter conjecture: that dark matter is conjugate (momentum) matter; and that magnetic monopoles exist in abundance in momentum space. Yes, I know electric/magnetic and space/momentum are different dualities. Also, conjugate gravitational collapse drives positional expansion acceleration. Don't kill them off unless theory and data kill them off and you ask me explicitly that I concur. Do not hide the killing in a massive change." ('Since the handoff', entry "2026-10-29, later the same day"; [timeline](/wiki/history/timeline#pauls-rule-on-deaths-2026-10-29-later)).

## Why negative results count as the most reliable

#11's methodological preface ranks them first among the program's new structural results (Tier 3): "Negative results (§9, §7.1, and the failure of #14's model in §12) are the most reliable Tier-3 items, since the program's incentive gradient ran against them." (#11 §2). The three named are the tests of [momentum clumping](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping) (§9), whose results stand while the founding image has been P.R.'s open conjecture since v2.5, the exclusions in [why X must be massive](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/forcing-chain) (§7.1), and the failure of #14's model (§12; [Obituary: #14's superheavy-soliton model](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/obituary)), which §2 called "the §12 obituary" until the amendment of 2026-10-29 (1c80d01). The tiers, and what "incentive gradient" means, are on [Claim tiers and selection bias](/wiki/method/claim-tiers#negative-results-the-most-reliable-tier-3-items).

## How a negative result is reported

- **In the abstract.** #11's plain-language abstract says that following the idea "put its most cherished consequence — dark matter clumping in velocity instead of space — to the program's own tests, which it failed for particle dark matter and, where it would be mandatory for wave dark matter, at the faintest dwarf galaxies; P.R. holds it open as his conjecture (§9)". Until v2.3 it said that the third candidate "was worked out, failed its own abundance arithmetic, and is retired".
- **In the assessment.** #11 §15 opens its assessment with the founding image's tests: "The founding image failed the program's own calculations for particle dark matter and, where it would have been mandatory for wave dark matter, the test of the faintest dwarf galaxies; P.R. holds it open as his conjecture (v2.5)". Until v2.5 the passage opened with its death ([Assessment: a live research program](/wiki/program/assessment)).
- **In a section of its own.** #11 §9 was titled "Obituary: the death of momentum clumping" from v2.0 to v2.4. - **In the predictions table.** #11 §13 ends: "Withdrawn with cause: #14's superheavy-soliton model (§12; dead, with P.R.'s concurrence of 2026-10-29). Withdrawn and under P.R.'s review, not dead (v2.5): velocity-space granularity in halos; Fornax friction suppression (§9)." ## The rescue that was declined

After round 3, the soliton candidate could have been kept by adding a free [dilution factor](/wiki/cosmology/early-universe/relic-yield-and-dilution#entropy-dilution-t). Decision 2 rejected that route: it would restore the mass "only by adding a free dilution factor of at least 10³–10⁴ (ruling 2), probably far more (ruling 3)", and the mass "would then no longer be a prediction". It cited the clumping obituary as the precedent for keeping a dead claim dead (`review/round3-adjudication.md`, 'Decisions'; [Round-3 decisions and lessons](/wiki/method/review/round-3/decisions)). In the terms of [Lakatos's methodology](/wiki/method/lakatosian-methodology), a free parameter added to save a claim is an ad hoc adjustment (the wiki's reading). The decision was one Paul had delegated, and on 2026-10-29 he narrowed it to #14's model, "which used ordinary kinematics": the declined rescue concerns that model, and "The candidate #14 modeled, conjugate monopoles localized in momentum, was never tested and is P.R.'s open conjecture again (#11 v2.4 §12)" (Decision 2, "Narrowed by Paul (2026-10-29)"); the note goes on: "On 2026-10-29 Paul concurred that #14's model is dead. The clumping obituary cited above as precedent is no longer a death: he did not concur, and the founding image is his open conjecture again (#11 v2.5 §9)." Decision 3 followed: the queued "√N floor" calculation was dropped, because its premise was inverted and "it has no candidate left to serve" ([Under review: the √N topological floor (chat-only)](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/sqrt-n-floor)).

The clumping rows were withdrawn on the same terms: "Rows withdrawn permanently." (`HANDOFF.md` §2); "Rows 3 and 4 of the predictions table are permanently withdrawn, on both islands." (`notes/ufd-endpoint.md`). Since v2.5 #11 has listed both as "Withdrawn and under P.R.'s review, not dead (v2.5)" (§13).

## What a failure leaves standing

## In the wiki

Since 2026-10-29 the wiki's rule has been "Dead means killed by theory and data, with Paul's explicit concurrence" (`wiki/CLAUDE.md`, citing the root `CLAUDE.md`'s 'Method'). Dead claims are reported prominently, on their own pages; a claim whose death Paul has not concurred in, and has contested, is recorded as open or as his conjecture, with the evidence against it stated in full; a failed model or version of an idea is not the death of the idea; "Of the deaths recorded before 2026-10-29, Paul concurred only in #14's model; the others he has not contested are `under-review`, not dead, until he reviews each"; and "The wiki never declares a death on its own reading." Until that day the rule was "Dead stays dead": "Claims killed in `HANDOFF.md` §2, #11 §9 and §12, and the adjudications are reported as dead, prominently, on their own pages." The page of a dead claim has status `dead` and a title beginning "Obituary:", follows the order of #11's obituary (what was claimed, how it was tested, why it failed, what survives), and links the claim's home section, the ruling that killed it and [the graveyard](/wiki/status/graveyard), which since 2026-10-29 has held #14's model alone. The page of a claim under review has status `under-review` and a title beginning "Under review:", keeps the old title's wording after the colon, states the evidence against the claim in full, as results, and is listed in [claims under review](/wiki/status/under-review). A claim under review that never had a page of its own, the closed-universe note's symmetric reading, is told in a folded section headed "Under review: …" on [the page on a closed universe](/wiki/cosmology/scale-factor-duality/closed-universe#under-review-the-symmetric-reading-dv). The pages of the restored ideas keep the record of their deaths as history, each with its date or version.

The old rule covered claims recorded dead in the documents. One obituary rested on the wiki's own reading instead: the page on #12's magnetic identification recorded it as dead although no review or document had killed it. Flag 60 found that such a verdict adds a result, which the wiki does not do; Paul accepted the flag on 2026-10-29, and the page became a conjecture page ([Conjecture: the momentum sector as the magnetic mirror](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification)).
