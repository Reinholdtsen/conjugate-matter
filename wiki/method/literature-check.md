*The literature check is a search of the published physics literature, made on 2026-11-02, for seven of P.R.'s conjectures, to find which of them are already in print. Agents searched in three rounds, a second agent checked all but four of the findings that place a paper close to a conjecture or against it, a panel of judges reread those four with the rest of the evidence, and code found every quotation in the note again in its source. One conjecture, that dark matter is conjugate matter, is published in its core; three are published in part; three were not found. Prior art says who stated an idea first. It does not bear on whether the idea is right, and it changes no claim's status.*

**Status:** Record — a summary of `notes/literature-check.md`, searched and verified by independent agents, not through a blind review round. Its citations were retrieved by live search [S], and [the literature-check script](/wiki/method/verification/literature-check-script) checks its quotations, citations, verdicts and main counts against the stored record, and the algebra behind flag 66, on every run of the gate; it does not test the agents' judgments behind the verdicts. The first conjecture's prior art is read paper by paper in `notes/dual-sector.md` (searched and verified by independent agents and weighed by a panel; not reviewed), whose quotations, citations, dates and counts [the dual-sector script](/wiki/method/verification/dual-sector-script) checks against its record; the same investigation found the work of Winn that the table below gives for C2–C4, and swept conference abstracts.

## How it was done

- **Round 1.** Five searchers, one per theme, each followed by a verifier who reread every finding at its source and searched again.
- **Round 2.** A citation screen: every record in INSPIRE, the database of the high-energy physics literature, that cites one of ten works of the Born-reciprocity literature, 498 records. Four readers read the 143 flagged records that the first round had not assessed.
- **Round 3.** Sweeps outside INSPIRE, through OpenAlex, Crossref, DataCite, Zenodo, other open archives and the web, and through older lines of work.
- **Round 4.** A panel of seven judges reread the evidence for each verdict. One of the three judges for C1 was asked to argue for novelty.

Each finding relates one paper to one conjecture: *anticipates* (the paper states the conjecture in substance), *partial* (a close variant or a central part), *related* (the theme or mechanism), *evidence against*, or *collision* (the same words with another meaning). A conjecture's verdict is the strongest relation that survives verification, a rule written down before the search agents were launched, together with the expected verdicts ([pre-registration](/wiki/method/review/pre-registration)). Four of the seven expected verdicts held, C3's provisionally, and all three misses underestimated the prior art; each miss is a verdict that rests on a reading of the conjecture, and read the other way each would have held, while C5's hit, whose verdict also rests on a reading, would have become a miss. In its four workflows, 31 agents took part and produced 287 findings; in a fifth round, two further judges labeled the closest account of C3 found, Khademi 2009, from its abstract.

**The first conjecture.** A group around Djordje Minic has proposed that dark matter is the dual of the Standard Model in a doubled, Born-reciprocal formulation of string theory, first in a talk of 9 September 2019 and in writing in Minic 2020, posted on 29 February 2020, and restated by Minic and coauthors to April 2026; in the words of Berglund, Hübsch and Minić 2021, dark matter "comes from the degrees of freedom dual to the visible matter" [S]. Every statement is a proposal; none is derived. [Freidel, Leigh and Minic 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.7080) (Phys. Lett. B) call the dual coordinates "coordinates in momentum space" [S], and the group's "dual" has three senses. In its account of quantum mechanics the dual coordinate is momentum, rescaled, in one phase space at ℏ, the program's sense; where the dark sector is built, it is a coordinate of a doubled phase space at the string length, with a momentum of its own; and in one review the dual fields do not commute with ours. So their model doubles phase space where the program polarizes one. "Correlated" is the group's own word for how its dual sector meets ours: the two sectors are independent at leading order in the string length and coupled beyond gravity beyond it, with a strength no paper computes, and in [Hübsch and Minic 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06207) they interact only by gravity below about 4.5×10¹⁴ GeV. The group explains why dark-matter searches have seen nothing so far and expects detection. The program's [conjugate matter](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter) couples to ours by gravity alone under the strict reading of [postulate P4](/wiki/program/postulates/p4-polarized-matter), which the [shared-photon form](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification#shared-photon) of the monopole candidate would need restated, and requires a [permanent null](/wiki/predictions/direct-detection-null) in every non-gravitational search. Its polarization is chosen by the vacuum, in a spontaneous breaking of Born reciprocity with an [order parameter](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/order-parameter) ([P5](/wiki/program/postulates/p5-spontaneous-selection)); the group places a breaking tied to the choice of polarization in the vacuum, for another symmetry and with no order parameter (flag 70, pending). Neither of the program's two forms, [χ particles](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/chi-particles) and [conjugate monopoles](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/momentum-space-monopoles), was found in the literature, inside the group or outside it, nor in a sweep of conference abstracts, slides and proceedings from 2015 to 2026, which found the identification itself only in the group's own talks. The group's papers are set beside the program's claims, element by element, on [dark matter as the dual Standard Model](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/dual-standard-model).

Four verdicts rest on a reading of the conjecture that the judges state. C1's would be partly anticipated if strict P4 and ordinary momentum, rather than the dual coordinate at the string scale, belong to its substance, the reason one judge gave for medium confidence, or if its gloss, matter *organized around momentum*, is read as a clause about dynamics, dark matter that interacts or is localized in momentum, a dissent kept on record by the judge asked to argue for novelty. C4's would be related only if the placement in momentum alone is the conjecture. C5's counts the partner's identity, the momentum description of our own universe, as part of the conjecture; if that identity is counted as framework, Henry-Couannier's contracting partner could be called an anticipation of the conjecture's founding sentence, though never of the route that remains, a reading its judge does not adopt; the round-1 verifier for C5 finds an anticipation arguable only for the founding sentence read as a statement about how the two scale factors move, since in Dark Gravity the contraction of the conjugate side does not power our acceleration. C6's counts Bose condensation of dark matter as a form of the conjecture; restricted to the founding image of several velocity flocks, it would be related only. Which reading is P.R.'s conjecture is open for each of the four ([the open items](/wiki/status/open-problems#open-from-the-literature-check)).

The page of each conjecture linked above gives the details under "In the literature": a section, or for the first a paragraph of its audit entry, with the group's proposal set out on [dark matter as the dual Standard Model](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/dual-standard-model).

## What it means for the checkpoint

No claim's status changes. The suggested changes concern what [checkpoint #11](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) cites, in six flags of `review/wiki-build-flags.md`, all pending:

- **Flag 64:** #11 cites none of the published work on C1's identification; its addendum suggests citing as well the talk of 2019 and Minic 2020 §5–§6.2 for the identification, and Hübsch and Minic 2024 for its regime of coupling by gravity alone.
- **Flag 65:** the partial prior art for C4, C5 and C6 is uncited in #11 §11.4, §10 and App. A items 3 and 9.
- **Flag 66:** [the duality dictionary](/wiki/cosmology/scale-factor-duality/duality-dictionary#in-the-literature) is, as a map of fluid variables, the scale-factor duality of string cosmology with sources.
- **Flag 67:** the bridge between electric–magnetic duality and Born reciprocity behind [one enlarged modular structure](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/unified-modular-structure#in-the-literature) is a published remark.
- **Flag 68:** the exclusion of [the minimal hidden sector](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/w-prime-overclosure#in-the-literature) was published first, independently, by [Brümmer, Ferrante, Fischer and Frigerio](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21924) (arXiv 2025, before #14 v3; Phys. Rev. D 2026).
- **Flag 69:** [row 1](/wiki/predictions/self-interaction-knee#the-data-situation)'s "zero cluster signal (particle χ)" meets two fits that mildly prefer a nonzero cross-section at cluster speeds.

The note also sets out published work that bears on #11 without changing a verdict: a third way to hide the magnetic analogs of the [shared-photon form](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification#shared-photon), screening by a magnetic condensate; that where one kind of charge is the only one, which kind is called magnetic is a convention, which bears on the [separate-photon form](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification#separate-photon); that the superseded rule of [the duality dictionary](/wiki/cosmology/scale-factor-duality/duality-dictionary#in-the-literature) is itself a published map, and that in a contracting universe anisotropy grows like a stiff fluid; a published measurement for the statement that the data exclude w = −2 (w is dark energy's [equation of state](/wiki/cosmology/dark-energy/quintessence#dark-energy-and-the-equation-of-state), its pressure divided by its energy density); a mechanism other than the gate for scattering that is strong in dwarfs and weak in clusters; published counterparts, without clumping, of [Fornax friction suppression](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/fornax-friction) and [velocity-space granularity in halos](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/velocity-granularity), two consequences the lab notebook drew from [clumping in momentum](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/founding-image); and sources for two statements of #11 §11.3 that carry no citation (`notes/literature-check.md` §3–§7, §9).

For [the † literature battery](/wiki/method/literature-battery), the check found that the bibliographic data of #11's references [3]† (both papers), [13]† (its first paper) and [18]† (Montonen and Olive) match INSPIRE. Whether they support what #11 cites them for was not checked.

## Limits

A "not found" means not found by these searches.

- OpenAlex's free daily quota ran out during round 3, so the sweeps finished on the other indexes and the web, and the OpenAlex queries planned after that point did not run, among them four on explanations of the monopole null result. A rerun on 2026-11-03 found the quota not yet renewed, and Semantic Scholar refused too; the queries are listed in `review/literature-check/round5/not-run-queries.json`.
- The closest account of C3, Khademi 2009, "Why magnetic monopoles are not seen?", is read only in its abstract; its two pages, held in a repository that refuses access, could raise C3's verdict, which is provisional until they are read.
- Literature not in English was not searched.
- The large output of Carlos Castro Perelman on Born's reciprocal relativity was screened through INSPIRE and read only in part.
- The passages of two sources, Henry-Couannier 2018 and [Goldhaber 2017](https://doi.org/10.1063/PT.3.3571), were read only through a web fetch, since the code checks cannot read them; the note paraphrases them.
- The labels are agents' judgments, tested by verifiers, a panel and code, not by a human expert.
