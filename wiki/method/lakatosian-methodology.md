*Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes is an account, from the philosophy of science, of how a body of scientific work is judged over time rather than one hypothesis at a time. A programme holds a hard core of assumptions fixed and surrounds it with auxiliary hypotheses that are revised when predictions fail. It counts as progressive while its revisions lead to new predictions, some of which are confirmed, and as degenerating while its revisions only absorb failures after the fact. The Broken-Born program describes its own status in these terms, "a Lakatosian research program, not a theory", and checkpoint #11 closes with an assessment sorted into hard core, progressive signs, degeneration risks and falsification exposure.*

**Status:** Established — [T], explained here from general knowledge; no source document cites Lakatos's own work, so the citation below is † — the program's use of it is the self-assessment in [Assessment: a live research program](/wiki/program/assessment), a record, not a result.

## Key facts

- **The methodology [T].** Imre Lakatos proposed it as a refinement of Karl Popper's falsificationism. A research programme has a hard core that its negative heuristic shields from refutation, a protective belt of auxiliary hypotheses that takes the blame for conflicts with data and is modified to absorb them, and a positive heuristic, a plan for developing the belt. A change to the belt is theoretically progressive if it predicts some novel fact, empirically progressive if some of those predictions are confirmed, and degenerating if it is ad hoc. A single failed prediction does not refute a programme; on Lakatos's account a degenerating programme is displaced by a progressive rival. (I. Lakatos, "Falsification and the Methodology of Scientific Research Programmes"†.)
- **The criterion as the program states it.** "Programs that keep predicting novel facts are progressive; programs that only redescribe are degenerating." (#9 §11, the v1 assessment, superseded as reference by #11 §15.)
- **The program's hard core.** #11 §15: "hard core (spontaneously broken reciprocity, ω-only rigidity) intact, twice sharpened by forced corrections (P2′; the w-dictionary)." See [the hard core](/wiki/program/hard-core), [P2′](/wiki/program/postulates/p2-prime) and [the duality dictionary](/wiki/cosmology/scale-factor-duality/duality-dictionary).
- **The assessment's categories.** #11 §15 sorts the record into progressive signs ("forced convergence onto consistency islands; predictions not previously posed (rows 3, 11); an unsolicited identity (§7.3)", among others), degeneration risks ("tunable wave-island collective sector (quarantined); gate tail not yet forced", among others) and falsification exposure ("genuine and near-term (rows 1, 4, 7)"). The full lists are on [Assessment: a live research program](/wiki/program/assessment).
