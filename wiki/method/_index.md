*The method is the set of rules by which the Broken-Born program labels, checks and reviews its own claims, together with the working arrangement between Paul and Claude that the rules serve. Every claim carries a tag saying where it came from, every asserted number is to be computed by a script kept in the repository, the scripts run in one gate that must pass before work counts as finished, and manuscripts go to blind referees only after the expected verdict has been written down. The rules exist because physics drafted by an AI has a known failure mode, fluent confabulation, and because Paul cannot independently check most of the technical physics; checkpoint #11 says that "every claim here carries a tag saying where it came from" for that reason (§2).*

## Map

The section runs from the frame the program judges itself by, through the arrangement that produces its claims and the labels that qualify them, to the machinery that checks them. Only the first page is established material from outside the program; the rest record how this program works.

- [Lakatos's methodology of research programmes](/wiki/method/lakatosian-methodology) comes first because it supplies the vocabulary of the program's self-description: a hard core held fixed, auxiliary claims that may be revised, and programmes that are progressive or degenerating. It is established philosophy of science [T].
- [The collaboration: roles, attribution and conventions](/wiki/method/collaboration) says who does what. Paul (P.R.) supplies conjecture and direction and holds a veto; Claude supplies derivations, counterexamples, verification and drafting. The rest of the method exists so that Paul can tell sound results from confabulation without checking the physics himself.
- [Provenance tags and citation marks](/wiki/method/provenance-tags) defines the labels every claim carries ([D] [T] [P] [O] [V], and † or [S] on each citation) and records how the tag system grew from the lab notebook to #11.
- [Claim tiers and selection bias](/wiki/method/claim-tiers) ranks the claims by how far they can be trusted, from machine-checked algebra to novel structural physics at an "honest prior per claim ~50–80%" (#11 §2), and names the bias the program watches for.
- [Open: the † literature battery](/wiki/method/literature-battery) follows the tags because it is the open task they create: every reference cited from model memory in #11 and #13 still has to be checked against its source before any external use.
- [Negative results and dead claims](/wiki/method/negative-results) states the rule that failures are reported prominently and stay dead, and why #11 counts them among its most reliable results.
- [Verification](/wiki/method/verification) holds the rules for numbers (the iron rule, independent anchors, the reduced-model and error-bar rules), the gate `scripts/check.sh`, and one page for each family of scripts the gate runs.
- [Blind review](/wiki/method/review) closes the section: fresh AI referees, the expected verdict written down in advance, the referees' measured failure mode, and the four review rounds.
