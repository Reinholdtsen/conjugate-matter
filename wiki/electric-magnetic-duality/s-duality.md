*S-duality is the proposed equivalence between a gauge theory at coupling g and a theory at a coupling of order 1/g in which electrically charged particles and [magnetic monopoles](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles) exchange roles, so that a strongly coupled theory has a weakly coupled description. It grows out of a symmetry of Maxwell's equations in empty space, which are unchanged when the electric and magnetic fields are exchanged. Montonen and Olive proposed the quantum version in 1977. [The checkpoint](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) records it as exact in maximally supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory, one of the few theories in which it has passed non-trivial tests, and Seiberg's related dualities supply the "magnetic quarks" of a dual description. In string theory S-duality sits with T-duality inside a larger U-duality. The program uses S-duality as a structural comparison, because it acts on the coupling through the same matrix as [Born reciprocity](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/born-reciprocity), and it carries a standing caution: outside special supersymmetric theories the duality is an expectation, not an established symmetry.*

## Duality in Maxwell's equations [T]

Without charges, Maxwell's equations keep their form when the fields are exchanged as E → B, B → −E (units with c = 1). The exchange is a quarter-turn in the plane of (E, B), and applying it twice reverses both fields:

```text
(E, B) → (B, −E) → (−E, −B)
```

Electric charges spoil the symmetry, because Gauss's law for E has a source and Gauss's law for B does not. Adding magnetic charges and currents restores it, with the electric and magnetic sources exchanged together with the fields. A particle carrying magnetic charge alone is a magnetic monopole ([magnetic monopoles](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles)).

In quantum mechanics the two kinds of charge are not independent. Dirac showed in 1931 that a monopole of charge g can coexist with an electric charge e only if their product is quantized; the checkpoint writes the condition e·g ~ 2π (#11 §11.2; [Dirac quantization and the DSZ pairing](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/dirac-quantization), where DSZ is Dirac–Schwinger–Zwanziger). A small electric charge therefore forces a large magnetic one. Exchanging the electric and magnetic descriptions of a theory exchanges weak coupling with strong coupling, which is why the quantum form of the duality is also called strong–weak duality. #11 §11.1 abbreviates it as "electric–magnetic (E↔B, g → 1/g)".

## The coupling and the generator S [T]

A gauge theory's coupling g and its vacuum angle θ combine into one complex number, the complexified coupling. In the usual normalization τ_c = θ/2π + 4πi/g² [T]. The notation is #11's; τ_c is unrelated to the dark-energy field τ ([notation](/wiki/notation)). Duality transformations act on τ_c by fractional linear maps with integer coefficients:

```text
τ_c → (a τ_c + b)/(c τ_c + d),    a, b, c, d integers,  ad − bc = 1      (the group SL(2,ℤ))
T = [[1, 1], [0, 1]]:   τ_c → τ_c + 1      (θ shifted by 2π)
S = [[0, 1], [−1, 0]]:  τ_c → −1/τ_c       (electric ↔ magnetic)
```

At θ = 0 the S map sends τ_c = 4πi/g² to ig²/4π, which has the same form with g replaced by 4π/g [T]. #11 §11.3 states the action the program uses: "S-duality acts on the complexified coupling by τ_c → −1/τ_c, generated by the same [[0,1],[−1,0]] rotation as Born's ρ and our Z → −Z⁻¹". The same matrix is the quarter-turn of the Maxwell exchange above, of Born reciprocity, (x, p) → (p, −x), and of the program's [modular inversion](/wiki/phase-space/modular-field/modular-inversion). The program's use of the coincidence is the subject of [the symplectic charge lattice](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/symplectic-charge-lattice).

## Montonen–Olive duality [T]

The simplest gauge theory with monopoles has an SU(2) symmetry broken to U(1) by a Higgs field in the adjoint representation. Its spectrum contains electrically charged vector bosons W± of mass g·v, where v is the symmetry-breaking scale, and magnetically charged ['t Hooft–Polyakov monopoles](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/thooft-polyakov-monopole) of mass M = 4πf v/g, with f = 1 in the Bogomolny–Prasad–Sommerfield (BPS) limit of a vanishing Higgs self-coupling (#14 §2, §3 give both formulas for the hidden sector). In that limit the exchange g → 4π/g turns the W mass into the monopole mass and back [T].

Montonen and Olive took the symmetry of the mass spectrum as a sign of an exact duality: a dual theory, at coupling 4π/g, in which the monopoles are the elementary gauge particles and the W bosons are the solitons. Their title states the conjecture as a question, "Magnetic monopoles as gauge particles?" (#11 ref [18]†).

The proposal has a defect in the bosonic theory, which the v3 worklog states: "the bosonic spectrum mismatches (W spin 1, monopole spin 0)". The W is a vector particle and the monopole is not, so the two cannot be exchanged by an exact symmetry. In N = 4 super Yang–Mills theory (SYM), which has the most supersymmetry (SUSY) a four-dimensional gauge theory without gravity can have, the monopoles come in multiplets that include spin 1, so the mismatch disappears; #14 cites Osborn 1979 for this setting (#14 ref [8]). The checkpoint's summary is that Montonen–Olive duality is "exact in maximally supersymmetric Yang–Mills" (#11 §11.2 [T]).

How well tested the exactness is depends on the theory. Harvey's lectures, as the round-3 referee quoted them after checking the source: the duality has been "put to non-trivial tests in finite N = 4 Super Yang-Mills theory and special finite N = 2 theories", while for the non-supersymmetric theory "there is no reason to think that the duality of the spectrum should be maintained by quantum corrections" (`review/round3/referee/audit-notes.md`, Step 1).

## Seiberg duality and magnetic quarks [T]

Seiberg found a related equivalence in N = 1 supersymmetric versions of quantum chromodynamics (QCD), the theory of quarks and gluons. A theory with a given number of colors and quark flavors has, at long distances, the same physics as a second gauge theory with a different number of colors, whose charged matter fields are called "magnetic quarks" [T, Seiberg 1995 [18†]]. In part of the range of flavors, the second description is weakly coupled where the first is strongly coupled. The equivalence holds for the long-distance limit of the two theories, not at every scale [T].

#11 cites it for the vocabulary: "magnetic quarks" is "standard vocabulary via Seiberg duality [18†]" (§11.2). The checkpoint's italic opening dates the idea "proposed in 1977, named in 1994"; its reference list dates Seiberg's paper 1995, the journal year, while the preprint number hep-th/9411149 dates from November 1994 [T]. The popular article #12 says that "'magnetic quark' has been standard vocabulary in field theory since the 1990s" ([the popular articles](/wiki/history/documents/popular-articles)).

## T-, S- and U-duality in string theory [T]

String theory has dualities of two kinds [T]:

- **T-duality** relates a closed string theory on a circle of radius R to the same or a related theory on a circle of radius 1/R in string units. A closed string on a circle carries two whole numbers, its momentum around the circle, as a wave on a ring does, and the number of times it winds; T-duality exchanges them. The doubled geometry that makes the exchange manifest is the subject of [double field theory](/wiki/phase-space/born-geometry/double-field-theory).
- **S-duality** in string theory relates strong and weak string coupling, and in some compactifications reduces to Montonen–Olive duality of the four-dimensional gauge theory.

Hull and Townsend (1995)† proposed that in the type II superstrings on a torus both are parts of a single larger discrete symmetry, U-duality. #11 §11.3 describes the situation as "T- and S-duality cohabiting inside U-duality in string theory [T]", and uses it as the model for its conjecture that the program's two polarization choices are parts of one structure ([open problem 8](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/unified-modular-structure); OP8 is item 8 of #11 §14).

## The exactness burden

Any argument that needs S-duality as an exact symmetry outside supersymmetric theories inherits the caution above. The program met the burden through the soliton manuscript #14, whose second version fixed the hidden sector's coupling by a conjectured action of reciprocity as the modular S map ([Obituary: the coupling α_d ≈ 34](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/alpha-d-coupling)).

- **Round 2 posed it.** Among the valid hits pending for v3: "SUSY-exactness burden for Montonen–Olive (either reciprocity supplies the rigidity or the dual sector is approximately SUSY)" (`HANDOFF.md` §6; [review rounds 1 and 2](/wiki/method/review/rounds-one-and-two)).
- **The worklog answered it** as item R1, marked "ready": "Exact S-duality has been tested only in N=4 SYM and special finite N=2 theories. For non-SUSY theories there is "no reason to think" it survives quantum corrections, and the bosonic spectrum mismatches (W spin 1, monopole spin 0). That is an expectation, not a no-go [Harvey hep-th/9603086; Osborn 1979]. v3 must state it. If F2 is adopted, S no longer carries α_d, and the burden shrinks to the §2 "full mirror spectrum" remark." (`review/v3-worklog.md`, 'Round-2 pending list').
- **F2 was adopted.** Finding F2 separated the hidden sector's weak elementary coupling from its monopoles' strong coupling, and Paul decided on 2026-10-19 that "S-duality is no longer what fixes α_d" ('Findings neither referee raised'). The conjecture that reciprocity acts on couplings as S is superseded.
- **v3 stated it.** "Exact electric-magnetic duality is also not assumed. It has been tested non-trivially only in N=4 super-Yang-Mills and certain finite N=2 theories, and there is no reason to expect it to survive quantum corrections in a non-supersymmetric theory [8]. Nothing here requires it: the monopole physics used is semiclassical, at alpha_W' ~ 1/34." (#14 §2). The manuscript was withdrawn after round 3 over its abundance arithmetic ([Obituary: superheavy conjugate solitons](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/obituary)).
- **The checkpoint today** cites Montonen–Olive as literature for the [magnetic mirror-family](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-mirror-family), stating exactness only for maximally supersymmetric Yang–Mills (§11.2), and uses S only for its action on τ_c (§11.3).

## How the program uses it

- **As established physics behind [founding supposition 11](/wiki/program/founding-suppositions).** P.R.'s supposition of "A full magnetic mirror-family, quarks included" is "alive as established physics (Montonen–Olive, Seiberg, dual superconductor)" (#11 App. A item 11; [the dual superconductor](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/dual-superconductor)).
- **As the third appearance of one matrix.** S's matrix is the matrix of ρ and of the modular inversion Z → −Z⁻¹ ([reciprocity as modular inversion](/wiki/phase-space/modular-field/modular-inversion)); #11 calls this "the third appearance of one matrix in this program" ([the symplectic charge lattice](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/symplectic-charge-lattice)).
- **As the model for open problem 8.** The U-duality inclusion is the analogy for one enlarged structure containing both polarization choices ([open problem 8](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/unified-modular-structure)).
- **Not as an explanation of the missing monopoles.** The audit that killed that explanation turned on the point that electric–magnetic duality, not Born reciprocity, is the duality that relates magnetic charges to electric ones ([Obituary: conjugate monopoles explain the null result](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopole-explanation)).
