*This is P.R.'s conjecture that [magnetic monopoles](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles) exist but belong to the conjugate, momentum-polarized half of physics, so that searches, which look for objects localized in position, do not find them. It is his [founding supposition 5](/wiki/program/founding-suppositions#5-monopoles-live-in-conjugate-space-explaining-non-detection). [The checkpoint](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) gives two grounds against it: the symmetry that exchanges electric and magnetic charge is [electric–magnetic duality](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/s-duality), not the position–momentum [reciprocity](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/born-reciprocity) the program is built on; and the absence is expected under several standard histories of the early universe, [inflationary dilution](/wiki/cosmology/early-universe/inflation-and-dilution) among them. Neither refutes it: one form of P.R.'s [magnetic-mirror conjecture](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification) contests the first, and a null result that is expected anyway lends the explanation no support without refuting it. That form, in which the momentum-polarized sector carries magnetic charges of our own photon, is the explanation's setting. It needs the postulate that keeps the two sectors' forces apart restated, and something that hides the objects from detectors and from astrophysical bounds. None of this has been computed.*

**Status:** Conjecture — [O] — P.R.'s conjecture (#11 §11.1, App. A item 5; flag 62 in `review/wiki-build-flags.md`). Neither ground given against it refutes it, and the null result lends it no support. Its setting is §11.4's shared-photon form, which needs P4 restated and conditions that hide the objects, none of them computed. No derivation, calculation or script stands behind it. Home section: [electric–magnetic duality](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality); listed with the [conjectures](/wiki/status/conjectures).

## The conjecture

A magnetic monopole is a particle carrying magnetic charge alone: a north pole without a south pole. Many theories that unify the forces predict them, and none has been detected ([magnetic monopoles](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles); [monopole flux bounds](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-searches)) [T].

P.R.'s founding supposition 5 offers an explanation drawn from the program's picture of two polarizations of matter: "Monopoles live in conjugate space, explaining non-detection" ([#11](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) App. A item 5; [founding suppositions and their fates](/wiki/program/founding-suppositions)). In the program's postulates, matter comes in an x-polarized sector, ours, and a p-polarized sector, χ, and "gauge forces do not cross polarizations" (#11 §4, P4; [P4: polarized matter and strict sector separation](/wiki/program/postulates/p4-polarized-matter)). On the supposition, the monopoles belong to the second sector.

P.R.'s argument is that searches are made in position space and conjugate monopoles are by definition compact in momentum space, so the searches would not detect them, and this explains their absence (flag 62). An object compact in momentum is spread in position by about ħ/Δp, as a narrowband signal is long in time [T] ([the Fourier transform](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/fourier-transform)).

#11 §11.1 records the explanation of the null result as open [O], P.R.'s conjecture, "since neither ground given against it kills it". Its audit of the founding suppositions (App. A item 5) lists "Monopoles live in conjugate space, explaining non-detection" as "**open in both halves**":

- as explanation, "P.R.'s conjecture [O] under §11.4's shared-photon form, which neither ground kills (wrong duality is contested by that form, which needs P4 restated; the null result is expected under several standard histories, so it lends no support either way — flags 59, 62)";
- as ontology, "alive and consequence-bearing (P.R.'s "true-but-irrelevant is measure zero"), with P.R.'s conjecture that such monopoles are abundant in momentum space (§12); #14's model of the candidate it fed failed (§12). Its ontology half, joined with item 11, is P.R.'s magnetic-mirror conjecture (§11.4) [O]."

The italic opening of §11 puts it in plain words: "The founding idea that conjugate-space monopoles explain why no compass finds a north pole without a south is open, as P.R.'s conjecture."

BBT, Broken-Born theory, is the program's earlier name. The parenthesis "(p-polarized solitons)" is the explanation. The first paper-shaped consolidation, v1 (#9; [the checkpoint and its predecessor](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint)), already carried the row without it, "Permanent null; Berry-curvature monopoles in solids as the only shadow", and had no section on monopoles (`notes/broken-born-program-v1.md` §9).

The checkpoint's monopole audit is §11.1, in the charge-lattice section added in v2.0 (`HANDOFF.md` §3). No version of the audit before v2.0's is in the repository.

The repository's one-line description reads "Physics hypothesis explaining dark matter and missing magnetic monopoles" (`README.md`), which states as the program's result what the record holds as an open conjecture (flag 37; [CLAUDE.md and the README](/wiki/history/documents/project-instructions)).

## The grounds against it

#11 §11.1, "The monopole audit, revised [D]", gives two grounds against the explanation. They are reasoning from established physics, not calculation; no script and no review round checked them.

> The relevant duality is electric–magnetic (E↔B, g → 1/g), not x↔p; only the shared-photon form of §11.4 contests this ground, and that form needs P4 restated. The null result is expected under several standard histories, so it lends the explanation no support: inflation after a unification transition dilutes monopoles [T], and without grand unification there are none to dilute, since the Standard Model has no topologically stable monopoles [T]; flag 59 lists further histories (†). Inflation is the standard account, not an observed epoch.

### The wrong duality

Two different symmetries are in play.

- **Electric–magnetic duality** acts on fields and charges: it exchanges the electric and magnetic fields, E ↔ B, and with them electric and magnetic charges. Because Dirac's condition fixes the product of the two kinds of charge, it also exchanges weak and strong coupling, the "g → 1/g" of #11's sentence ([S-duality and Montonen–Olive](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/s-duality); [Dirac quantization and the DSZ pairing](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/dirac-quantization)) [T].
- **Born reciprocity**, the symmetry the program is built on, acts on phase space: ρ: (x, p) → (p, −x), implemented on quantum states by the [Fourier transform](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/fourier-transform) (#11 §3; [Born reciprocity](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/born-reciprocity)).

The ground is that the supposition uses the second symmetry to explain a fact about the first. Whether a particle carries magnetic charge is a question about where it sits among electric and magnetic charges; which polarization its matter belongs to is a question about position and momentum. The duality that relates magnetic charges to electric ones, and that bears on why one kind is seen and the other is not, is electric–magnetic duality. The same section of the checkpoint finds that this duality has its own account of why magnetic particles are not seen: Dirac's condition makes them strongly coupled and heavy ([the magnetic mirror-family](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-mirror-family)). The observation of §11.3 that the two dualities share a matrix is, on its own, a structural comparison, not a mechanism that moves monopoles between sectors ([the symplectic charge lattice](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/symplectic-charge-lattice)).

The ground assumes that the two dualities are independent. §11.3 also conjectures [O] that the phase-space polarization and the charge-lattice polarization are components of one enlarged structure ([open problem 8](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/unified-modular-structure)), and flag 59 noted that #11 stated both without noting the conflict. P.R.'s magnetic-mirror conjecture is what §11.3's conjecture would give if one vacuum event selected both the position polarization and the electric sublattice (#11 §11.4). In its shared-photon form the momentum-polarized sector's analogs carry magnetic charge of our photon, and §11.1 names that form as the one that contests the ground.

### Expected under standard histories [T]

#11 §11.1 names two histories, each tagged [T], under which no monopole of our sector would be seen today, and points to flag 59 for more.

- **Inflation after a unification transition.** A symmetry-breaking transition in the early universe that can produce monopoles produces at least about one per causally connected region ([the Kibble mechanism](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/kibble-mechanism)), and for superheavy monopoles this is far too many: "density of such monopoles in the early universe is estimated to be unacceptably large" (Preskill 1979, as [the round-3 audit](/wiki/method/review/round-3/rulings) quotes the abstract). Annihilation does not remove enough of them; the v3 worklog records Preskill's result that "annihilation cannot reduce n/T³ below ~10⁻¹⁰ at m ≈ 10¹⁶ GeV" ('Findings neither referee raised', F1). A period of inflation after the monopoles form dilutes them to an unobservable density, provided reheating stays below the transition ([the monopole problem](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-problem); [inflation, reheating and entropy dilution](/wiki/cosmology/early-universe/inflation-and-dilution)). Inflation is the standard account, not an observed epoch.
- **No grand unification.** The monopoles in question are those of grand unified theories. The Standard Model has no topologically stable monopoles, since the vacuum manifold of its electroweak breaking is the 3-sphere and π₂(S³) = 0 ([why such a monopole is stable](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/thooft-polyakov-monopole#why-it-is-stable-winding-t)), so without unification there are none to dilute.

Flag 59 lists further standard histories that the record does not mention, all cited from memory (†): a maximum temperature below the unification scale; symmetry non-restoration at high temperature (Dvali, Melfo and Senjanović 1995); a temporary phase in which electromagnetism is broken (Langacker and Pi 1980); and domain walls sweeping monopoles away (Dvali, Liu and Vachaspati 1998). The record applies a late release of entropy only to hidden-sector monopoles (#11 §12 [29S]; round-3 ruling 3).

The strong magnetic coupling of #11 §11.2 is not such a history. Preskill's analysis already concerns strongly coupled Dirac monopoles, whose annihilation is inefficient (flag 59; [annihilation is not enough](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-problem#annihilation-is-not-enough-t)). §11.2's "heavy and bound" remains #11's account of why the magnetic mirror-family is not seen ([why the mirror is not seen](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-mirror-family#why-the-mirror-is-not-seen)), not a cosmological history.

With these accounts available, the null result is expected without a conjugate sector, so it gives the explanation no support.

### Why neither ground kills it

- **The first ground is contested.** It assumes that the two dualities are independent, while §11.3 conjectures [O] that they are one structure (flag 59), and §11.1 names §11.4's shared-photon form as the form that contests it. Flag 62 calls that form "item 5's explanation in a new shape: magnetic charges of our photon in the p-polarization, unseen because they are not localized in position". The form needs P4 restated, and P4 is a postulate [P] (flag 60).
- **The second ground shows the explanation unnecessary, not false.** Inflationary dilution accounts for the absence only if a unification transition produced monopoles, inflation followed it and reheating stayed below it (flag 59). An expected null result lends the explanation "no support either way" (App. A item 5).

§11's plain-language passage sums up both grounds: "so their absence lends the idea no support; neither point refutes it." Beyond the two grounds, what stands against the explanation is "P4 as written, which excludes the form, together with the form's uncomputed conditions (§11.4)" (flag 62).

## Its setting: the shared-photon form

P.R.'s magnetic-mirror conjecture (#11 §11.4 [O]) holds that momentum matter is similar to position matter, each electrically charged x-polarized particle having a magnetically charged p-polarized analog. It has two forms ([Conjecture: the momentum sector as the magnetic mirror](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification)).

- In the **separate-photon form**, #14 v2's, the analogs are magnetic under the p-polarized sector's own gauge field. That form "is consistent with P4 and leaves the absence of our own monopoles to §11.1", so the explanation has no place in it ([the separate-photon form](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification#separate-photon)).
- In the **shared-photon form**, the reading of [the popular article #12](/wiki/history/documents/popular-articles), the analogs carry magnetic charge of our photon: "the magnetic half of electromagnetism lives in the p-polarization, and by Dirac's condition their existence would quantize electric charge [T, 17†]" (#11 §11.4; [the conjecture page](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification#shared-photon)).

§11.4 ends its account of the shared-photon form by naming it the setting of App. A item 5's explanation of the null result (flag 62): it "requires P4 restated and the hiding conditions above, none of them yet computed".

The conditions are these.

- **P4 restated.** "P4 as written excludes this form ("gauge forces do not cross polarizations", in the program since #1), so it needs P4 restated" (§11.4). P4 is also read strictly, with no kinetic mixing between the sectors beyond what gravity induces (round-3 Decision 1; [P4](/wiki/program/postulates/p4-polarized-matter)).
- **Hiding.** "Localized in position at dark-matter density, such objects would exceed the Parker bound at #14's benchmark masses (#14 v2 and v3 §4; not gated)" ([the Parker bound](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-searches#the-parker-bound-t)). #14 made the comparison for its own objects as if they carried magnetic charge under our photon, at dark-matter density and at its benchmark: their flux "exceeds the Parker bound (~1e-15 for M <~ 1e17 GeV) [20] by 7.5 orders of magnitude" (#14 §4; withdrawn manuscript). §11.4 continues: "Something must therefore hide them, and neither option has been computed". One is p-localization, which is open ("p-local solitons are p-localized" [O]), "with the spread in the objects' field profile rather than their quantum state, wider than detectors and than the coherence length of galactic magnetic fields, and decoupled from the photon–baryon plasma before recombination (†)". The other is "binding into magnetically neutral composites ("heavy and bound", §11.2)".

The spread has to be in the field profile because a particle in a spread-out quantum state is still detected whole, at a rate set by how many there are [T] ([hiding, on the conjecture page](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification#shared-photon)).

## What it needs

None of the following has been computed.

- **The kinematics of momentum-polarized matter.** The root `CLAUDE.md` lists, for the monopole dark-matter candidate and for this explanation, "p-localization, and the kinematics of momentum-polarized matter, never modeled (#14 §1 assumed ordinary kinematics)" ('Since the handoff', "Open from the monopole discussion"). #14's first section says so itself: "Throughout, the hidden sector is described by ordinary relativistic quantum field theory. Whether the parent framework requires a different kinematics for momentum-polarized matter is an open question that this proposal does not use" (`notes/knot-dark-matter-standalone.md` §1). [Conjugate special relativity](/wiki/conjugate-relativity), #13, "treats the momentum polarization's kinematics but not localization" (flag 60).
- **p-localization.** #11 §14 item 8: "the third candidate of §12 and §11.4's shared-photon form need p-localization: "p-local solitons are p-localized" [O]". Whether topology can localize individual objects in momentum is the [topological loophole](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/topological-loophole).
- **The hiding width.** For the shared-photon form, "p-localization in the field profile and the momentum width that would hide the analogs from detectors, from the Parker bound and from the plasma before recombination, or their binding into magnetically neutral composites. The Parker comparison at general mass is not gated" (root `CLAUDE.md`, "Open from the monopole discussion").
- **A restated P4**, which no document has written.
- **What stays open in either form** of the magnetic-mirror conjecture, among them a mechanism by which one order parameter selects both the position polarization and the electric sublattice ([what the conjecture needs](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification#what-it-needs)).

The [open-problems register](/wiki/status/open-problems) lists them among the open items of the monopole discussion.

## What it bears on

- **Row 8.** [Row 8](/wiki/predictions/monopole-null) of #11 §13 keeps a permanent null for "Magnetic monopoles (ours)", with "Berry-curvature monopoles in solids as the only shadow", and is killed by "Direct detection". In the explanation's setting the objects would be our photon's magnetic charges, "unseen because they are not localized in position" (flag 62), so under the explanation the row's permanent null is a null of searches made in position space.
- **The other half of the supposition.** If conjugate-sector monopoles exist, they gravitate, so "true but irrelevant" is empty; P.R. conjectures that they are abundant in momentum space ([conjugate monopoles as ontology](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/conjugate-monopoles)). The checkpoint words the correction two ways, "the empty set" and "measure zero", and does not reconcile them ([two wordings](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/conjugate-monopoles#two-wordings-one-status-unresolved)).
- **The dark-matter candidate.** The supposition fed a third dark-matter candidate, topological solitons of the p-polarized sector localized in momentum, P.R.'s open conjecture ([Conjecture: magnetic monopoles abundant in momentum space](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/momentum-space-monopoles); #11 §12). #14's model of it, superheavy monopoles of a hidden gauge sector with ordinary kinematics, failed on its recomputed abundance in round 3 of [blind review](/wiki/method/review) ([Obituary: #14's superheavy-soliton model](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/obituary)); that model did not treat localization in momentum, so the candidate itself has not been tested.
- **The laboratory shadow.** "Weyl semimetals host catalogued families of momentum-space Berry-curvature monopoles [16†] — right geometry, different flux" (#11 §11.1; [the laboratory shadow](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/conjugate-monopoles#the-laboratory-shadow-t)).
- **The charge lattice.** The same section of #11 adds the observation that choosing which charges count as electric is itself a polarization [D — structural; math T] ([the symplectic charge lattice](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/symplectic-charge-lattice)).
- **A different object.** Selecting the polarization can leave point defects "of the monopole type" where the order parameter ranges over the whole manifold (#11 §14 item 12, [O]; [Open: point defects of the polarization (OP12)](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/topological-defects#open-point-defects-of-the-polarization-op12)). These are defects of the program's own order parameter, and the record does not identify them with conjugate monopoles.
