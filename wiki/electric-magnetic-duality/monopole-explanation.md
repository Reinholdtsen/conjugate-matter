*This was the claim that [magnetic monopoles](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles) exist but belong to the conjugate, momentum-polarized half of physics, which would explain why no experiment has found one. It was the program's [founding supposition 5](/wiki/program/founding-suppositions). [The checkpoint](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint)'s audit of its monopole claims split the supposition in two and killed this explanatory half on two counts: the symmetry that exchanges electric and magnetic charge is [electric–magnetic duality](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/s-duality), not the position–momentum [reciprocity](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/born-reciprocity) the program is built on, a ground that only one form of a newer conjecture contests; and the absence of monopoles is expected under several standard histories of the early universe, [inflation](/wiki/cosmology/early-universe/inflation-and-dilution) among them, so it lends the program no support. The claim is dead, and under the program's postulates as written it stays dead. The contesting form, one of two forms of P.R.'s open [magnetic-mirror conjecture](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification), would put magnetic charges of our own photon in the momentum-polarized sector, which the postulate separating the two sectors' forces excludes; whether that form, with the postulate restated, would reopen the explanation awaits Paul's ruling. The other half, that such monopoles may exist and would gravitate, survives on [a separate page](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/conjugate-monopoles), and so does [the prediction](/wiki/predictions/monopole-null) that no monopole of our sector will be found.*

**Status:** Dead — [D] for the audit, with the standard histories it cites [T] (#11 §11.1, its grounds restated in v2.3 on Paul's ruling on flag 59); [the handoff](/wiki/history/documents/handoff) lists it among the dead claims on the earlier grounds ("wrong duality; inflation suffices", `HANDOFF.md` §2). Under P4 as written it stays dead; whether §11.4's shared-photon form, with P4 restated, would reopen it is flag 62, pending Paul's ruling. The verdict is an argument, and no script checks it. Home section: [electric–magnetic duality](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality); listed in [the graveyard](/wiki/status/graveyard).

## What was claimed

A magnetic monopole is a particle carrying magnetic charge alone: a north pole without a south pole. Many theories that unify the forces predict them, and none has been detected ([magnetic monopoles](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles); [monopole flux bounds](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-searches)) [T].

P.R.'s founding supposition 5 offered an explanation drawn from the program's picture of two polarizations of matter: "Monopoles live in conjugate space, explaining non-detection" ([#11](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) App. A item 5; [founding suppositions and their fates](/wiki/program/founding-suppositions)). In the program's postulates, matter comes in an x-polarized sector, ours, and a p-polarized sector, χ, and "gauge forces do not cross polarizations" (#11 §4, P4; [P4: polarized matter and strict sector separation](/wiki/program/postulates/p4-polarized-matter)). On the supposition, the monopoles belong to the second sector, so detectors built of our matter do not register them.

The supposition left one trace in the technical record before the audit, in [row 8](/wiki/predictions/monopole-null) of the [lab notebook](/wiki/history/documents/lab-notebook)'s [prediction table](/wiki/predictions/table-history) (`notes/broken-born-theory.md` §9):

| # | Observable | Standard expectation | BBT requirement | Kill condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | Magnetic monopoles | Model-dependent | Permanent null (p-polarized solitons); Berry-curvature monopoles in solids as the only shadow | Direct monopole detection |

BBT, Broken-Born theory, is the program's earlier name. The parenthesis "(p-polarized solitons)" is the explanation. The first paper-shaped consolidation, v1 (#9; [the checkpoint and its predecessor](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint)), already carried the row without it, "Permanent null; Berry-curvature monopoles in solids as the only shadow", and had no section on monopoles (`notes/broken-born-program-v1.md` §9). The repository's one-line description still reads "Physics hypothesis explaining dark matter and missing magnetic monopoles" (`README.md`), which is stale (flag 37 in `review/wiki-build-flags.md`; [CLAUDE.md and the README](/wiki/history/documents/project-instructions)).

## How it was tested

By argument, not by calculation. The checkpoint's v2.0, which added the charge-lattice section 11 (`HANDOFF.md` §3), carries the audit as §11.1, "The monopole audit, revised [D]", and repeats its verdict in the audit of founding suppositions (App. A item 5). No earlier version of the audit is in the repository. The verdict is reasoning from established physics; no script and no review round checked it. In v2.3 (2026-10-29) the checkpoint restated both grounds on Paul's ruling on flag 59 in `review/wiki-build-flags.md`; no script or review round checks the restatement either.

## Why it failed

#11 §11.1, as v2.3 states it:

> As an explanation of the null result: dead. The relevant duality is electric–magnetic (E↔B, g → 1/g), not x↔p; only the shared-photon form of §11.4 contests this ground [O], and that form needs P4 restated. And the null result is expected under several standard histories, so it lends the program no support: inflation after a unification transition dilutes monopoles [T], and without grand unification there are none to dilute, since the Standard Model has no topologically stable monopoles [T]; flag 59 lists further histories (†). Inflation is the standard account, not an observed epoch (v2.3; flag 59). Whether the shared-photon form would reopen the explanation is P.R.'s ruling (flag 62).

The passage gives two grounds. Until v2.3 the second read "inflationary dilution already accounts for absence [T]" (v2.0 to v2.2), which the appendix and the handoff abbreviated as "inflation suffices". Flag 59, raised by Paul, found that this wording showed the explanation to be unnecessary, not false, and that it held only if a unification transition produced monopoles, inflation followed it and reheating stayed below it. Paul accepted the flag on 2026-10-29.

Flag 62, raised by the update's source reviewer on 2026-10-29 and pending, observes that "In v2.3 neither ground of §11.1 kills App. A item 5's explanation unconditionally. The second shows it unnecessary, not false (flag 59), and the first is contested by §11.4's shared-photon form." In the flag's words, what keeps the explanation dead "is P4 as written, which excludes the form, together with the form's uncomputed conditions (§11.4)", and the root `CLAUDE.md` keeps claims listed dead in `HANDOFF.md` §2 dead, as this one is. The flag sets out two options for the ruling: that the explanation stays dead, and the shared-photon form, if pursued, "carries its own account of the null result as a consequence to be tested"; or that the explanation "is reclassified as open, conditional on the shared-photon form and a restated P4".

### The wrong duality

Two different symmetries are in play.

- **Electric–magnetic duality** acts on fields and charges: it exchanges the electric and magnetic fields, E ↔ B, and with them electric and magnetic charges. Because Dirac's condition fixes the product of the two kinds of charge, it also exchanges weak and strong coupling, the "g → 1/g" of #11's sentence ([S-duality and Montonen–Olive](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/s-duality); [Dirac quantization and the DSZ pairing](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/dirac-quantization)) [T].
- **Born reciprocity**, the symmetry the program is built on, acts on phase space: ρ: (x, p) → (p, −x), implemented on quantum states by the [Fourier transform](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/fourier-transform) (#11 §3; [Born reciprocity](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/born-reciprocity)).

The supposition used the second symmetry to explain a fact about the first. Whether a particle carries magnetic charge is a question about where it sits among electric and magnetic charges; which polarization its matter belongs to is a question about position and momentum. The duality that relates magnetic charges to electric ones, and that bears on why one kind is seen and the other is not, is electric–magnetic duality. The same section of the checkpoint finds that this duality has its own account of why magnetic particles are not seen: Dirac's condition makes them strongly coupled and heavy ([the magnetic mirror-family](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-mirror-family)). The observation of §11.3 that the two dualities share a matrix is a structural comparison, not a mechanism that moves monopoles between sectors ([the symplectic charge lattice](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/symplectic-charge-lattice)).

The ground is contested. §11.3 also conjectures [O] that the phase-space polarization and the charge-lattice polarization are components of one enlarged structure ([open problem 8](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/unified-modular-structure)), and flag 59 noted that #11 stated both without noting the conflict. Since v2.3, §11.1 names the one form that contests the ground: "only the shared-photon form of §11.4 contests this ground [O], and that form needs P4 restated". P.R.'s magnetic-mirror conjecture is what §11.3's conjecture would give if one vacuum event selected both the position polarization and the electric sublattice (#11 §11.4), and in its shared-photon form the momentum-polarized sector's analogs carry magnetic charge of our photon ([Conjecture: the momentum sector as the magnetic mirror](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification#shared-photon)). P4 as written excludes that form: "gauge forces do not cross polarizations" (#11 §4).

### Expected under standard histories [T]

#11 §11.1 names two histories, each tagged [T], under which no monopole of our sector would be seen today, and points to flag 59 for more.

- **Inflation after a unification transition.** A symmetry-breaking transition in the early universe that can produce monopoles produces at least about one per causally connected region ([the Kibble mechanism](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/kibble-mechanism)), and for superheavy monopoles this is far too many: "density of such monopoles in the early universe is estimated to be unacceptably large" (Preskill 1979, as [the round-3 audit](/wiki/method/review/round-3/rulings) quotes the abstract). Annihilation does not remove enough of them; the v3 worklog records Preskill's result that "annihilation cannot reduce n/T³ below ~10⁻¹⁰ at m ≈ 10¹⁶ GeV" ('Findings neither referee raised', F1). A period of inflation after the monopoles form dilutes them to an unobservable density, provided reheating stays below the transition ([the monopole problem](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-problem); [inflation, reheating and entropy dilution](/wiki/cosmology/early-universe/inflation-and-dilution)). Inflation is the standard account, not an observed epoch.
- **No grand unification.** The monopoles in question are those of grand unified theories. The Standard Model has no topologically stable monopoles, since the vacuum manifold of its electroweak breaking is the 3-sphere and π₂(S³) = 0 ([why such a monopole is stable](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/thooft-polyakov-monopole#why-it-is-stable-winding-t)), so without unification there are none to dilute.

Flag 59 lists further standard histories that the record does not mention, all cited from memory (†): a maximum temperature below the unification scale; symmetry non-restoration at high temperature (Dvali, Melfo and Senjanović 1995); a temporary phase in which electromagnetism is broken (Langacker and Pi 1980); and domain walls sweeping monopoles away (Dvali, Liu and Vachaspati 1998). The record applies a late release of entropy only to hidden-sector monopoles (#11 §12 [29S]; round-3 ruling 3).

The strong magnetic coupling of #11 §11.2 is not such a history. Preskill's analysis already concerns strongly coupled Dirac monopoles, whose annihilation is inefficient (flag 59; [annihilation is not enough](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-problem#annihilation-is-not-enough-t)). §11.2's "heavy and bound" remains #11's account of why the magnetic mirror-family is not seen ([why the mirror is not seen](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-mirror-family#why-the-mirror-is-not-seen)), not a cosmological history.

With these accounts available, the null result gives no support to a conjugate sector: it is expected without one.

## The split

The audit did not kill the whole supposition. #11 App. A item 5:

> Monopoles live in conjugate space, explaining non-detection — **split**: as explanation, dead (wrong duality, which only §11.4's shared-photon form contests, and that form needs P4 restated; the null result is expected under several standard histories, so it lends no support — v2.3, flag 59); as ontology, alive and consequence-bearing (P.R.'s "true-but-irrelevant is measure zero"); the knot candidate it fed is retired (§12). Its ontology half, joined with item 11, is P.R.'s magnetic-mirror conjecture (§11.4) [O]; whether the conjecture's shared-photon form would reopen the explanation is P.R.'s ruling (flag 62).

v2.2's parenthesis read "(wrong duality; inflation suffices)", and the item ended with the knot candidate.

The other half, conjugate monopoles as objects that may exist, survives because the program's postulate P4 couples both matter sectors to the same geometry, so that such objects would gravitate ([conjugate monopoles as ontology](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/conjugate-monopoles)). P.R. supplied the correction that a conjugate object cannot be "true but irrelevant".

The item's last sentence is new in v2.3. The supposition's ontology half, joined with item 11, a magnetic mirror-family, is P.R.'s magnetic-mirror conjecture: "momentum matter is similar to position matter, each electrically charged x-polarized particle having a magnetically charged p-polarized analog" (#11 §11.4 [O]; [Conjecture: the momentum sector as the magnetic mirror](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification)). In its [shared-photon form](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification#shared-photon) the analogs would carry magnetic charge of our photon. P4 as written excludes that form, so it needs P4 restated, and something would have to hide the analogs from detectors and from [the Parker bound](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-searches#the-parker-bound-t): p-localization, localization in momentum, or binding into magnetically neutral composites, neither of which has been computed. #11 §11.4 ends its account of the form: "Under P4 as written, App. A item 5's explanation of the null result stays dead; whether this form, with P4 restated, would reopen it is P.R.'s ruling (flag 62)." The separate-photon form, consistent with P4, "leaves the absence of our own monopoles to §11.1".

### Two wordings, one verdict (unresolved)

#11 states the correction twice, in different words. §11.1: "true but irrelevant" is "the empty set (a correction supplied by P.R.)". App. A item 5: "P.R.'s "true-but-irrelevant is measure zero"". The empty set says there is no such case; measure zero says such cases are negligible. The checkpoint does not reconcile the two, and the verdict on the explanation is the same under either.

## What survives

- **The ontology** [D]: if conjugate-sector monopoles exist, they gravitate ([conjugate monopoles as ontology](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/conjugate-monopoles)).
- **The prediction for our sector.** [Row 8](/wiki/predictions/monopole-null) of #11 §13 keeps a permanent null for "Magnetic monopoles (ours)", with "Berry-curvature monopoles in solids as the only shadow", and is killed by "Direct detection". The parenthesis "(p-polarized solitons)" is gone. #11 gives the row no separate rationale; its account of the absence of monopoles, in §11.1, is now the several standard histories above [T], and until v2.3 it was inflationary dilution.
- **The laboratory shadow**: "Weyl semimetals host catalogued families of momentum-space Berry-curvature monopoles [16†] — right geometry, different flux" (#11 §11.1).
- **The charge lattice.** The same section of #11 adds the observation that choosing which charges count as electric is itself a polarization [D — structural; math T] ([the symplectic charge lattice](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/symplectic-charge-lattice)).

The candidate the supposition fed is dead as well. Superheavy monopoles of a hidden gauge sector were proposed as the [dark matter](/wiki/dark-matter), worked up as the standalone manuscript [#14](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate), and retired on 2026-10-19 after [round 3](/wiki/method/review/round-3) of [blind review](/wiki/method/review) found their abundance misnormalized ([Obituary: superheavy conjugate solitons](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/obituary)).
