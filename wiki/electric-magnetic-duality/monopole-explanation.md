*This was the claim that magnetic monopoles exist but belong to the conjugate, momentum-polarized half of physics, which would explain why no experiment has found one. It was the program's founding supposition 5. The checkpoint's audit of its monopole claims split the supposition in two and killed this explanatory half on two counts: the symmetry that exchanges electric and magnetic charge is electric–magnetic duality, not the position–momentum reciprocity the program is built on, and inflation already accounts for the absence of monopoles without new physics. The claim is dead and stays dead; the other half, that such monopoles may exist and would gravitate, survives on a separate page.*

**Status:** Dead — [D] for the audit, with inflationary dilution [T] (#11 §11.1); [the handoff](/wiki/history/documents/handoff) lists it among the dead claims ("wrong duality; inflation suffices", `HANDOFF.md` §2). The verdict is an argument, and no script checks it. Home section: [electric–magnetic duality](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality); listed in [the graveyard](/wiki/status/graveyard).

## Key facts

- **What was claimed** ([#11](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) App. A item 5): "Monopoles live in conjugate space, explaining non-detection" ([founding suppositions](/wiki/program/founding-suppositions)). The repository's one-line description still reads "Physics hypothesis explaining dark matter and missing magnetic monopoles" (`README.md`), which is stale ([conflicts between sources](/wiki/history/documents/source-conflicts)).
- **How it entered the predictions.** The lab notebook's row 8 read "Permanent null (p-polarized solitons); Berry-curvature monopoles in solids as the only shadow" (`notes/broken-born-theory.md` §9). The checkpoint keeps the permanent null without the parenthesis (#11 §13; [row 8](/wiki/predictions/monopole-null)).
- **How it was tested.** By argument, not calculation: the checkpoint's monopole audit (#11 §11.1, "The monopole audit, revised [D]") and its audit of the founding suppositions (#11 App. A).
- **Why it failed** (#11 §11.1 [D]): "As an explanation of the null result: dead — the relevant duality is electric–magnetic (E↔B, g → 1/g), not x↔p, and inflationary dilution already accounts for absence [T]." The duality that exchanges electric and magnetic charge is electric–magnetic duality, whose strong–weak form is [S-duality](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/s-duality); the symmetry the program is built on, [Born reciprocity](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/born-reciprocity), exchanges position and momentum. The inflationary account is [the monopole problem](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-problem) and its standard resolution ([inflation, reheating and entropy dilution](/wiki/cosmology/early-universe/inflation-and-dilution)).
- **The split** (#11 App. A item 5): "**split**: as explanation, dead (wrong duality; inflation suffices); as ontology, alive and consequence-bearing (P.R.'s "true-but-irrelevant is measure zero"); the knot candidate it fed is retired (§12)."
- **Two wordings, one verdict** (unresolved). #11 §11.1 calls "true but irrelevant" "the empty set (a correction supplied by P.R.)"; App. A item 5 calls it "measure zero". The checkpoint does not reconcile the two, and the verdict on the explanation is the same under either.
- **What survives.** [Conjugate monopoles as ontology](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/conjugate-monopoles) [D]; the permanent null for monopoles of our sector, which [row 8](/wiki/predictions/monopole-null) keeps without the notebook's "(p-polarized solitons)"; and the observation that the choice of electric charges is itself a polarization, [the symplectic charge lattice](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/symplectic-charge-lattice). The candidate the supposition fed is dead as well ([Obituary: superheavy conjugate solitons](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/obituary)).
