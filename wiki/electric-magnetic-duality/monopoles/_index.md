*A magnetic monopole is a particle that carries magnetic charge alone, a north pole without a south pole. Dirac showed in 1931 that quantum mechanics allows such a particle only if the product of its magnetic charge with every electric charge is quantized, and gauge theories whose symmetry breaks in a suitable pattern contain monopoles as stable, heavy configurations of their fields. The early universe should have made many of them, yet every search has come up empty, and the standard account of that absence is inflation. This section holds that established physics; the program's own monopole claims are in the parent section, and its retired monopole dark-matter candidate is filed with the dark-matter candidates.*

## Map

[Dirac quantization and the DSZ pairing](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/dirac-quantization) comes first because every later page uses it. Dirac's condition ties a magnetic charge g to the electric charge e (e·g ~ 2π in the checkpoint's normalization), and the Dirac–Schwinger–Zwanziger pairing extends it to particles that carry both kinds of charge; the page also gives the consequence that a weakly coupled theory has strongly coupled monopoles. [The 't Hooft–Polyakov monopole](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/thooft-polyakov-monopole) is the concrete monopole of a gauge theory whose SU(2) symmetry breaks to U(1): stable by topology, with a mass set by the symmetry-breaking scale.

[The Kibble mechanism](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/kibble-mechanism) counts the defects a cooling universe produces at such a transition: at least about one per horizon volume, a floor that slower or first-order transitions only raise. [The monopole problem](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-problem) is the consequence for superheavy monopoles: far too many would be made, annihilation cannot remove enough of them, and inflation is the standard remedy. [The monopole flux bounds](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-searches) close the section with the observational limits: the Parker bounds, from the survival of galactic magnetic fields, and the direct search of the Monopole, Astrophysics and Cosmic Ray Observatory.

All five pages are literature [T]. Where a page states how the program used a result — mostly in [#14](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate), the withdrawn soliton manuscript — it says so and links the program page that carries the use.
