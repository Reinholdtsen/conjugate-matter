*Electric–magnetic duality is the symmetry of Maxwell's equations under exchange of the electric and magnetic fields: exact in empty space, and extended to matter only if magnetic charges exist alongside electric ones. A particle with magnetic charge alone is a magnetic monopole, a north pole without a south pole; none has ever been detected, and the theory of monopoles and of the dualities that relate them to ordinary charges is established physics. The Broken-Born program meets this subject three times: one of its founding ideas tried to explain the absence of monopoles and died, a weaker form of the same idea survives, and the program observes that choosing which particles count as electric is the same mathematical operation as choosing which coordinates count as position. Whether those two choices are one structure is an open conjecture.*

## Map

[Magnetic monopoles](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles) comes first. It holds Dirac quantization and the Dirac–Schwinger–Zwanziger (DSZ) pairing of charges, the 't Hooft–Polyakov monopole of a broken gauge symmetry, the Kibble mechanism by which a cooling universe makes such defects, the monopole problem, and the observational flux bounds. All five pages are literature, and every monopole claim the program has made answers to them.

[S-duality and Montonen–Olive](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/s-duality) follows, because it is the form of electric–magnetic duality the program's charge-lattice argument uses: an exchange of electric and magnetic descriptions that also exchanges weak and strong coupling, tested as an exact symmetry only in special supersymmetric theories. [The dual superconductor](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/dual-superconductor) is the leading picture of why quarks are never seen alone, in which a condensate of magnetic monopoles squeezes the field between quarks into strings. [The magnetic mirror-family](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-mirror-family) collects these results as the fate of the program's [founding supposition 11](/wiki/program/founding-suppositions): a magnetic counterpart for every electric particle is established physics, and Dirac's condition accounts for why the counterparts are not seen. These four entries are literature.

The program's own claims follow in the order of [the checkpoint's](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) §11. [Obituary: conjugate monopoles explain the null result](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopole-explanation) records the death of the explanatory half of founding supposition 5: the relevant duality is electric–magnetic, not position–momentum, and inflation already accounts for the absence of monopoles. [Conjugate monopoles as ontology](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/conjugate-monopoles) is the half that survived [D]: if such objects exist, they gravitate. [The symplectic charge lattice](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/symplectic-charge-lattice) is the program's structural observation [D — structural; math T]: the DSZ pairing makes charge space symplectic, a duality frame is a Lagrangian polarization, and one matrix generates Born's ρ, the modular inversion Z → −Z⁻¹ and S-duality. [Open: one modular structure for both polarizations](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/unified-modular-structure) is the conjecture [O] that the phase-space and charge-space polarizations are components of one structure; it is open problem 8 of the checkpoint.

[Obituary: the magnetic identification (popsci-only)](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification) comes last. It records a claim found only in [the popular article #12](/wiki/history/documents/popular-articles), that the momentum-polarized sector is the magnetic side of electromagnetism, which was never a program claim and conflicts with the technical record.
