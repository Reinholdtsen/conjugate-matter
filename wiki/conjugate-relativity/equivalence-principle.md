## Background: two relativity principles [T]

- **The principle of relativity.** The laws of physics take the same form in every inertial frame, and no experiment done inside a closed laboratory can detect its uniform motion. Galileo stated it for mechanics; Einstein's special relativity of 1905 extended it to all of physics, with Lorentz boosts relating the frames ([special relativity: the template](/wiki/conjugate-relativity/special-relativity)).
- **The equivalence principle.** In its weak form it is the universality of free fall: a test body's trajectory depends only on where it starts and how fast it moves, not on what it is made of. In Einstein's form, within a small enough region a freely falling frame is an inertial frame, so local physics in it is that of special relativity, and a uniformly accelerated frame cannot be told apart from one at rest in a uniform gravitational field. Einstein used it as the step from special to general relativity, in which the freely falling frames are the local inertial frames ([general relativity and the graviton](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/general-relativity)).

The two principles have different subjects: the first relates frames in uniform motion, the second relates free fall to inertial motion and acceleration to gravity.

## The claim

#13 §5, after defining the dual-inertial frames:

> "The handshake worth recording [D — structural]: *the dual principle of relativity — equivalence of all force-free frames — is the equivalence principle*, arrived at from kinematics rather than gravitation. In this framework Einstein's two relativity principles are the two polarizations of one statement."

The argument has three steps, each taken from #13:

1. **The dual-inertial frames.** In the dual kinematics the rate of motion through momentum space, dp/dt, is a force, and the dual state of rest is dp/dt = 0, "force-free motion — our free fall" (§5; [dual inertia: free fall as rest](/wiki/conjugate-relativity/dual-inertia)). This identification is on #13 §7's list of fixed results.
2. **The dual principle of relativity.** Transcribed from special relativity, it says that physics is the same in all dual-inertial frames. #13 words it as the "equivalence of all force-free frames".
3. **The reading.** Since the force-free frames are the freely falling ones, the dual principle states that physics is the same in all freely falling frames, which #13 identifies with the equivalence principle. Read in the position description, the statement that physics is the same in all inertial frames is Einstein's principle of relativity; read in the momentum description, which [Born reciprocity](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/born-reciprocity) exchanges with it, the same statement is the equivalence principle: hence "the two polarizations of one statement".

What kind of result this is:

- #13 tags it [D — structural] and calls it "the handshake worth recording"; no further derivation is given. The step rests on the identification in step 1, which uses the general-relativistic reading of free fall as force-free ([dual inertia: which reading of free fall](/wiki/conjugate-relativity/dual-inertia#which-reading-of-free-fall)).
- #13 does not say which form of the equivalence principle it means. Its wording names the force-free frames; its dual-inertial class (§5) also contains the uniformly forced frames, "our Rindler frames", and #13 does not say whether the dual principle extends to them.
- The claim is not on §7's list of "Kinematic structure fixed [D]", which ends with the "dual-inertial = free-fall identification" it rests on.

## What the two polarizations share

#13 §6 opens with what the breaking leaves common to both polarizations:

> "Shared: the time coordinate (in the 3+1 presentation used here) and the massless graviton, so both polarizations fall identically."

- **The time coordinate.** #13 works in a 3+1 presentation, in which space and momentum trade places and time is left alone; the construction that also exchanges time and energy is open ([Open: the four-dimensional Born map and dual dynamics](/wiki/conjugate-relativity/four-dimensional-born-map)). The shared time is the t in the dual-inertial condition dp/dt = 0.
- **The massless graviton.** The checkpoint has one massless graviton, the combination of its two spin-2 fields that is even under reciprocity, and the massive field X, which is odd: "Massless graviton = reciprocity-even" (#11 §7.5; [spectrum, parameters and reciprocity parity](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/spectrum-and-parity); [Row 6: the exactly massless graviton](/wiki/predictions/massless-graviton)).
- **Falling alike.** In the checkpoint, that both kinds of matter fall alike is a postulate. [P4](/wiki/program/postulates/p4-polarized-matter) [P] says that x-polarized matter (the Standard Model) and p-polarized matter (χ) "both couple universally to H" (#11 §4), and #11 §8 has conjugate matter χ propagate "on g (equivalence principle; Bullet-Cluster-safe)" ([conjugate matter and the velocity gate](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter)). The lab notebook's version of P4 said it outright: "the equivalence principle holds across polarizations" (#1 §1, superseded as a reference). #13 §6's "so both polarizations fall identically" is untagged and states the same consequence.

So the program contains two statements about the equivalence principle: the postulated universal coupling of P4, which makes both polarizations fall alike in our description, and #13's structural reading of the equivalence principle as the momentum polarization's relativity principle.

## What the breaking makes different

### The statement

#13 §6 continues:

> "Broken: the long-range/short-range asymmetry — our null structure is policed by massless gravity, the dual structure's dynamical part by the massive X, so dual-geometric effects are Yukawa-cut in momentum [D — checkpoint §7]."

Its plain-language passage: "our light cone is enforced by a massless field and theirs by a massive one, so ours reaches across the universe and theirs is short-ranged."

### Background: range and mass [T]

A force carried by a massless field, like gravity or electromagnetism, falls off as an inverse square and has no limit to its range. A force carried by a field of mass M is cut off exponentially: its potential has the Yukawa form e^{−r/R}/r, with range R = ℏ/(Mc). In units with ℏ = c = 1 the range is 1/M.

### What the checkpoint derives

#13 §1 had already described the dual cone as "the causal structure of the *slaved* geometry g⁻¹", with the massive field X as "the independently dynamical part of momentum-space geometry", which "modifies the dual cone only at short range in momentum [D — §7 of the checkpoint]" ([two causal cones](/wiki/conjugate-relativity/two-causal-cones); [the momentum graviton X](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton)). Both citations point to #11 §7, the section on X. What #11 §7.5 states there [D] is a range in position for X's couplings to ordinary matter: "SM couplings gravitational-strength and Yukawa-cut at 1/M_X" (SM: Standard Model; [safety by range](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/fifth-force-safety)).

The reading "in momentum" has a counterpart only in the original chat, where the handoff records "dual Newtonian limit = Yukawa in momentum = the gate" (`HANDOFF.md` §4; [the gate mechanism](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/gate-mechanism); [two gravitational charges and the conjugate field equations (chat-only)](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/chat-only-results)). ## A different question: the dual equivalence principle

The handoff's list of results that exist only in the chat contains a question with a similar name. Its entry on two gravitational charges ends: "whether invariant length λ and dual charge lock together = 'dual equivalence principle' open question" (`HANDOFF.md` §4). The ordinary equivalence principle locks a body's gravitational charge to its inertial mass; the chat's question is whether the charge that sources X is locked in the same way to the invariant length λ_χ of a conjugate quantum ([the invariant-length shell](/wiki/conjugate-relativity/invariant-length-shell)). It is chat-only, open, and separate from #13's claim on this page.
