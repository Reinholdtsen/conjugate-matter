*Momentum-space black holes are a conjecture from the program's original chat: read in the momentum description, a black hole's horizon is the surface at which hovering in place would require the maximum force, and there may be mirror objects, regions trapped in momentum rather than in space. The handoff document records the idea among the results that exist only in the chat, at conjecture tier, together with "dual Hawking quanta" that would be huge in position space; the popular article #12 repeats part of it and adds that such a mirror object would look like compact dark matter. No document derives any of it, it appears in neither #11 nor #13, and it is not one of the program's dark-matter candidates. Its status is conjecture (chat-only).*

**Status:** Conjecture (chat-only), partly repeated in the popular article #12 — "Conjecture tier" in `HANDOFF.md` §4, which is not a house tag — no derivation and no `verify/` script. Listed in [Conjectures: chat-only and popsci-only claims](/wiki/status/conjectures).

## Key facts

- **The chat record.** `HANDOFF.md` §4, the handoff's list of results that exist only in the chat, has "Momentum-space black holes: velocity traps", with our horizon read in the momentum description as a "max-force surface (hovering at horizon requires F → c⁴/G)", and "dual Hawking quanta are position-space-huge. Conjecture tier; partially in #12."
- **The force in it.** c⁴/G is #13's maximum force c̃ at the Planck value of the Born length ([The maximum force c̃ = c⁴/G](/wiki/conjugate-relativity/maximum-force)); the conjecture in the general-relativity literature is c⁴/4G, and the factor of 4 is open ([Maximum force and maximal acceleration in the literature](/wiki/conjugate-relativity/maximum-force-conjectures)).
- **The popular version.** #12 ("A second speed of light"): "In the momentum description, the same horizon is the surface where remaining stationary would require force beyond the limit. The framework also permits, though it has not yet constructed, the mirror-image object: a trapped region in momentum rather than position, from which no attainable force can extract a captive. Such an object would be opaque in the momentum description and transparent in ours — undetectable by light, evident only through its gravity, and therefore indistinguishable, to our instruments, from compact dark matter." #12 is never a status source ([The popular articles](/wiki/history/documents/popular-articles)).
- **Background [T].** In general relativity a horizon bounds a region from which nothing, light included, can escape, and Hawking radiation is the thermal emission that quantum theory assigns to a horizon [T].
- **Not a candidate.** #11 §12 lists two dark-matter candidates, χ particles and the X-condensate; momentum-space black holes are not among them ([Dark-sector candidates](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates)).
