*Row 6 is the program's prediction about the graviton, the massless quantum of gravity. The program's geometry carries two spin-2 fields, the ordinary metric and a momentum-side partner, and under Born reciprocity one combination of them is even and the other odd. The even combination is the ordinary graviton and is exactly massless; the odd one is the [momentum graviton X](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton), which is massive and so acts only over a short range. Observations of gravitational waves bound the graviton's mass, and in the program those bounds apply to the even combination, which meets them identically; a nonzero mass attributed to it would kill the claim.*

**Status:** Alive — [D] (#11 §7.5); [D + T] in calc note #8 (`notes/debt1a-iv-beta-coefficients.md` §3) — it follows from reciprocity acting on the two spin-2 frames as an exchange (#11 §7.3); the parity assignment is not among the machine checks.

## Key facts

- **The row** ([checkpoint #11](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) §13). Observable: "Gravitational waves". Standard expectation: "m_g = 0". Requirement: "Massless eigenstate exactly massless (reciprocity-even)". Killed by: "Nonzero mass attributed to the even eigenstate".
- **The spectrum** (#11 §7.5, [D]): "Five βₙ → two parameters (M_X ∝ 2(β₁+β₂); one cubic shape)." The massless graviton is "reciprocity-even", with gravitational-wave graviton-mass bounds "satisfied identically", and "X = reciprocity-odd". Ordinary matter couples to X at gravitational strength, cut off by a Yukawa factor at range 1/M_X. See [spectrum, parameters and reciprocity parity](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/spectrum-and-parity) and [safety by range](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/fifth-force-safety).
- **The parity** ([the Debt-1 calc notes](/wiki/history/documents/debt-1-notes), #8 §3, [D + T]): "the massless graviton is the reciprocity-**even** combination (e + f); X is reciprocity-**odd** (e − f). Reciprocity is a selection rule on vertices." The two frames e and f have equal effective Planck masses, "forced by the same symmetry", and the bounds from gravitational waves "apply to the even eigenstate, which is exactly massless".
- **Where the symmetry acts.** "Reciprocity acts on the wedge sector as frame exchange, forcing **βₙ = β₄₋ₙ**" (#11 §7.3; [the palindrome](/wiki/gravity/palindromic-branch/palindrome)). The setting is [Hassan–Rosen bimetric gravity](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/hassan-rosen-bimetric) with the second frame confined to a compact space ([constrained bimetric gravity](/wiki/gravity/tuning-test/constrained-bimetric)); X's mass comes from the polarization choice ([why X must be massive](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/forcing-chain)).
- **The bound.** Calc note #7 §4 cites graviton-mass bounds in "m_g ≲ 10⁻²³ eV territory" from gravitational-wave observations and treats them as a check on a mixing angle; #8 §3 and #11 §7.5 replace that reading with the exact masslessness of the even eigenstate. In [general relativity](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/general-relativity) the graviton is massless; a graviton mass would make gravitational waves of different frequencies travel at different speeds, which observed signals constrain [T].
- **v1's version.** #9 §9's row 6 also required "massive X short-range" and was killed by "Any nonzero graviton mass attributed to the even eigenstate". #9 §11 counted "even-eigenstate masslessness as a bimetric-parity statement" among its "novel predictions not previously posed"; #11 §15 names only rows 3 and 11 as such ([history of the table](/wiki/predictions/table-history)).
- **Open nearby.** The v3 worklog notes that reciprocity's forcing of equal effective Planck masses (M_f = M_g) "rules out the M_f ≪ M_g escape" from the known instabilities of bimetric cosmology, and that "Whether a large mass alone is enough at M_f = M_g is open" (`review/v3-worklog.md`, item R7; [Open: stability at equal Planck masses](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton/equal-planck-mass-stability)).
