*Row 11 asks what dark matter is made of, if the program is right. The program offers two answers: [χ particles](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/chi-particles), the momentum-polarized counterpart of ordinary matter, which scatter off each other only through a [velocity gate](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/velocity-gate); or [an X-condensate](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/x-condensate), a coherent, space-filling oscillation of the field [X](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton), the massive partner of the graviton. #11 states that the two can be told apart using rows 1–3 of its [predictions table](/wiki/predictions) (the knee in the self-interaction, the isotropy of scattering and the shape of the knee's tail) together with signatures of a coherent field. How to make that separation is itself open problem 9 of #11 §14, so the row is a diagnostic still to be worked out, with no kill condition. #11 counts it among the program's predictions not previously posed. Until 2026-10-19 the question had three answers; the third, [superheavy solitons](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/obituary), is retired.*

**Status:** Open — [O] (#11 §14 item 9); the two-candidate framework is [D] and each candidate [O] (#11 §12) — a diagnostic row with no kill condition and nothing yet to machine-check. Listed in [open problems and the queue](/wiki/status/open-problems#the-checkpoints-open-items-11-14).

## The row

[Checkpoint #11](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) §13, row 11:

| Observable | Standard expectation | Broken-Born requirement | Killed by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dark-sector microstructure | (not previously posed) | Particles vs condensate, separable via rows 1–3 + coherence signatures | Diagnostic |

"Microstructure" is what dark matter consists of at the smallest scale on which it has structure: individual particles, or a field oscillating coherently. The open problem behind the row, #11 §14 item 9: "Discriminating the two remaining dark-sector candidates (§12) [O]."

## The two candidates

#11 §12 lists the candidates with their discriminators, tagged "[D — framework; each candidate O]": the framework that produces them is derived, and each candidate is open ([dark-sector candidates](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates)).

| Candidate | Origin | Stability | Distinctive handle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| χ particles (§8) | p-polarized matter field | assumed | knee at v\*, sharp tail; isotropic scattering; permanent direct-detection null |
| X-condensate | oscillating massive spin-2 (bigravity-DM lineage [23†]) | m_X-protected | unifies dark sector with geometry; coherent-field signatures; knee shape wave-like |

DM is dark matter. "The 5.4 : 1 abundance question (polarization-genesis) applies to both" (#11 §12; [Open: polarization-genesis and the 5.4 : 1 ratio (OP7)](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/polarization-genesis)).

- **χ particles** are the p-polarized matter of postulate [P4](/wiki/program/postulates/p4-polarized-matter). They gravitate like ordinary matter and scatter off each other through a gate in relative momentum, mediated by [exchange of X](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/gate-mechanism), which makes their self-interaction strong at low collision speeds and absent at high ones ([conjugate matter and the velocity gate](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter)). Their stability is assumed.
- **The X-condensate** is a state of the [momentum graviton X](/wiki/gravity/momentum-graviton), the massive, reciprocity-odd spin-2 field of the program's geometry, coupled to ordinary matter at "gravitational-strength" and "Yukawa-cut at 1/M_X" (#11 §7.5; [Yukawa forces](/wiki/gravity/spin-2-fields/yukawa-forces#the-momentum-graviton-x)). A field that oscillates uniformly through space behaves on average as pressureless matter [T], and the idea's lineage is [bigravity dark matter](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/bigravity-dark-matter), Babichev et al. [23†], an entry of #11's reference list that pairs one paper's title with another's arXiv number (flag 20 in `review/wiki-build-flags.md`). Its stability is "m_X-protected", stated without an argument ([the three handles](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/x-condensate#the-three-handles)).

## The standard expectation

"(not previously posed)" (#11 §13). #11 §15 counts the row, with [row 3](/wiki/predictions/island-discriminant), among its progressive signs: "predictions not previously posed (rows 3, 11)". The general distinction between particle dark matter and dark matter that behaves as a classical wave, because its [occupation numbers](/wiki/dark-matter/wave-dark-matter/occupation-number) are enormous, is established [T] ([wave dark matter](/wiki/dark-matter/wave-dark-matter)); what is the program's own is the pair of candidates and the claim that rows 1–3 separate them ([Assessment: a live research program](/wiki/program/assessment#predictions-not-previously-posed-rows-3-and-11)).

## The proposed discriminators

### Rows 1–3

- **[Row 1: the knee](/wiki/predictions/self-interaction-knee).** "Knee at v\* ~ 10² km/s; zero cluster signal (particle χ)" (#11 §13). The knee is the drop in the self-interaction cross-section at the gate's velocity scale v\*. Its zero cluster signal is stated for particle χ.
- **[Row 2: isotropy](/wiki/predictions/scattering-isotropy).** "Isotropic below the gate". The argument is a two-body scattering calculation on [the particle island](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/classicality-theorem#the-particle-island), and the χ row of the candidate table lists "isotropic scattering". No document states isotropy, or its absence, for a condensate.
- **[Row 3: the island discriminant](/wiki/predictions/island-discriminant).** "Knee tail: sharp ⇒ particle; (v\*/σ)⁴ ⇒ wave", with σ the halo's velocity dispersion. The particle law is the gate's own cutoff; the wave law was derived in [Debt 3](/wiki/history/debts) for very light χ, which behaves as a classical wave (#11 §9; [the two laws](/wiki/predictions/island-discriminant#the-two-laws)). The candidate table assigns a "sharp tail" to χ particles and a "knee shape wave-like" to the X-condensate.

Read against the table, the three rows separate the candidates through the knee: a sharp tail with isotropic scattering is the χ-particle signature, a wave-like tail the X-condensate's. Several links in that reading are missing from the record:

- **The condensate's knee is not derived.** The (v\*/σ)⁴ law belongs to wave χ, a regime of the matter field χ. No document derives a knee for a condensate of X, or relates the condensate to wave χ ([caveats and open questions](/wiki/predictions/island-discriminant#caveats-and-open-questions)). A wave-like tail would therefore point away from particle χ without, on the record, separating the X-condensate from wave χ.
- **The particle tail may not be sharp.** Whether the gate's tail is Gaussian or rational is open problem 4 of #11 §14. [Calc note #6](/wiki/history/documents/debt-1-notes) calls rational gates "power-law-tailed" (`notes/debt1a-ii-X-classification.md` §4), so if the gate is rational, both branches have power-law tails, and no source compares them ([Open: the gate shape (OP4)](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/gate-shape)).
- **Rows 1 and 2 have no wave form.** [Calc note #2](/wiki/history/documents/debt-3-notes) asked for rows 1–2 to be "re-derived in wave language" (`notes/debt3a-stream-stability.md`, 'Consequences for the predictions table'); the (v\*/σ)⁴ law answers this for the knee's tail only ([Open: rows 1 and 2 in wave language](/wiki/dark-matter/momentum-clumping/wave-island/condensation-rate#open-rows-1-and-2-in-wave-language)).

### Coherence signatures

The second half of the requirement, "coherence signatures", matches the X-condensate's handle "coherent-field signatures". A coherent field oscillates in phase over a region, like a single standing wave, rather than as a gas of independent quanta [T]. No document says what the signatures are, at what scale they would appear, or how they would be observed ([the X-condensate candidate](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/x-condensate#the-three-handles)).

### What does not discriminate

- **Direct detection.** [Row 7](/wiki/predictions/direct-detection-null)'s permanent null holds for both candidates: "both dark-sector candidates SM-decoupled" (#11 §13), SM being the Standard Model.
- **Gravity.** Both candidates gravitate like ordinary dark matter.
- **Abundance.** The 5.4 : 1 question applies to both, and neither has a mechanism that produces the ratio.

## Why it is open

Telling the candidates apart needs calculations the record does not contain. For the X-condensate, no document computes how the oscillation would be produced, its amplitude, its lifetime or its coherence signatures ([what is open](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/x-condensate#what-is-open)). For χ particles, [the gate's shape](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/gate-shape) and [the dual-stress enhancement](/wiki/dark-matter/conjugate-matter/dual-stress-enhancement) that would make their self-interaction large are open problems 4 and 5 of #11 §14. The row records the question and the observables that would answer it; how the answer would be read off is open problem 9 ([open problems and the queue](/wiki/status/open-problems)). The current work queue in the root `CLAUDE.md` does not include it.

## The data situation

The row has no data of its own. Its discriminators are rows 1–3, whose data situations are on their pages: the knee's band and the cluster limits on [row 1](/wiki/predictions/self-interaction-knee#the-data-situation), isotropy on [row 2](/wiki/predictions/scattering-isotropy#the-data-situation), the knee's tail on [row 3](/wiki/predictions/island-discriminant#the-data-situation). No coherence signature has been specified, so none has been looked for.

## Superseded: the three-way question

Until 2026-10-19 the question had three answers. #11 v2.0 listed a third candidate, superheavy topological solitons of a hidden gauge sector, and [the handoff](/wiki/history/documents/handoff) listed among the open items "Dark-sector trilemma discrimination: particles vs X-condensate vs solitons" (`HANDOFF.md` §2, item 8). As imported into the repository (commit 8d588f6), v2.0's row 11 required "Particles / condensate / knots separable via rows 1–3 + compact-object lensing + coherence signatures", and its open problem 9 read "Trilemma discrimination program (§12) [O]". The lensing clause matches the solitons' handle in v2.0's candidate table, "compact-object probes (lensing) if heavy".

[Round 3](/wiki/method/review/round-3) of [blind review](/wiki/method/review) found the solitons' abundance misnormalized, and [Decision 2](/wiki/method/review/round-3/decisions) retired them as a program-native proposal (`review/round3-adjudication.md`, 'Decisions'):

> Consequence: the trilemma becomes two program-native candidates (gated χ particles; the X-condensate), plus the remark that the framework is compatible with known PeV-scale topological dark matter.

#11 v2.1 rewrote the row as "Particles vs condensate, separable via rows 1–3 + coherence signatures" and the open problem as "Discriminating the two remaining dark-sector candidates (§12) [O]" (commit e1c1f67). The candidate's obituary is [Obituary: superheavy conjugate solitons](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/obituary).

Both popular articles predate the retirement and still describe three answers: #12 says the framework "narrows the answer to three candidates" ('Dark matter as the other polarization'), and #10 that "Dark matter's identity, meanwhile, has narrowed to a three-way question with named experimental discriminators" ('Dividends and daydreams'). They are not sources for the question's status ([the popular articles](/wiki/history/documents/popular-articles)).

[O]" (`notes/debt1a-ii-X-classification.md` §4). [The lab notebook](/wiki/history/documents/lab-notebook)'s v0.4 addendum records it as "A new open direction: massive-X condensate as the dark matter itself". v1 listed it as open problem 7, "X-condensate as the dark matter itself (bigravity-DM-style), potentially eliminating χ and unifying the dark sector with the geometry [O — direction, deliberately unopened]" (#9 §10), and named it beside χ in row 7.
- **The row.** v1's table has no row 11. #11 v2.0 added it with three candidates; v2.1 reduced it to two; v2.2 left it unchanged ([the history of the predictions table](/wiki/predictions/table-history#the-checkpoint-v21-after-round-3)).
- **The assessment.** #11 §15's italic summary lists "a two-way question about dark matter's nature with named discriminators" among the program's products and adds that "a third option, superheavy knots, was tested and retired".
