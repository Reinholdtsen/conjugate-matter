*Row 8 predicts that no magnetic monopole of our own electromagnetism, an isolated magnetic north or south pole, will ever be detected. The program's first reason for this prediction, that such monopoles live in the conjugate sector, died when its monopole audit found that the relevant duality is the electric–magnetic one rather than Born reciprocity, and that inflation already accounts for the absence of monopoles. What the row adds is the one laboratory analog it names: monopoles of Berry curvature in momentum space, found in crystals called Weyl semimetals. A direct detection of a monopole of our sector would kill the row.*

**Status:** Alive (#11 §13 row 8) — the row itself carries no tag; the audit behind it is [D], with inflationary dilution [T] and the Weyl-semimetal analog a memory citation [16†] (#11 §11.1) — not machine-checked. The lab notebook's rationale for the null, "p-polarized solitons", is dead.

## Key facts

- **The row** ([checkpoint #11](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) §13). Observable: "Magnetic monopoles (ours)". Standard expectation: "Model-dependent". Requirement: "Permanent null; Berry-curvature monopoles in solids as the only shadow". Killed by: "Direct detection".
- **The audit** (#11 §11.1, [D]): "As an explanation of the null result: dead — the relevant duality is electric–magnetic (E↔B, g → 1/g), not x↔p, and inflationary dilution already accounts for absence [T]. As ontology: alive and consequence-bearing — by P4, conjugate-sector solitons gravitate". See [Obituary: conjugate monopoles explain the null result](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopole-explanation), [conjugate monopoles as ontology](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/conjugate-monopoles), [the monopole problem](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-problem) and [inflation, reheating and entropy dilution](/wiki/cosmology/early-universe/inflation-and-dilution).
- **The shadow** (#11 §11.1): "Weyl semimetals host catalogued families of momentum-space Berry-curvature monopoles [16†] — right geometry, different flux." Reference [16†] is S.-Y. Xu et al., "Discovery of a Weyl fermion semimetal," Science 349, 613 (2015), and B. Q. Lv et al., Phys. Rev. X 5, 031013 (2015), cited from model memory and awaiting [the † literature battery](/wiki/method/literature-battery).
- **Background** [T]. In a Weyl semimetal, the points where two electron bands touch act as sources and sinks of Berry curvature, a field defined over momentum space; they are monopoles in momentum space rather than in position space, and of Berry curvature rather than of the magnetic field.
- **The rationale that died.** v0.1's row read "Permanent null (p-polarized solitons); Berry-curvature monopoles in solids as the only shadow", killed by "Direct monopole detection" (#1 §9). That explanation is dead and the row survives with #11's framing; v1 (#9 §9) carried the row without a section supporting it, and #11 added "(ours)" ([history of the table](/wiki/predictions/table-history)). Founding supposition 5, "Monopoles live in conjugate space, explaining non-detection", is "**split**" in #11 App. A ([founding suppositions and their fates](/wiki/program/founding-suppositions)).
- **Not the soliton candidate.** Superheavy monopoles of a hidden gauge sector as the dark matter were a separate proposal, retired on 2026-10-19 ([Obituary: superheavy conjugate solitons](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/obituary)). The repository README's tagline, "Physics hypothesis explaining dark matter and missing magnetic monopoles", is stale on the monopole point.
- **The experimental side.** Searches for monopoles of our sector are described in [monopole flux bounds](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-searches).
