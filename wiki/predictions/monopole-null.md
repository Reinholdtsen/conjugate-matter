*Row 8 predicts that no [magnetic monopole](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles) of our own electromagnetism, an isolated magnetic north or south pole, will ever be detected. Many theories that unify the forces predict such monopoles, and searches have found none [T]. The program's founding explanation of that absence, [that monopoles live in the conjugate sector](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopole-explanation), is P.R.'s open conjecture, and neither ground given against it refutes it. The duality that relates magnetic to electric charge is the [electric–magnetic](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality) one, not [Born reciprocity](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/born-reciprocity), and only one form of P.R.'s [magnetic-mirror conjecture](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification#shared-photon) contests that; and the absence is expected under several standard histories, [inflation](/wiki/cosmology/early-universe/inflation-and-dilution#inflation-and-our-own-monopoles) after a unification transition among them, so it lends the explanation no support either way. In that form of the conjecture, momentum-sector partners of our charged particles carry magnetic charge of our own photon but are not localized in position, so searches, which are made in position space, would not register them; the form needs a postulate of the program restated and conditions nobody has computed. The row adds one laboratory analog: monopoles of Berry curvature in momentum space, found in crystals called [Weyl semimetals](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/conjugate-monopoles#the-laboratory-shadow-t). A direct detection of a monopole of our sector would kill the row.*

**Status:** Alive (#11 §13 row 8) — the row carries no tag; the audit behind it is [D]; the absence is expected under several standard histories (#11 §11.1: inflation after a unification transition [T] and no grand unification [T]; flag 59 lists further histories †); the conjugate-sector explanation is P.R.'s conjecture [O] (flag 62); the Weyl-semimetal analog is a memory citation [16†] (#11 §11.1) — not machine-checked. [The lab notebook](/wiki/history/documents/lab-notebook)'s rationale for the null, "p-polarized solitons", is that explanation ([Conjecture: conjugate monopoles explain the null result](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopole-explanation)).

## The row

[Checkpoint #11](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) §13, row 8:

| Observable | Standard expectation | Program requirement | Killed by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Magnetic monopoles (ours) | Model-dependent | Permanent null; Berry-curvature monopoles in solids as the only shadow | Direct detection |

"Ours" restricts the row to monopoles that carry magnetic charge under our own electromagnetism, the electromagnetic U(1) of the Standard Model. Monopoles of a hidden or conjugate gauge sector are not covered by it. In the shared-photon form of P.R.'s magnetic-mirror conjecture, momentum-sector objects would carry magnetic charge of our photon, and in the wiki's reading the row would then cover them ([below](#the-explanations-setting-a-null-of-position-space-searches)).

## The observable [T]

A magnetic monopole is a particle that carries magnetic charge alone ([magnetic monopoles](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles)). Dirac showed that a single monopole anywhere would require every electric charge to be a multiple of a basic unit, and that the magnetic charge is then large, inversely proportional to the electric one ([Dirac quantization and the DSZ pairing](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/dirac-quantization), DSZ being Dirac–Schwinger–Zwanziger). Grand unified theories, in which the forces of the Standard Model merge at high energy, contain monopoles as solitons of the gauge and Higgs fields, with masses set by the unification scale ([the 't Hooft–Polyakov monopole](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/thooft-polyakov-monopole)).

Two kinds of observation bear on them ([monopole flux bounds](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-searches)):

- **Direct searches.** A monopole passing through a superconducting loop changes the current in the loop by a fixed step, whatever its speed; a fast monopole passing through matter ionizes it far more strongly than a singly charged particle [T]. Detectors built on these effects count monopoles crossing them.
- **Astrophysical bounds.** Monopoles moving through the Galaxy's magnetic field would gain energy from it and drain it; the field's survival caps their flux (the Parker bound) [T].

## The standard expectation

"Model-dependent" (#11 §13). Whether monopoles of our sector should be found depends on the theory and the cosmology [T]. A symmetry-breaking transition that can produce monopoles produces at least about one per causally connected region ([the Kibble mechanism](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/kibble-mechanism)); for superheavy monopoles that is far too many, and annihilation does not remove enough of them. A period of inflation after they form dilutes them to an unobservable density. This is [the monopole problem](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-problem) and its standard remedy ([inflation, reheating and entropy dilution](/wiki/cosmology/early-universe/inflation-and-dilution#inflation-and-our-own-monopoles)). Whether any monopoles remain to be found then depends on when they formed relative to inflation and on their mass [T]. Inflation is the standard account, not an observed epoch, and it is not the only one: without grand unification there are none to dilute, since the Standard Model has no topologically stable monopoles [T] (#11 §11.1; [the electroweak vacuum manifold](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/topological-defects#vacua-as-a-coset)). Flag 59 in `review/wiki-build-flags.md` lists further standard histories that the record does not mention, all cited from memory (†): a maximum temperature below the unification scale; symmetry non-restoration at high temperature (Dvali, Melfo and Senjanović 1995); a temporary phase with electromagnetism broken (Langacker and Pi 1980); and domain walls sweeping monopoles away (Dvali, Liu and Vachaspati 1998).

#11 §11.2 gives, from the literature, its account of why the magnetic mirror-family, a magnetic counterpart for every electrically charged particle, is not seen [T]: "Non-observation is structural: Dirac's condition e·g ~ 2π pins the magnetic coupling near 4π/e² ≈ 34 — the magnetic world is our strong-coupling mirror, heavy and bound." That account is not a history under which the null result is expected: Preskill's analysis of the monopole problem already concerns strongly coupled Dirac monopoles, whose annihilation is inefficient (flag 59). `review/wiki-build-flags.md` item 28 records that the number is right and the formula beside it is not: "With α = e²/4π, 4π/e² = 1/α ≈ 137; e·g = 2π gives g²/4π = π/e² = 1/(4α) ≈ 34.26." The flag is pending, and this page quotes #11 as written ([the magnetic mirror-family](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-mirror-family#diracs-condition-forces-a-strong-magnetic-coupling-t)).

## The program's requirement

### The audit

The checkpoint's monopole audit, #11 §11.1, "The monopole audit, revised [D]", records the explanation of the null result as open [O], P.R.'s conjecture (flag 62), "since neither ground given against it kills it". The grounds:

> The relevant duality is electric–magnetic (E↔B, g → 1/g), not x↔p; only the shared-photon form of §11.4 contests this ground, and that form needs P4 restated. The null result is expected under several standard histories, so it lends the explanation no support: inflation after a unification transition dilutes monopoles [T], and without grand unification there are none to dilute, since the Standard Model has no topologically stable monopoles [T]; flag 59 lists further histories (†). Inflation is the standard account, not an observed epoch.

The audit goes on to the objects themselves:

> As ontology: alive and consequence-bearing — by P4, conjugate-sector solitons gravitate; "true but irrelevant" is the empty set (a correction supplied by P.R.); P.R. conjectures that they are abundant in momentum space. Their candidacy as the dark matter is open (§12).

§11.1 adds that #14's model of the candidate failed and that the candidate itself was never modeled.

On the wiki's reading of the audit, the row's footing depends on which form of [P4](/wiki/program/postulates/p4-polarized-matter) holds:

- **Under P4 as written**, the row has no rationale of its own. The absence of our monopoles is expected under standard histories, the two #11 names tagged [T] and the others cited from memory (†), none of them dependent on Born reciprocity. The row's content is the commitment itself: a permanent null, with a direct detection as its kill condition.
- **Under the explanation's setting**, the shared-photon form with P4 restated, the null has a rationale of the program's own, P.R.'s conjecture: magnetic charges of our photon in the momentum polarization, not localized in position ([below](#the-explanations-setting-a-null-of-position-space-searches)). Its conditions have not been computed.

### The explanation, P.R.'s conjecture

The null was first the lab notebook's: "Permanent null (p-polarized solitons)" (#1 §9 row 8). It rested on [founding supposition 5](/wiki/program/founding-suppositions#5-monopoles-live-in-conjugate-space-explaining-non-detection), "Monopoles live in conjugate space, explaining non-detection": on the wiki's reading, monopoles would belong to the p-polarized sector, which our detectors cannot register because "gauge forces do not cross polarizations" (#11 §4, [P4](/wiki/program/postulates/p4-polarized-matter)). #11 sets the explanation in §11.4's shared-photon form, which needs P4 restated ([below](#the-explanations-setting-a-null-of-position-space-searches)). The audit gives two grounds against the explanation, and neither refutes it ([why neither ground kills it](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopole-explanation#why-neither-ground-kills-it)):

- **The wrong duality.** Electric–magnetic duality exchanges electric and magnetic fields and charges, and with Dirac's condition weak and strong coupling ([S-duality and Montonen–Olive](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/s-duality)). Born reciprocity ρ: (x, p) → (p, −x) acts on phase space ([Born reciprocity](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/born-reciprocity)). Whether a particle is magnetically charged is a question about the first symmetry, not the second, if the two are independent; §11.3 conjectures [O] that they are one structure (flag 59; [one enlarged modular structure](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/unified-modular-structure)). Only the shared-photon form of P.R.'s magnetic-mirror conjecture contests this ground, and that form needs P4 restated (#11 §11.1; [shared photon](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification#shared-photon)). The magnetic-mirror conjecture is what §11.3's conjecture would give if one vacuum event made both choices (#11 §11.4).
- **No support.** The absence is expected without any conjugate sector, under several standard histories, so it lends the explanation "no support either way" (App. A item 5): it shows the explanation unnecessary, not false, and inflationary dilution accounts for the absence only if a unification transition produced monopoles, inflation followed it and reheating stayed below it (flag 59).

#11 App. A item 5 records the supposition as "**open in both halves**": as explanation, "P.R.'s conjecture [O] under §11.4's shared-photon form, which neither ground kills (wrong duality is contested by that form, which needs P4 restated; the null result is expected under several standard histories, so it lends no support either way — flags 59, 62)"; as ontology, "alive and consequence-bearing (P.R.'s "true-but-irrelevant is measure zero"), with P.R.'s conjecture that such monopoles are abundant in momentum space (§12)". The full account is [Conjecture: conjugate monopoles explain the null result](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopole-explanation).

### The explanation's setting: a null of position-space searches

#11 §11.4 records P.R.'s magnetic-mirror conjecture [O], which joins founding supposition 5 with supposition 11, the full magnetic mirror-family: "P.R.'s conjecture: momentum matter is similar to position matter, each electrically charged x-polarized particle having a magnetically charged p-polarized analog" ([Conjecture: the momentum sector as the magnetic mirror](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification)). Its two forms bear on the row differently:

- **Separate photon.** The analogs are magnetic under the p-polarized sector's own gauge field, and the form "leaves the absence of our own monopoles to §11.1". Under it the row rests on the standard expectations above.
- **Shared photon.** The analogs carry magnetic charge of our photon. "P4 as written excludes this form", so it needs P4 restated. "Localized in position at dark-matter density, such objects would exceed the Parker bound at #14's benchmark masses (#14 v2 and v3 §4; not gated)", so something must hide them, and "neither option has been computed" (§11.4): p-localization, localization in momentum, with the spread in the objects' field profile, wider than detectors and than the coherence length of galactic magnetic fields, and decoupled from the photon–baryon plasma before recombination (†); or binding into magnetically neutral composites.

The shared-photon form is the explanation's setting: it "requires P4 restated and the hiding conditions above, none of them yet computed" (#11 §11.4; flag 62). P.R.'s argument is that searches are made in position space and conjugate monopoles are by definition compact in momentum space, so the searches would not detect them, and this explains their absence (flag 62). Under the explanation, then, magnetic charges of our photon would exist, and the row's permanent null would be a null of searches made in position space. What stays open is p-localization in the field profile and the momentum width that would hide the objects from detectors, from the Parker bound and from the plasma before recombination, or their binding into magnetically neutral composites, or screening by a magnetic condensate (root `CLAUDE.md`, 'Since the handoff'; [what it needs](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopole-explanation#what-it-needs)).

### Conjugate monopoles as ontology and as dark matter

The ontology half of the supposition concerns monopoles of the conjugate sector. Under P4 as written they carry no charge under our photon, so row 8 does not cover them; in the shared-photon form they would (above). If they exist, they gravitate, because P4 couples both sectors of matter to the same geometry ([conjugate monopoles as ontology](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/conjugate-monopoles)). Under P4 as written they would interact with our matter only through gravity, so the flux searches below do not test them ([our sector's monopoles](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-searches#our-sectors-monopoles)).

P.R. conjectures that they are abundant in momentum space, and their candidacy as the dark matter is open: they are the third candidate of #11 §12 ([Conjecture: magnetic monopoles abundant in momentum space](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/momentum-space-monopoles)). #14's model of the candidate, superheavy monopoles of a [hidden SU(2) → U(1) sector](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/hidden-sector) built with ordinary kinematics, failed on its recomputed abundance: [round 3](/wiki/method/review/round-3) of [blind review](/wiki/method/review) found its abundance estimate [misnormalized](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/kibble-normalization) (#11 §12; [Obituary: #14's superheavy-soliton model](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/obituary)). The hidden sector, as the program's proposal, and the Kibble normalization are both under review (root `CLAUDE.md`, 'Since the handoff'). #14's model did not treat localization in momentum, so the candidate itself has not been tested: "Throughout, the hidden sector is described by ordinary relativistic quantum field theory" (#14 §1). Round-3 Decision 2, which covers #14's model, retired it as a program-native proposal (`review/round3-adjudication.md`, 'Decisions'). #14 used the flux bounds only counterfactually, to show that its monopoles could not have carried our magnetic charge: "Not monopoles of our U(1). If these objects carried magnetic charge under our electromagnetism, they would be classic monopoles and excluded." (#14 §4; [how the program uses the bounds](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-searches#how-the-program-uses-the-bounds)).

### The only shadow: Berry-curvature monopoles

#11 §11.1 ends the audit with an analog in condensed matter: "Their laboratory shadow is real: Weyl semimetals host catalogued families of momentum-space Berry-curvature monopoles [16†] — right geometry, different flux."

In a crystal, an electron's quantum state in each energy band depends on its crystal momentum, and the way the state's phase turns as the momentum changes defines a field over momentum space, the Berry curvature [T]. In a Weyl semimetal two bands touch at isolated points of momentum space, and each touching point is a source or a sink of Berry curvature with a quantized total flux: a monopole in momentum space [T]. In the wiki's reading, "right geometry" refers to the location, momentum space, the space toward which the program's conjugate sector is polarized. "Different flux" says what the monopole is a source of: Berry curvature, a property of electron states, not a magnetic field ([the laboratory shadow](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/conjugate-monopoles#the-laboratory-shadow-t)).

Reference [16]† is S.-Y. Xu et al., "Discovery of a Weyl fermion semimetal," Science 349, 613 (2015), and B. Q. Lv et al., Phys. Rev. X 5, 031013 (2015). Both are cited from model memory and await [the † literature battery](/wiki/method/literature-battery).

The row calls these monopoles "the only shadow". The documents do not say what the phrase commits the program to. In the wiki's reading it names the one realized system with monopoles in momentum space, an analog rather than a test: their existence neither supports nor threatens the null for monopoles of our sector.

## What kills it

"Direct detection" (#11 §13): a confirmed monopole of our electromagnetism, registered for instance by the current step it induces in a superconducting loop or by its ionization track [T]. The lab notebook's wording was "Direct monopole detection" (#1 §9).

In the wiki's reading, under P4 as written #11 attributes the absence to standard histories rather than to the program (§11.1), so a detection would refute the program's commitment to a permanent null rather than a mechanism of its own. Under the explanation's setting the commitment rests instead on P.R.'s conjecture that the objects are hidden from searches in position space. The documents do not say what else a detection would bear on.

## The data situation

No monopole of our sector has been detected [T]. The flux limits the record quotes come from the review of #14, where the round-3 referee checked each against its source (`review/round3/referee/audit-notes.md`, Step 1):

```text
[20] PDG 2024 (Milstead & Weinberg), eq. (95.9): Parker F < 1e-15 cm^-2 sr^-1 s^-1 for M <~ 1e17 GeV. SUPPORTS.
[21] MACRO final: Table 2: "<= 2.8e-16 for m >~ 1e10 GeV, beta > 1e-4"; global 1.4e-16. SUPPORTS the 2.8e-16 figure exactly.
```

PDG is the Particle Data Group; MACRO, the Monopole, Astrophysics and Cosmic Ray Observatory, was an underground detector built for monopole searches. F is the flux of monopoles per unit area, time and solid angle, and β the speed in units of c. The v3 worklog adds how to cite the first: "For the 10⁻¹⁵ Parker value, cite PDG 2024 or Turner–Parker–Bogdan, PRD 26, 1296 (1982), not Parker 1970 alone" ('Verified references for v3'). The bounds, their derivation and the extended Parker bound are on [monopole flux bounds](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-searches).

These limits are consistent with the row and cannot confirm it. #11 §15 names rows 1, 4 and 7 as the program's near-term falsification exposure; row 8 is not among them.
