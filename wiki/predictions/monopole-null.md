*Row 8 predicts that no [magnetic monopole](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles) of our own electromagnetism, an isolated magnetic north or south pole, will ever be detected. Many theories that unify the forces predict such monopoles, and searches have found none [T]. The program's first explanation of that absence, [that monopoles live in the conjugate sector](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopole-explanation), died in the checkpoint's monopole audit: the duality that relates magnetic to electric charge is the [electric–magnetic](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality) one, not [Born reciprocity](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/born-reciprocity), and the absence is expected under several standard histories, [inflation](/wiki/cosmology/early-universe/inflation-and-dilution#inflation-and-our-own-monopoles) after a unification transition among them, so it lends the program no support. The row keeps the null with those standard expectations and adds one laboratory analog: monopoles of Berry curvature in momentum space, found in crystals called [Weyl semimetals](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/conjugate-monopoles#the-laboratory-shadow-t). A direct detection of a monopole of our sector would kill the row. One form of P.R.'s open [magnetic-mirror conjecture](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification#shared-photon), in which every electrically charged particle has a partner in the momentum sector that carries magnetic charge of our own photon, contests the first ground of the audit. That form needs a postulate of the program restated, and whether it would reopen the dead explanation awaits Paul's ruling.*

**Status:** Alive (#11 §13 row 8) — the row carries no tag; the audit behind it is [D], the absence is expected under several standard histories (#11 v2.3 §11.1: inflation after a unification transition [T] and no grand unification [T]; flag 59 lists further histories †), and the Weyl-semimetal analog is a memory citation [16†] (#11 §11.1) — not machine-checked. [The lab notebook](/wiki/history/documents/lab-notebook)'s rationale for the null, "p-polarized solitons", is dead ([its obituary](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopole-explanation)).

## The row

[Checkpoint #11](/wiki/history/documents/checkpoint) §13, row 8:

| Observable | Standard expectation | Program requirement | Killed by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Magnetic monopoles (ours) | Model-dependent | Permanent null; Berry-curvature monopoles in solids as the only shadow | Direct detection |

"Ours" restricts the row to monopoles that carry magnetic charge under our own electromagnetism, the electromagnetic U(1) of the Standard Model. Monopoles of a hidden or conjugate gauge sector are not covered by it.

## The observable [T]

A magnetic monopole is a particle that carries magnetic charge alone ([magnetic monopoles](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles)). Dirac showed that a single monopole anywhere would require every electric charge to be a multiple of a basic unit, and that the magnetic charge is then large, inversely proportional to the electric one ([Dirac quantization and the DSZ pairing](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/dirac-quantization), DSZ being Dirac–Schwinger–Zwanziger). Grand unified theories, in which the forces of the Standard Model merge at high energy, contain monopoles as solitons of the gauge and Higgs fields, with masses set by the unification scale ([the 't Hooft–Polyakov monopole](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/thooft-polyakov-monopole)).

Two kinds of observation bear on them ([monopole flux bounds](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-searches)):

- **Direct searches.** A monopole passing through a superconducting loop changes the current in the loop by a fixed step, whatever its speed; a fast monopole passing through matter ionizes it far more strongly than a singly charged particle [T]. Detectors built on these effects count monopoles crossing them.
- **Astrophysical bounds.** Monopoles moving through the Galaxy's magnetic field would gain energy from it and drain it; the field's survival caps their flux (the Parker bound) [T].

## The standard expectation

"Model-dependent" (#11 §13). Whether monopoles of our sector should be found depends on the theory and the cosmology [T]. A symmetry-breaking transition that can produce monopoles produces at least about one per causally connected region ([the Kibble mechanism](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/kibble-mechanism)); for superheavy monopoles that is far too many, and annihilation does not remove enough of them. A period of inflation after they form dilutes them to an unobservable density. This is [the monopole problem](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-problem) and its standard remedy ([inflation, reheating and entropy dilution](/wiki/cosmology/early-universe/inflation-and-dilution#inflation-and-our-own-monopoles)). Whether any monopoles remain to be found then depends on when they formed relative to inflation and on their mass [T]. Inflation is the standard account, not an observed epoch, and it is not the only one: without grand unification there are none to dilute, since the Standard Model has no topologically stable monopoles [T] (#11 §11.1; [the electroweak vacuum manifold](/wiki/symmetry-breaking/topological-defects#vacua-as-a-coset)). Flag 59 in `review/wiki-build-flags.md` lists further standard histories that the record does not mention, all cited from memory (†): a maximum temperature below the unification scale; symmetry non-restoration at high temperature (Dvali, Melfo and Senjanović 1995); a temporary phase with electromagnetism broken (Langacker and Pi 1980); and domain walls sweeping monopoles away (Dvali, Liu and Vachaspati 1998).

#11 §11.2 gives, from the literature, its account of why the magnetic mirror-family, a magnetic counterpart for every electrically charged particle, is not seen [T]: "Non-observation is structural: Dirac's condition e·g ~ 2π pins the magnetic coupling near 4π/e² ≈ 34 — the magnetic world is our strong-coupling mirror, heavy and bound." That account is not a history under which the null result is expected: Preskill's analysis of the monopole problem already concerns strongly coupled Dirac monopoles, whose annihilation is inefficient (flag 59). `review/wiki-build-flags.md` item 28 records that the number is right and the formula beside it is not: "With α = e²/4π, 4π/e² = 1/α ≈ 137; e·g = 2π gives g²/4π = π/e² = 1/(4α) ≈ 34.26." The flag awaits Paul's ruling, and this page quotes #11 as written ([the magnetic mirror-family](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-mirror-family#diracs-condition-forces-a-strong-magnetic-coupling-t)).

## The program's requirement

### A null with the standard explanation

The checkpoint's monopole audit (#11 §11.1, "The monopole audit, revised [D]", as v2.3 words it):

> As an explanation of the null result: dead. The relevant duality is electric–magnetic (E↔B, g → 1/g), not x↔p; only the shared-photon form of §11.4 contests this ground [O], and that form needs P4 restated. And the null result is expected under several standard histories, so it lends the program no support: inflation after a unification transition dilutes monopoles [T], and without grand unification there are none to dilute, since the Standard Model has no topologically stable monopoles [T]; flag 59 lists further histories (†). Inflation is the standard account, not an observed epoch (v2.3; flag 59). Whether the shared-photon form would reopen the explanation is P.R.'s ruling (flag 62). As ontology: alive and consequence-bearing — by P4, conjugate-sector solitons gravitate; "true but irrelevant" is the empty set (a correction supplied by P.R.).

The part on the explanation gives the row its footing. #11 gives the null no rationale of its own: the absence of our monopoles is expected under standard histories, the two #11 names tagged [T] and the others cited from memory (†), none of them dependent on Born reciprocity. The row's content is the commitment itself: a permanent null, with a direct detection as its kill condition.

Until v2.3 the audit's second ground was inflation alone: "inflationary dilution already accounts for absence [T]" (#11 v2.2 §11.1). Flag 59 in `review/wiki-build-flags.md` found that this showed the explanation unnecessary, not false, and that it held only if a unification transition produced monopoles, inflation followed it and reheating stayed below it; Paul accepted the restatement on 2026-10-29.

### The rationale that died

The null was first the lab notebook's: "Permanent null (p-polarized solitons)" (#1 §9 row 8). It rested on [founding supposition 5](/wiki/program/founding-suppositions#5-monopoles-live-in-conjugate-space-explaining-non-detection), "Monopoles live in conjugate space, explaining non-detection": monopoles would belong to the p-polarized sector, which our detectors cannot register because "gauge forces do not cross polarizations" (#11 §4, [P4](/wiki/program/postulates/p4-polarized-matter)). The audit killed that explanation on two independent grounds:

- **The wrong duality.** Electric–magnetic duality exchanges electric and magnetic fields and charges, and with Dirac's condition weak and strong coupling ([S-duality and Montonen–Olive](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/s-duality)). Born reciprocity ρ: (x, p) → (p, −x) acts on phase space ([Born reciprocity](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/born-reciprocity)). Whether a particle is magnetically charged is a question about the first symmetry, not the second. Only the shared-photon form of P.R.'s magnetic-mirror conjecture contests this ground, and that form needs P4 restated (#11 §11.1; [shared photon](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification#shared-photon)). The conjecture is what §11.3's conjecture, that the phase-space polarization and the charge-lattice polarization are components of one enlarged modular structure, would give if one vacuum event made both choices (#11 §11.4; [one enlarged modular structure](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/unified-modular-structure)).
- **No support.** The absence is expected without any conjugate sector, under several standard histories, so it gives no support to one. Until v2.3 this ground was inflation alone ("inflation suffices" in App. A item 5 and the handoff).

#11 App. A item 5 records the supposition as "**split**: as explanation, dead (wrong duality, which only §11.4's shared-photon form contests, and that form needs P4 restated; the null result is expected under several standard histories, so it lends no support — v2.3, flag 59); as ontology, alive and consequence-bearing", and since v2.3 adds: "Its ontology half, joined with item 11, is P.R.'s magnetic-mirror conjecture (§11.4) [O]; whether the conjecture's shared-photon form would reopen the explanation is P.R.'s ruling (flag 62)." In v2.2 the explanation was "dead (wrong duality; inflation suffices)". The full record is [Obituary: conjugate monopoles explain the null result](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopole-explanation).

### P.R.'s magnetic-mirror conjecture

#11 v2.3 §11.4 records P.R.'s magnetic-mirror conjecture [O], which joins founding supposition 5 with supposition 11, the full magnetic mirror-family: "P.R.'s conjecture: momentum matter is similar to position matter, each electrically charged x-polarized particle having a magnetically charged p-polarized analog" ([Conjecture: the momentum sector as the magnetic mirror](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification)). Its two forms bear on the row differently:

- **Separate photon.** The analogs are magnetic under the p-polarized sector's own gauge field, and the form "leaves the absence of our own monopoles to §11.1".
- **Shared photon.** The analogs carry magnetic charge of our photon. "P4 as written excludes this form", so it needs P4 restated. It also needs something to hide the analogs, and "neither option has been computed" (§11.4): p-localization, localization in momentum, with the spread in the objects' field profile, wider than detectors and than the coherence length of galactic magnetic fields, and decoupled from the photon–baryon plasma before recombination (†); or binding into magnetically neutral composites. On the row, #11 §11.4 says: "Under P4 as written, App. A item 5's explanation of the null result stays dead; whether this form, with P4 restated, would reopen it is P.R.'s ruling (flag 62)" ([what it would explain](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/magnetic-identification#what-it-would-explain)). Flag 62 is pending. Its options are that the explanation stays dead, the shared-photon form, if pursued, carrying its own account of the null result as a consequence to be tested; or that the explanation is reclassified as open, conditional on that form and a restated P4.

### Not a statement about conjugate monopoles

The surviving half of the supposition concerns monopoles of the conjugate sector, which under P4 as written carry no charge under our photon, so row 8 does not cover them. If they exist, they gravitate, because P4 couples both sectors of matter to the same geometry ([conjugate monopoles as ontology](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/conjugate-monopoles)). They would interact with our matter only through gravity, so the flux searches below do not test them ([our sector's monopoles](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-searches#our-sectors-monopoles)).

Their candidacy as the dark matter is dead. Superheavy monopoles of a hidden SU(2) → U(1) sector were developed as the standalone proposal #14 and retired on 2026-10-19, after [round 3](/wiki/method/review/round-3) of [blind review](/wiki/method/review) found their abundance estimate misnormalized (#11 §12; [Obituary: superheavy conjugate solitons](/wiki/dark-matter/candidates/soliton-candidate/obituary)). #14 had used the flux bounds only counterfactually, to show that its monopoles could not have carried our magnetic charge: "Not monopoles of our U(1). If these objects carried magnetic charge under our electromagnetism, they would be classic monopoles and excluded." (#14 §4; [a counterfactual for the soliton candidate](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-searches#a-counterfactual-for-the-soliton-candidate)).

### The only shadow: Berry-curvature monopoles

#11 §11.1 ends the audit with an analog in condensed matter: "Their laboratory shadow is real: Weyl semimetals host catalogued families of momentum-space Berry-curvature monopoles [16†] — right geometry, different flux."

In a crystal, an electron's quantum state in each energy band depends on its crystal momentum, and the way the state's phase turns as the momentum changes defines a field over momentum space, the Berry curvature [T]. In a Weyl semimetal two bands touch at isolated points of momentum space, and each touching point is a source or a sink of Berry curvature with a quantized total flux: a monopole in momentum space [T]. In the wiki's reading, "right geometry" refers to the location, momentum space, the space toward which the program's conjugate sector is polarized. "Different flux" says what the monopole is a source of: Berry curvature, a property of electron states, not a magnetic field ([the laboratory shadow](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/conjugate-monopoles#the-laboratory-shadow-t)).

Reference [16]† is S.-Y. Xu et al., "Discovery of a Weyl fermion semimetal," Science 349, 613 (2015), and B. Q. Lv et al., Phys. Rev. X 5, 031013 (2015). Both are cited from model memory and await [the † literature battery](/wiki/method/literature-battery).

The row calls these monopoles "the only shadow". The documents do not say what the phrase commits the program to. In the wiki's reading it names the one realized system with monopoles in momentum space, an analog rather than a test: their existence neither supports nor threatens the null for monopoles of our sector.

## What kills it

"Direct detection" (#11 §13): a confirmed monopole of our electromagnetism, registered for instance by the current step it induces in a superconducting loop or by its ionization track [T]. The lab notebook's wording was "Direct monopole detection" (#1 §9).

In the wiki's reading, since #11 attributes the absence to standard histories rather than to the program (§11.1), a detection would refute the program's commitment to a permanent null rather than a mechanism of its own. The documents do not say what else it would bear on.

## The data situation

No monopole of our sector has been detected [T]. The flux limits the record quotes come from the review of #14, where the round-3 referee checked each against its source (`review/round3/referee/audit-notes.md`, Step 1):

```text
[20] PDG 2024 (Milstead & Weinberg), eq. (95.9): Parker F < 1e-15 cm^-2 sr^-1 s^-1 for M <~ 1e17 GeV. SUPPORTS.
[21] MACRO final: Table 2: "<= 2.8e-16 for m >~ 1e10 GeV, beta > 1e-4"; global 1.4e-16. SUPPORTS the 2.8e-16 figure exactly.
```

PDG is the Particle Data Group; MACRO, the Monopole, Astrophysics and Cosmic Ray Observatory, was an underground detector built for monopole searches. F is the flux of monopoles per unit area, time and solid angle, and β the speed in units of c. The v3 worklog adds how to cite the first: "For the 10⁻¹⁵ Parker value, cite PDG 2024 or Turner–Parker–Bogdan, PRD 26, 1296 (1982), not Parker 1970 alone" ('Verified references for v3'). The bounds, their derivation and the extended Parker bound are on [monopole flux bounds](/wiki/electric-magnetic-duality/monopoles/monopole-searches).

These limits are consistent with the row and cannot confirm it. #11 §15 names rows 1, 4 and 7 as the program's near-term falsification exposure; row 8 is not among them.
