*Born's question asks why physics singles out position when quantum kinematics treats position and momentum as equals. In quantum mechanics a particle can be described by its position or by its momentum; the Fourier transform, the operation that turns a waveform into its spectrum, converts one description into the other exactly, and exchanging the two leaves the kinematics unchanged. Yet atoms are small in space rather than in speed, forces fall off with distance rather than with difference in velocity, and general relativity curves space while leaving momentum flat. Max Born raised the question in 1938. The Broken-Born program is one proposed answer: the symmetry is exact in the laws and broken by the vacuum.*

**Status:** Established — [T]; #11 cites Born [1†, 2†], the 2+1-dimensional result [5†] and the curved-momentum-space lineage [4†, 6†], all from model memory and not yet source-checked — the framing of these facts as the program's starting question is #11 §3's.

## Key facts

- "Quantum kinematics is exactly invariant under ρ: (x,p) → (p,−x); the Fourier transform implements it unitarily [1†, 2†]" (#11 §3). The v1 paper adds that "the canonical commutator is blind to it" (#9 §1). See [Born reciprocity](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/born-reciprocity), [the Fourier transform as a phase-space rotation](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/fourier-transform) and [the canonical commutator](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/canonical-commutator).
- The asymmetry lives in the dynamics. #9 §1: "Hamiltonians are p²/2m + V(x), interactions are local in x, the mass shell is a momentum-space structure with no positional twin, and general relativity curves x-space while leaving momentum fibers flat".
- #11 §3 calls the flat momentum fibers "an architectural assumption, not a measurement, and false in the one exactly solved quantum gravity (2+1d, where momenta live on a curved group manifold [5†]; curved momentum space has an independent lineage from Snyder [4†] through relative locality [6†])". See [curved momentum space](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/curved-momentum-space).
- Born's papers, as #11 lists them (both †): [1] M. Born, "A suggestion for unifying quantum theory and relativity," Proc. R. Soc. Lond. A 165, 291 (1938); [2] M. Born, "Reciprocity theory of elementary particles," Rev. Mod. Phys. 21, 463 (1949).
- Freidel, Leigh and Minic stated the idea the program builds on: "if all probes agree on what we mean by space-time, then we have broken Born duality — there is a preferred choice of polarization, the space-time one" [26S, p. 3]. They treat the breaking as kinematical (#11 §3). See [broken Born duality](/wiki/phase-space/reciprocity/broken-born-duality).
- The program's answer is the [hard core](/wiki/program/hard-core): the symmetry is exact and spontaneously broken, and some breaking is unavoidable because "[x,p] = iℏ forbids joint locality, so any world with local physics must select a Lagrangian polarization" (#11 §3).
