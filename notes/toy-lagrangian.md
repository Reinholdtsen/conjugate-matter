## Success criteria (fixed 2026-10-19, before any construction)

The referees' wording (rounds 1–2): "an action in which ρ is a genuine Noether
symmetry, spontaneously broken, with η the order parameter". Round 3 adds that
the framework does no work. So the criteria come in two tiers. The first tier
is the referees' letter; the second is what would answer round 3.

**Tier 1 (the letter)**

- **S0 — prior art.** Establish what already exists before claiming
  anything. Each element of the toy is either new or cited. (Lesson of the
  compatibility theorem, which turned out to be Freidel–Leigh–Minic's.)
- **S1 — symmetry of an action.** An explicit action S[fields] on which ρ
  acts as a symmetry: S is invariant, not merely related to its image by a
  unitary equivalence. Invariance is verified symbolically.
- **S2 — ρ-related degenerate vacua.** The minima of S form a set on which ρ
  acts without fixed points: no vacuum is ρ-invariant, and ρ maps each vacuum
  to another one. Verified.
- **S3 — η as order parameter.** A local operator built from η (equivalently
  the real structure K, with η = ωK) has a nonzero expectation value in each
  vacuum and is odd under ρ. Verified.

**Tier 2 (the spirit)**

- **S4 — quantum stability.** The breaking survives quantum fluctuations in
  the stated dimension.
  - In 0+1 dimensions (quantum mechanics), tunnelling restores any symmetry,
    so the toy starts in 1+1.
  - In 1+1, a discrete symmetry can break (as in the 2D Ising model). A
    continuous compact one cannot (Coleman–Mermin–Wagner), though there is a
    claimed exception for non-amenable groups, to be checked.
  - Pass requires a citation-backed argument, and preferably a lattice
    simulation showing the order parameter stays nonzero.
- **S5 — the vacuum choice has a consequence for matter.**
  - Matter coupled to the order parameter through ρ-symmetric couplings
    acquires, in the selected vacuum, interactions local in the selected
    Lagrangian (x) and nonlocal in its complement (p). In the ρ-image vacuum
    it is the other way round.
  - This is the analogue of a Yukawa coupling giving masses through a
    vacuum expectation value. It is the content of the program's claim that
    "forces fade with distance, never with velocity difference" because the
    vacuum is polarized.
  - Pass requires an explicit computation, e.g. of a two-point function or
    an effective interaction, showing the anisotropy.

## Predictions (written before construction)

- **S1–S3 will pass easily.** A coset sigma model on the space of
  compatible (H, K) pairs does it classically, which is close to a
  construction by definition. Expect S0 to find it, or something close, in
  the Born-geometry literature.
- **S4 will pass only for a discrete reduction** (the symmetry group cut
  down to the group generated by ρ) or via the non-amenability exception.
  The continuous U(1) of phase-space rotations that contains ρ would be
  restored in 1+1.
- **S5 is the real test, and I expect it not to pass at the first attempt.**
  The likely outcome is that locality has to be defined relative to K by
  hand.
  - The candidate route that might do better is a field theory on
    phase space whose own vacuum breaks x ↔ p, with no separate K field.
  - Recalled prior art for that route (†, unverified): Langmann–Szabo
    duality and the Grosse–Wulkenhaar model at its self-dual point.
- **If S1–S4 pass and S5 fails,** the honest conclusion is that acceptance
  criterion 1 as the referees worded it is too weak to answer "the framework
  does no work". That would be reported as such.

## Chunk 1 results (2026-10-19)

**Baseline coset model** ([verify/toy_coset.py](../verify/toy_coset.py),
gated). d = 1; fields H (phase-space metric, in the upper half-plane Z) and
K (real structure, K² = 1, Kᵀ ω K = −ω), with η = ωK.

- Action:
  L = (f_H²/8) Tr(H⁻¹∂H H⁻¹∂H) + (f_K²/4) Tr(H⁻¹ ∂Kᵀ H ∂K) + (λ/8)|{K, ω⁻¹H}|²_H.
  - K's kinetic term is contracted with H. That makes it positive definite,
    whereas K's own target space (de Sitter dS₂) has an indefinite metric,
    which would give a ghost.
- **S1 pass [V]:** all three terms are invariant under a generic symplectic
  S (H → SᵀHS, K → S⁻¹KS), under ρ, and under K → −K.
- **S2 pass [V]:**
  - The vacua are the compatible pairs (H, K): the upper half-plane times a
    circle of K's, a 3-dimensional space.
  - ρ acts as Z → −1/Z on H and as K → −K at H = 1. No vacuum is
    ρ-invariant.
  - Only {±1} leaves a vacuum fixed, so Sp(2,R) → Z₂ = {1, ρ²}.
- **S3 pass [V]:** O = K₁₁ is +1 in (1, K₀) and −1 in its ρ-image.
- **Spectrum [V]:** the potential's Hessian has rank 1, giving three
  Goldstone modes and one massive mode.
  - The compact Goldstone is the rotation of K at fixed H: the Lagrangian
    Grassmannian Λ(1), which is the checkpoint's Maslov circle (the τ field
    of §10).
  - The other two are the non-compact upper half-plane directions.
- **As predicted, S1–S3 come almost by construction.** This is a coset
  model of Sp(2,R) → Z₂.
- **S4 is the open question.** The breaking is continuous, so in 1+1 the
  compact circle mode should restore ⟨K⟩ (Coleman–Mermin–Wagner) unless the
  non-amenable directions change that. Waiting on the S0 literature check.

**S0 — prior art (literature subagent, 2026-10-19; every citation retrieved
[S], nothing from memory).**

- **Result: no existing action has Born reciprocity exactly symmetric and
  spontaneously broken with η or K as order parameter.** Zero phrase hits on
  INSPIRE full text and arXiv abstracts. INSPIRE's coverage is incomplete,
  so this is evidence of absence, not proof.
- The ingredients exist separately:
  - **The idea:** Freidel–Leigh–Minic.
    - 1307.7080 (PLB 730 (2014) 302): "both elements (η, J) of the chiral
      structure are dynamical. The solutions are labelled by bi-Lagrangians
      and spacetime is a derived dynamical concept".
    - 1405.3949 (IJMPD 23 (2014) 1442006) ties fixed η to locality and finds
      linearized solutions that depend "only on X or only on P".
    - **They have priority on the idea and must be cited.** They write no
      potential and do no symmetry-breaking analysis, and call Born's
      breaking kinematical, not spontaneous.
  - **An interacting action exactly invariant under x ↔ p:**
    Grosse–Wulkenhaar at Ω = 1 (hep-th/0402093, EPJC 35 (2004) 277), with an
    all-orders vanishing β-function there (Disertori–Gurau–Magnen–Rivasseau,
    hep-th/0612251, PLB 649 (2007) 95). The duality is Langmann–Szabo's
    (hep-th/0202039, PLB 533 (2002) 168).
  - **Vacua of that model:** de Goursac–Tanasa–Wallet (0709.3950, EPJC 53
    (2008) 459) find non-constant vacua at Ω = 1 for μ² ≥ 4/θ. **Nobody has
    asked whether they break the duality.** Stripe phases in this literature
    break translations, not x ↔ p.
  - **Non-compact symmetries can break in low dimension:**
    Niedermaier–Seiler (hep-th/0312293, AHP 6 (2005) 1025);
    Duncan–Niedermaier–Seiler (hep-th/0405163, NPB 720 (2005) 235), SO(1,N)
    broken in D ≥ 2, evading Mermin–Wagner.
  - **Related, not the same:**
    - Marotta–Szabo Born sigma models (1910.09997): polarization chosen by
      gauging.
    - Double field theory with spontaneously broken O(D,D) (Angus–Kim–Park
      2111.07947): there H is the order parameter, with η fixed.
    - Low; Govaerts–Jarvis–Morgan–Low (0706.3736): contraction, not breaking.
- **Bearing on S5:** Langmann–Szabo (p. 6): at θ = 0 the position vertex is
  "fully local" and the momentum vertex non-local; "for non-singular θ, both
  vertex functions have the same non-local form". In that literature,
  locality in x arises only at the singular θ → 0 limit, not from a vacuum.

## Chunk 2 results (2026-10-19): the Grosse–Wulkenhaar route

Script: [verify/toy_gw.py](../verify/toy_gw.py), gated. θ = 1.

- Inputs come from the sources:
  - the matrix base from de Goursac–Tanasa–Wallet App. A;
  - the duality from Grosse–Wulkenhaar hep-th/0402093 eq. 1:
    p = x̃ = 2θ⁻¹x, normalization π√|det θ|.
- The explicit Laguerre form of f_mn is checked exactly against
  H ⋆ f_mn = θ(m+½) f_mn and f_mn ⋆ H = θ(n+½) f_mn.

**The convention question from chunk 1 is resolved.**

- **The duality acts as D f_mn = (−1)^m f_mn [V]**, to 1.4×10⁻¹². This is
  a direct numerical 2D Fourier integral with Grosse–Wulkenhaar's kernel,
  not a recalled eigenvalue.
  - It is left multiplication by parity, Π = e^{−iπN}.
- Why it looked contradictory: Grosse–Wulkenhaar's duality alternates the
  sign of the Fourier exponent around the ⋆-vertex. So:
  - **Real model:** the duality is not a symmetry of the field space [V].
    Πφ is not Hermitian, and Tr((Πφ)⁴) ≠ Tr φ⁴. The real action is only
    covariant: its quartic is invariant in the alternating form
    Tr((ψψ†)²) with ψ = Πφ.
  - **Complex model:** the duality is a genuine field symmetry,
    S[Πφ] = S[φ] [V].
- **The Ω = 1 kinetic form is diagonal in the matrix base** and equals
  8π(m+n+1)|φ_mn|² [V]. (A first check used finite-difference derivatives
  and failed at the 0.3% level. The fix was exact derivatives, not a
  looser tolerance.)

**S1–S3 for the complex self-dual model (μ² = 4, so levels 0 and 1
condense): all pass [V].**

- **S1:** the duality Π is a symmetry. It is one element of a continuous
  symmetry: S[UφV†] = S[φ] for any diagonal unitaries U, V.
- **S2:**
  - Every minimum found by descent from random starts is diagonal, with
    |φ_mm|² = q_m = max(0, −(2m+1−μ²)/(2λ)).
  - S_min matches its exact value.
  - The relative phase β between levels 0 and 1 is a flat direction (a
    Goldstone mode).
  - The duality maps β to β + π, so no vacuum is invariant once level 1
    condenses (μ²θ > 12 in GW units).
- **S3:** the order parameter is the vacuum's x-versus-p polarization,
  Δ(β) = ⟨x²⟩_vacuum − ⟨x²⟩_dual = Δ(0) cos β.
  - β = 0 is sharp in x, β = π is sharp in p, β = ±π/2 is balanced.
  - It is odd under the duality: Δ(β + π) = −Δ(β).
- This is, as far as the S0 search found, the first explicit statement that
  the self-dual Grosse–Wulkenhaar vacua break the Langmann–Szabo duality —
  in the complex model, not the real one.

**The structural finding, common to both toys.**

- At the self-dual point, Born reciprocity is never the whole symmetry. It
  is one element of a continuous U(1):
  - the coset model: the U(1) stabilizer of H that rotates K;
  - Grosse–Wulkenhaar: the left phase rotations e^{−iαN}, with the duality
    at α = π.
- So "which polarization" is an angle on a Goldstone circle, not a discrete
  choice. The x-sharp and p-sharp vacua are joined by a flat direction.
- **Consequences for the program:**
  - (i) P5's "the vacuum selects a polarization; ours is x-tilted" needs
    explicit breaking of that U(1) — a potential on the circle — to make the
    selection definite.
  - (ii) In the coset toy, the circle is the Lagrangian Grassmannian Λ(1),
    the checkpoint's τ. So both toys support §10's identification of the
    dark-energy field with the polarization angle, and say the polarization
    is set by where τ sits on its potential.
  - (iii) It also means the present-day thawing of τ would be a slow drift
    of the polarization angle. That is a claim about the program, not
    verified here.
- This is model-level evidence from two toys, not a theorem.

## Chunk 3 results (2026-10-19): S4, quantum stability

**Grosse–Wulkenhaar route: S4 fails, by an exact argument [D].**

- The polarization angle β is the orbit of an exact continuous symmetry
  (S[UφV†] = S[φ], gated in `toy_gw.py`).
- β has no stiffness. The condensate occupies finitely many oscillator
  levels at fixed μ², so nothing couples β across space.
- So the quantum measure is uniform in β for every truncation N, and
  ⟨Δ⟩ = 0 exactly.
- Raising N adds only massive levels; the condensate does not grow.
- Even a potential on β cannot rescue it. The order-parameter sector stays
  finite-dimensional, so the system tunnels between the two vacua. This
  last step is argued, not proven: integrating out the massive tower is
  assumed not to make the barrier grow with N.
- **Net: chunk 2's classical breaking of the duality does not survive
  quantization.** The route gives a polarized classical vacuum and no
  quantum symmetry breaking.

**Coset route: S4 passes only with the τ-type potential [V]**
([verify/toy_s4.py](../verify/toy_s4.py), gated; fixed seeds; Metropolis
on L = 16, 32, 64).

- Reduction: at H = 1 the polarization field is the angle φ of K(φ), and ρ
  acts as φ → φ + π. The kinetic term is an XY stiffness. The τ-type
  potential −μ[Tr(K K_ref)]² = −2μ(1 + cos 2φ) keeps ρ.
- Results:

**What S4 says about the program.**

- In 1+1, Born reciprocity is spontaneously broken only after the
  continuous polarization rotation is explicitly broken down to a discrete
  group containing ρ. Then ρ breaks as a Z₂ (Ising class), with vacua
  ±K_ref: "x is position" and "p is position".
- The price is a rigid, unordered pair of axes (K_ref up to sign). That is
  more rigid structure than P2′'s "only ω is rigid", though less than a
  rigid η.
- **In 3+1, the program's actual dimension, Coleman–Mermin–Wagner does not
  apply.** There the self-dual coset model can break the continuous U(1)
  itself, with τ an exactly massless Goldstone. §10's τ has a potential, so
  the explicit breaking is in the program anyway.
- **Revised conclusion:** the toy supports "broken reciprocity" in the form
  "a pseudo-Goldstone polarization angle with a ρ-symmetric potential", not
  "only ω rigid, nothing else".

**Plan for S5 (chunk 4).** The most promising mechanism is the
section-condition logic of double field theory, which ties locality to η.

- In d = 1 the null lines of η = ωK are exactly the two Lagrangians L and
  L̃. FLM write L = ker(η + ω) and L̃ = ker(η − ω).
- A section condition η^{AB} ∂_A f ∂_B g = 0 on matter fields forces every
  field to depend on one of them only. That is locality on a Lagrangian
  section, with the other variable conjugate.
- But η and −η have the same null lines, so the section condition is blind
  to the sign of K: it selects the unordered pair {L, L̃}, the same
  information as K_ref. Which one a given matter species uses (P4's x- vs
  p-polarized matter) is then the spontaneously broken ρ.
- To do first: an S0-style literature check on dynamical selection of the
  section in DFT and metastring work; then an explicit computation in the
  coset toy coupled to phase-space matter.

## Chunk 4 (in progress, 2026-10-19): S5

Computation: [verify/toy_s5.py](../verify/toy_s5.py), gated. Literature
check on dynamical selection of the section: dispatched, pending. The
conclusion is written after it returns.

- **A [V]:** the null lines of η = ωK(φ) are exactly K's eigenlines L± for
  every φ, and these are the only null lines. K → −K sends η → −η with the
  same null lines. ρ maps K to −K and L+ onto L−.
- **B [V]:** the section condition built from η is purely off-diagonal in
  null coordinates (□ = c ∂_u∂_v).
  - Fields on one null line pass: F(u), G(u), and likewise on v.
  - Mixing the two lines fails: η(dF(u), dG(v)) = −F′G′. So does F(u) + G(v)
    paired with itself.
  - With −η the pairing only changes sign. **The section condition selects
    the unordered pair {L+, L−} and cannot tell position from momentum.**
- **C [V], the weak form of S5:**
  - The ρ-covariant interaction I_K[W] = ∫_{L+}(∫_{L−} W)² of a state's
    Wigner function W gives ∫|ψ(x)|⁴ dx (local in x) in the vacuum +K_ref,
    and ∫|ψ̃(p)|⁴ dp (local in p) in −K_ref.
  - For a non-self-dual test state the two vacua give different numbers
    (0.4070 vs 0.3600).
  - The combined map (ψ → Fψ, K → −K) leaves I unchanged.
  - The K-blind interaction ∫W² (= 1/2π for pure states) shows no
    asymmetry.
- Two checks were vacuous on the first draft (an always-true term; a
  condition multiplied by 0) and were replaced by real tests before commit.

**S5 literature (subagent, 2026-10-19; all citations retrieved [S]).**

- **Known [T]:** consistency confines fields to a single Lagrangian section.
  - Double field theory imposes the strong constraint for gauge closure:
    Hull–Zwiebach 0904.4664; Hohm–Hull–Zwiebach 1003.5027; reviews
    1305.1907, 1306.2643, 1309.2977.
  - It has been derived from modular invariance plus amplitude poles
    (Betz–Blumenhagen–Lüst–Rennecke 1402.1686) and from worldsheet locality
    (FLM metastring 1502.08005 §6).
  - **Every derivation selects only the pair {L, L̃}.** Betz et al.: "w = 0
    for M = 1 and p = 0 for M = −1". All sections are O(D,D)-equivalent.
- **Claimed only in words:** that the choice is dynamical.
  - Freidel–Rudolph–Svoboda 1706.07089: "might be subject to a dynamical
    selection rule".
  - FLM 1502.08005: "a dynamical question".
  - Bernardo–Brandenberger–Franzmann 1901.01209, whose support is a personal
    communication.
  - FLM 1405.3949's linearized □_η H = 0 is the weak constraint, and is
    solved along either null line.
- **Open:** a mechanism that picks L over L̃. Each candidate in the
  literature either selects the pair only, or uses outside input: which
  modes are light, distance-dependent interactions, or a curved position
  space.
- **Prior art for the program's own wording:** FLM 1502.08005: "if all
  probes agree on what we mean by space-time, then we have broken Born
  duality — there is a preferred choice of polarization". **#11 §3 must cite
  FLM for the idea.** What is the program's own: the order-parameter
  (spontaneous) formulation and its consequences.

**S5 conclusion.**

- **Weak form: passes [V].** A ρ-covariant coupling of matter to K makes
  interactions local in L+ in one vacuum and in L− in the other. This works
  as a Yukawa coupling does.
- **Strong form (locality forced, not chosen): not achieved.** It is also
  open in the literature. In the toy:
  - (i) the pair {L+, L−} is the eigenline pair of K. It is spontaneously
    selected only in the continuous version (3+1, angle φ a Goldstone);
    with the τ-type potential it is fixed by K_ref.
  - (ii) Which member is "position" is the spontaneously broken ρ, but
    matter's section is locked to K's sign only through the K-covariant
    coupling. Without that lock, matter's choice of section and K's sign
    are independent orders.
- **The lock is P4** (x-polarized matter, and its ρ-image, p-polarized
  matter). So in the toy, the program's explanation of "forces fade with
  distance" amounts to this: our matter is the x-polarized species. The
  explanatory gain is that x-locality comes packaged with a p-local mirror
  sector, the program's dark-matter claim. It does not derive x-locality
  from nothing.
