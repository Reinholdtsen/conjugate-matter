## 1. The question and the method

*In plain terms: the literature check of 2026-11-02 found that a group around Djordje Minic has proposed since 2020
that dark matter is the dual of ordinary matter, and the summary condensed that into one sentence. Paul asked for a
closer look. This note reads that group's papers one by one and compares what they say with what the program says,
point by point.*

**The request.** Paul, 2026-11-06, of the summary's sentence: "Please investigate the highlighted claim more
thoroughly. Create a page for it at the minimum, especially if the claim is true that there is overlap between our
conjectures and theirs. Also, whenever referenced include a link to the paper itself if possible."

**The clauses under test.**
- P1, core published: the identification, dark matter as the counterpart of ordinary matter in the conjugate (dual)
  description, is stated in substance by Minic and coauthors, first in 2020.
- P2, their sector correlated with ours: as a difference from the program, whose sectors couple by gravity alone
  (#11 P4, taken strictly; §13 row 7, "Permanent null").
- P3, neither form found: neither of C1's two forms, χ particles with a self-interaction gated by relative velocity
  (#11 §8) and magnetically charged solitons localized in momentum (#11 §12, §11.4), is in the literature.
- P4, added for Paul's second sentence: where else the group's published claims meet P.R.'s conjectures and the
  program's core claims.

**How it was done** [S].
- *Workflow A, read and verify.* Nine readers, each followed by a verifier: the priority question (r1); the dual
  Standard Model of 2020–2023 (r2); the restatements of 2022–2026 (r3); the multi-author and phenomenology papers
  and dark energy (r4); Modified Dark Matter, the group's older dark-matter model (r5); the foundations of the

  quoted statements; the verifiers found every quotation again (by code, or in the PDF for sources code could not

- *Workflow B, the panel.* Four judges read all the verified evidence: one asked to argue that the group's proposal
  is C1 in substance, one that it differs, a neutral content judge, and a judge of the overlaps beyond C1. A
  completeness critic, working beside the judges, looked for what was missing and closed what it could. The judges

- *Round 2.* An independent verifier of the critic's find of Winn's work outside the group, and a sweep of
  conference abstracts, a search route no earlier agent had used, each followed by an agent that tried to refute it

- The group, as this note uses the word: papers with Djordje Minic among the authors, and the papers of his

## 2. The verdicts

*In plain terms: all three parts of the summary's sentence hold, two of them only with qualifications. The idea that
dark matter is the dual of ordinary matter is published, but as a proposal, never derived, and first in a talk of
September 2019 rather than in 2020. The group's dark sector does interact with ours beyond gravity in most of its
papers, but only at higher orders in a string-scale length, with a strength no paper computes, and one paper of 2024
says the two interact only by gravity below a very high energy. Neither of the program's two forms of dark matter
was found, in the group's papers or elsewhere. The overlap with the program goes well beyond dark matter: much of
the program's geometric setting is in the group's earlier papers, though not its central move, the reading of the
choice of position as a spontaneously broken symmetry with an order parameter.*

## 3. What the group publishes, and when

*In plain terms: Minic first put the idea forward in a talk in Belgrade on 9 September 2019, with a question mark,
and in writing in February 2020; his coauthors restated it many times until 2026. Earlier papers of the group, from
2016 and 2017, had said something narrower: that strange non-local particles might be what dark matter is made of.*

**The statement** [S].
- Minic 2020 (arXiv, 29 February 2020; the proceedings of the Belgrade talk), §6.2: "The proposal here is that the
  dual sector (as already indicated in the previous section) should be interpreted as the dark matter sector, which
  is correlated to the visible sector via the dark energy sector". §5 writes the effective action of "Standard Model
  matter" and "their duals", which "could be interpreted as dark matter", and gives ordinary matter "natural cousins"
  in the dark sector.
- Berglund, Hübsch and Minić 2021 (arXiv, 29 October 2020), abstract: "dark matter, on the other hand, comes from the
  degrees of freedom dual to the visible matter". Edmonds, Minic and Takeuchi 2021, §4: "Thus the entire SM would
  have a dual SM (which we propose, describes the dark matter sector)." Berglund, Hübsch and Minic 2023 (Int. J. Mod.
  Phys. D), §6.4: "the dual particles are conjugate to the Standard Model particles in analogy with the canonical
  fact that momenta p are conjugate to coordinates q". The papers of Hübsch and Minic of 2024 and April 2026 restate
  it; their paper of 14 October 2026 is later than the program's lab notebook (September 2026) and is a
  restatement, not prior art.
- Further papers restate it: Jejjala, Kavic, Minic and Takeuchi (posted November 2020) relate the dual
  part of the matter sector to dark matter, in a weaker form; the review of Addazi et al. (posted November 2021; 162
  authors, Minic and Kowalski-Glikman among them) associates the duals of the observed fields with dark matter as a
  possibility; and two papers of Berglund et al. (February 2022, of 12 and 7 authors) restate it, crediting Minic
  2020.
- Every statement is worded as a proposal ("could be interpreted", "we propose", "candidates"); none is derived.
  Minic 2020 §3 adds that its doubled field formulation keeps the number of degrees of freedom of the ordinary one,
  and the paper does not say how its duals then make a separate dark sector (the verifier of r1, two judges); §5
  notes a possible ambiguity in the gauging that yields the dual fields.

**The first public statement** [S]. Minic's invited talk at the 10th Mathematical Physics Meeting, Belgrade, on
Monday 9 September 2019. Its slides are public (www.mphys10.ipb.ac.rs/slides/Minic.pdf; the PDF was created on 29
August 2019, and the server dates the file 10 September 2019). The Internet Archive's copy of 19 September 2019 is
the file's first 1 MiB, byte-identical to today's file; it holds pages 1–7, not the pages of slides 5 and 6, which
are dated by the file's own dates and by that copy's record of today's length and page count (the verifier of r1;
the critic). Slide 5 reads "Each particle of the Standard Model has a 'dual' particle", then "dual particles →
dual Standard Model (hidden sector?)", and makes metaparticles "dark matter quanta correlated to visible
(baryonic/Standard Model) quanta"; slide 6 marks the dual matter Lagrangian as
"dark matter sensitive to Λ and L_m(x)". The dual Standard Model carries a question mark, and the metaparticle line
alone is the claim of 2017 (below): the identification rests on slide 5's lines read together and on slide 6 (the
verifier of r1). The slides are handwritten; a reader, its verifier, the critic and round 2's refuter of the
conference sweep each read them from the images, and code could not check them (the slide text is set in capitals
there; it is given in lower case here). The talk precedes the written statement by 173 days.

**Possibly earlier in writing, unread.** Freidel, Leigh and Minic, Int. J. Mod. Phys. A 34, 1941004 (published
online 15 October 2019), is closed access; two attempts to read it were refused. Minic 2020 §7 cites it alone for
dark matter "represented by metaparticles", Berglund, Hübsch and Minic 2023 (Int. J. Mod. Phys. D) §4.3 cite it
with Minic 2020 for dual particles as dark-matter candidates, and its reference list includes six Modified Dark
Matter papers, so it may hold an earlier written statement. Its abstract mentions metaparticles but not dark
matter, and the abstract of the talk of February 2018 behind it has no dark matter (the critic). It cannot move the
first public statement before the Belgrade talk.

**Narrower precursors** [S]. The group's earlier papers proposed that non-local quanta of a quantum-gravity theory
make up dark matter, not that dark matter is the dual of visible matter.
- Chang, Minic, Sun and Takeuchi (arXiv, May 2016), §5: "Some weakly interacting chargeless non-local quanta would be
  the perfect candidates for dark matter." These are extended excitations of the same fields, not dual partners.
- Edmonds, Farrah, Minic, Ng and Takeuchi (arXiv, 13 September 2017), §5: "We expect that such non-local
  excitations (as part of a covariant non-commutative formulation, as implied by the metastring [131, 132]) can
  serve as unusual dark matter quanta." The same claim is in their paper of 30 December 2017.
- The sentence of Minic 2020's abstract that flag 64 quotes, "metaparticle quanta (the zero modes of the
  metastring) as the natural quanta of dark matter in this approach", is this narrower claim: a metaparticle is a
  particle and its dual together ("the metaparticle looks like two particles that are entangled through a Berry
  phase-like" coupling, §4.3). The identification proper is in §5 and §6.2.
- Further back, and further away: Jejjala, Kavic and Minic 2007 ask whether infinite statistics gives dark energy and
  dark matter at once; Ho, Minic and Ng 2010 propose "a duality between cold dark matter and modified Newtonian
  dynamics", a different duality (Modified Dark Matter, §5).

## 4. What their "dual" is

*In plain terms: the group uses the word "dual" in three senses. In its quantum-mechanics papers the dual coordinate
is simply momentum, rescaled, exactly as in the program. In its string theory, where the dark sector is actually
built, the dual coordinate is a second set of coordinates with momenta of their own, failing to commute with
ordinary position at a tiny length. And in one review the dark fields are "conjugate" to the visible ones in the
sense that the fields themselves fail to commute. The group says the first sense becomes the second; the program
works only with the first.*

- **Momentum, rescaled, in one phase space** [S]. In the group's account of quantum mechanics x̃ ≡ p/ε, with λε = ℏ,
  so that [x, x̃] = i (Minic 2020 §2; Freidel, Leigh and Minic 2016; Hübsch and Minic 2024 §3.2; some papers put 2πℏ
  for ℏ). Freidel, Leigh and Minic 2015, §2: "Y can be thought of as a momentum coordinate". This is the program's
  sense (#11 §3, P2′, P4).
- **A doubled phase space at a fundamental length** [S]. Where the dark sector is built, x̃ is the difference of the
  string's left- and right-moving coordinates, with [x, x̃] proportional to λ², λ the fundamental (string) length,
  and each of x and x̃ has its own momentum (Minic 2020 §4.1; Berglund, Hübsch and Minić 2021 §2). The dark fields
  are backgrounds of the dual momentum p̃, which become "dark matter" fields on ordinary spacetime "after
  integrating over the dual spacetime" (Hübsch and Minic 2024 §3.3). Two agents computed, from the papers' brackets,
  that this phase space has twice the dimension of the program's.
- **Conjugacy in field space** [S]. Berglund, Hübsch and Minic 2023 (Int. J. Mod. Phys. D), §6.4: "That is, the
  dual fields do not commute with Standard Model fields, and the two form an effective Heisenberg algebra." The
  authors induce it from the commutator of x and x̃ and call the reasoning beyond the rules of effective field theory.
- **The group joins the senses** [S]. Hübsch and Minic 2024 §3.2 say that the description with x̃ ≡ p/ε "will appear
  in what follows in the metastring formulation of string theory"; Berglund, Hübsch and Minic 2023 (Symmetry) §4.1
  call x̃ "the dual (momentum-like) spacetime".
- **A citation in the record names the second sense.** The phrase "canonically conjugate dual space", which the
  literature check cited for the momentum side, names the doubled coordinate: Berglund, Hübsch and Minić 2019 (Int.
  J. Mod. Phys. D) define "the canonically conjugate dual space, spanned by the difference of the left- and
  right-movers". In the full string phase space the canonical partner of x is the ordinary momentum p (Freidel,
  Leigh and Minic 2017, JHEP, eq. 69); only on the reduced space of x and x̃ does x̃, divided by 2πλ², play the role of
  x's conjugate (same paper, §4.2; the verifier of r6).
- The program polarizes one phase space (x, p) at ℏ with ω rigid; the group's dark sector lives in a doubled one.
  The literature check's summary, "their model doubles phase space where the program polarizes one", stands.

## 5. How their dark sector meets ours

*In plain terms: at the first level of approximation the group's two sectors are independent and share only gravity,
as in the program. Beyond it, the group's papers add couplings: a phase-like entanglement, terms that mix visible
and dark fields, a dark photon tied to ours. No paper says how strong these are, and one paper of 2024 puts them
above a very high energy, with only gravity below it. The group's older model of dark matter ties dark matter to
ordinary matter in galaxies, but as an observed regularity, without an interaction term.*

- **Leading order: independent** [S]. Berglund, Hübsch and Minic 2023 (Int. J. Mod. Phys. D), §6.3: "These fields
  are in general correlated, due to their non-commutativity. However, to first order in the fundamental length, we
  can treat them as independent." In Minic 2020 §5 the effective action at zeroth order in λ is a sum of the visible
  and dual Lagrangians on one metric, written on a gravitational background the paper holds fixed.
- **Beyond leading order: couplings beyond gravity** [S]. Minic 2020 §5: a Berry-phase-like term through which "dark
  matter quanta should be detectable through their particular metaparticle entanglement to visible matter".
  Berglund, Hübsch and Minić 2021: mixing terms between visible and dual fields, vector, scalar, pseudo-scalar and
  fermion mass mixing (axions are among their pseudo-scalars); "This correlation becomes
  invisible in effective field theory", and it vanishes as λ → 0 or as the infrared cutoff L → ∞; "Note that with a
  particular double scaling, this term can be finite!" (§4); and, §5, "In the dual QED sector, we should find a dual
  photon that is correlated to the visible
  photon, and that is distinct from the dark photon of effective field theory. The correlation is proportional to
  the fundamental length, and is finite even in the limit of zero momenta (deep infrared)." No paper computes the
  strength of any of these terms.
- **Gravity alone, in one paper** [S]. Hübsch and Minic 2024 (arXiv, July 2024), §5.3: dark and visible matter "mix
  via a Berry phase-like correlation term" whose mass scale "defines the decoupling energy, below which the dark and
  visible matter interact as required, only gravitationally", a scale they identify with 4.5×10¹⁴ GeV (their
  M_SM ≃ 4.5×10¹⁴ GeV). The statement is not in their papers of 2026 or in Berglund, Hübsch and Minic 2023
  (Symmetry). So one paper of the group, before the program, joins the identification to coupling by gravity alone
  at every accessible energy; the record's "not found" for that combination is too strong. It is one sentence, and
  the same paper keeps effects of the dual sector at low energy that it does not reconcile with it: a correlation of
  dual and visible fields at the length λ as the origin of the acceleration scale a₀ in galaxies (§3.4, below), and
  CP-violating phases of visible mixing from the dual sector (§5.4) (three judges). Nor does it join the
  identification to a permanent null: it traces an observed scaling, a₀, to that correlation.
- **The paper nearest the program** [S]. Hübsch and Minic's paper of April 2026, the group's last restatement
  before the program's notebook, keeps only the non-commuting dual fields and the a₀ correlation: the critic found in
  it no mixing term, no signal at accelerators and no decoupling energy, and no correlation of dark and visible
  matter in their paper of October 2026. The judges did not see this.
- **Modified Dark Matter's correlation** [S]. The group's older model ties the dark-matter profile of a galaxy to its
  baryons through an acceleration scale a₀ ~ cH₀. Its papers write no interaction term; the profile is fitted in
  ordinary general relativity: "Despite its appearance, Eq. (18) does not modify
  General Relativity (GR) since the derivation of the right-hand-side is based on GR" (Edmonds et al., January
  2016). Edmonds, Minic and Takeuchi's 2020 essay grants that, for galaxies, the scale may emerge in ΛCDM ("At this
  point in time, Occam's razor would favor the first option", the first option being that emergence), then argues
  from galaxy clusters for a fundamental acceleration scale; in 2017 the authors of Modified Dark Matter wrote that
  the dependence on a₀ goes beyond classical general relativity and local effective field theory. Gravity alone is a
  reading their papers allow, not the one they prefer (the verifier of r5). The group's 2024 essay on fuzzy dark
  matter, which has no dual sector, ties baryons and dark matter through the gravitational potential alone (Edmonds,
  Erlich, Minic and Takeuchi 2024). Ho, Minic and Ng 2010 speculate that the couplings of their dark matter "could be
  nonstandard". The later papers trace the a₀ scaling to the correlation of dual and visible fields (Hübsch and
  Minic 2024 §3.4), and the paper of April 2026 calls the observed scale evidence for that correlation (§5.1).
- **So "correlated" is the group's own word** and covers three things: non-commuting visible and dark fields at a
  string-scale length; couplings beyond gravity beyond leading order, of uncomputed strength; and the galactic
  regularity of Modified Dark Matter. The group's papers do not agree on the low-energy question, and none says which
  position is current.

## 6. Detection

*In plain terms: both the group and the program explain why dark matter has not been detected. The program says it
never will be, by any non-gravitational experiment. The group expects it to be, through unusual signals.*

- **Past nulls explained** [S]. Minic 2020 §5: "If the quanta of modified dark matter are metaparticles, this may
  explain why, so far, dark matter detection experiments have failed to detect dark matter particles." Chang, Minic,
  Sun and Takeuchi 2016: "Their non-locality would explain their invisibility to dark-matter detection experiments
  which rely on localized interactions between localized quanta."
- **Detection expected** [S]. Minic 2020 §5 (above: "should be detectable"); Berglund, Hübsch and Minić 2021 §5,
  the correlated dual photon and correlated events at accelerators; Edmonds et al. (September 2017), which leaves to
  a more concrete theory tests "at colliders, dark matter direct detection experiments and indirect detection
  experiments", as does their paper of January 2016.
- **The program**: "Permanent null (both dark-sector candidates SM-decoupled; P4 strict)", killed by "Any confirmed
  non-gravitational DM–SM coupling" (#11 §13 row 7). The literature check's "the school, like the program, expects no
  standard direct-detection signal" holds only for the group's explanation of past nulls, not for its expectations.
- The group's explanation of the null by the non-local or dual nature of the quanta, a conditional one, has the
  shape of P.R.'s conjecture C3 (monopoles in conjugate space explain why none has been found), applied to dark
  matter rather than to monopoles (related, three judges).

## 7. The two forms

*In plain terms: neither of the program's two kinds of dark matter was found, in the group's papers or outside them.
The group's dark matter has no force that depends on relative velocity, and nothing in it is a magnetic monopole.*

- **χ with a velocity gate: absent** [S]. None of the 41 full texts swept contains "relative velocit"; the one
  interaction law between metaparticles the group gives, a static potential of Friedel form (Berglund, Hübsch and
  Minic 2023 (Int. J. Mod. Phys. D), §4.3, footnote 31, citing unpublished work), has no velocity dependence. The
  group's dark matter is "fuzzy" in its own sense: visible and dark fields do not commute, and "Therefore, one could
  say that dark matter is fuzzy from
  the point of view of classical spacetime." (Hübsch and Minic 2024 §3.3).
  Outside the group, velocity-dependent self-interaction has ordinary mechanisms besides the Yukawa power law that
  #11 §13 row 1 names: resonant self-interaction (Chu, Garcia-Cely and Murayama 2019) and finite-size dark matter,
  whose cross-section "decreases with the velocity" (Chu, Garcia-Cely and Murayama 2020; the critic); what is not
  found is the gate's mechanism, not a falling cross-section.
- **Monopoles localized in momentum: absent** [S]. "Magnetic" appears in the group's papers as a label, "(“magnetic”)
  duals" for the dual fields (Minic 2020 §3, which points to the dark-matter duals of §5); as an analogy, the algebra
  of x and x̃ likened to electromagnetism with monopoles (Minic 2020, footnote 12); as worldsheet dyons, string
  states that carry momentum and winding (Freidel, Leigh and Minic 2014, Phys. Lett. B; 2015); as the pairing of the
  heterotic string's two E8 factors as Langlands, electric–magnetic, duals, for gauge groups only (Berglund, Hübsch
  and Minic 2021, arXiv:2111.14205); and as "conserved dual (magnetic) charges" of gravitational corner symmetries
  (Hübsch and Minic 2024, footnote 11). None is magnetically charged matter, and nothing is localized in x̃ or in
  momentum.
- Outside the group (§10), the judges cite Ran's dark state on the momentum side, topologically wound but not
  magnetic, and monopole dark matter in ordinary space; the magnetically charged partners Winn proposes, found after
  the panel, are elementary and in ordinary space.

## 8. The vacuum and the choice of polarization

*In plain terms: the program's central move is to read the choice of which phase-space directions count as position
as a spontaneously broken symmetry, chosen by the vacuum, with an order parameter. #11 credits Freidel, Leigh and
Minic with the idea that such a choice breaks Born's symmetry, and says they treat the breaking as kinematical. They
do more: they propose to make the choice dynamical, and a later paper of theirs places a symmetry breaking tied to
the choice in the vacuum, with the expansion of the universe selecting the frame. What none of their papers has is
the program's form of it: an exact symmetry that preserves ω, broken by a vacuum, with η as order parameter and a
potential.*

- **"Kinematical" is their word for gravity's breaking** [S]. Freidel, Leigh and Minic 2014 (Int. J. Mod. Phys. D),
  §1.3: "Moreover, the purely kinematical splitting of phase space into space and momentum space has to be made
  dynamical". In all 16 uses of "kinematical" in that paper and in their metastring paper (2015), the word describes
  the breaking by general relativity, which they credit to Born. #11 §3's "They treat the breaking as kinematical"
  gives their description of general relativity, not their proposal (flag 70). Against #11 the point is weaker than
  it looks: the same sentence of #11 reports that they call the choice a dynamical question, and the selector their
  metastring paper exhibits, worldsheet locality, is a consistency condition (the verifier of r7). Their stated aim
  is to restore Born's duality principle in quantum gravity (the 2014 paper, §1.1), App. A item 1's "Symmetrize QM
  around conjugate variables" as an aim (partial, the overlap and difference judges); the hard core's form, an exact
  symmetry broken spontaneously with only ω rigid, is not theirs.
- **The split carried by solutions** [S]. Same paper, §2: "This formulation does not require the presence of a
  bi-Lagrangian structure" K; "However when we look at solutions of string theory, we find this extra structure."
  The split is absent from the formulation and present in its solutions: the logical form of a spontaneous breaking,
  without the word, a potential or an order parameter (the verifier of r6; the overlap and content judges follow the
  reading, the content judge as an inference, and both label the overlap related).
- **A breaking in the vacuum sector, with the expansion as selector** [S]. Freidel, Kowalski-Glikman, Leigh and Minic
  (arXiv, 1 April 2021): "The symmetry breaking happens at the level of the vacuum sector, and thus a choice of ground

  O(1, d−1)₂, that within the sector of fixed non-zero p̃ it "is effectively spontaneously broken".
- **Locality, and the observer's choice** [S]. Freidel, Leigh and Minic (J. Phys. Conf. Ser., 2017) write that Born
  reciprocity is broken by choosing absolute locality, one spacetime shared by all probes: the premise of #11 §3's

  inequivalent lifts" η "that preserve the symplectic structure and the vacuum state", the manifold over which #11
  P2′'s order parameter ranges (Λ(d), §6), named as a space of choices, with no potential on it. The structure
  η = HJ of P2′ is theirs from 2013: both elements (η, J) "of the chiral structure are dynamical" (Freidel, Leigh

- **The other polarization equally available** [S]. Freidel, Kowalski-Glikman, Leigh and Minic 2018 (metaparticles),

## 9. Other overlaps

*In plain terms: several results the program derived or uses appear, in some form, in the group's earlier papers: the
positive form of its compatibility theorem, the inverse scaling of the momentum side as the universe grows, a dual
gravity term, and dark energy from the dual side. None of them is one of P.R.'s conjectures C2–C7, and several are
already credited to the same authors in another form.*

- **The compatibility theorem for positive H** [S]. #11 §5 credits Freidel, Leigh and Minic 2015 §2.1, a statement
  for a Lorentzian H with the B-field set to zero. Their 2016 paper gives the positive form in group language: "the
  group of transformations that preserves the symplectic structure and the polarization metric" η is GL(d) =
  Sp(2d) ∩ O(d, d), whose frames are "determining the vacuum metric" H = x q x + x̃ q⁻¹ x̃ with q positive (§2), that
  is H = diag(q, q⁻¹). They assert it without the block proof; the proof and battery test T1 stay the program's
  (flag 71).
- **The inverse scaling behind a·ã = 1** [S]. Freidel, Leigh and Minic 2016 §1.4: the symplectic map (x, x̃) →
  (ax, a⁻¹x̃) "squeezes the modular cell along the momentum direction and stretches it in the space direction";
  Freidel, Kowalski-Glikman, Leigh and Minic 2021 eq. (2) rescale the modular cell the same way, and tie the dual
  momentum to the cosmic scale factor; Hur, Jejjala, Kavic, Minic and Takeuchi 2025 convert our volume into the dual
  one through the uncertainty "relation implied by the fundamental non-commutativity of" x and x̃, δx δx̃ ∼ λ² (§2). The
  program derives a·ã = 1 from ω-rigidity at ℏ with one lapse (#11 §10, [D]). The squeeze of 2016 is the same
  commutator-preserving rescaling in one phase space at ℏ, used for a decoupling limit; the tie to the cosmic scale
  factor is made only for the doubled coordinate (2021). Three judges label the overlap related; the overlap judge
  labels it a close variant, the same algebra put to another use.
- **A dual Einstein term** [S]. Berglund, Hübsch and Minić 2019 (Phys. Lett. B) integrate the doubled gravitational
  action over x̃: "the curvature of the canonically conjugate dual space provides for a positive cosmological
  constant to leading order", and its size sets Newton's constant. That is a published treatment of the dual
  Einstein–Hilbert term of flag 49, with the opposite assignment to #11 §10's "dual curvature ↦ negative dark
  radiation".
- **Dark energy, both signs** [S]. Hur et al. 2025 publish two models. Under the group's earlier ultraviolet–infrared
  relation, "Clearly, w(a)<-1 for all a as anticipated", and "this model is clearly disfavored" by DESI's second
  release (§3.3); under an alternative, w(a) ≥ −1 in all cases (§3.4), w being dark energy's equation of state, its
  pressure divided by its energy density. Jejjala, Kavic, Minic and Takeuchi (arXiv, February 2022; arXiv:2202.05266)
  had found that equation of state below −1 (−1 is the value for a cosmological constant), which, they wrote, rules
  out quintessence. The phase-space-diameter note (§7) cites only the second model's result (flag 72). The sign
  follows from an assumed relation, so the group has no fixed prediction on #11 §13 row 4.
- **A momentum side with its own gravity** [S]. Freidel, Leigh and Minic 2014 (Phys. Lett. B): "not only will
  space-time become curved, but momentum space as well", an idea they trace to Born (2015 §1). #11 App. A item 10
  ("Momentum space has its own gravity") and §3's lineage of curved momentum space (Snyder, relative locality) do not
  cite it; the program's form, a massive spin-2 field X odd under reciprocity, has no counterpart in the group.
- **Newton's constant as a conversion factor between space and momentum** [S]. Freidel, Leigh and Minic 2014 (Int.
  J. Mod. Phys. D), §1.2: Newton's constant "can be considered as a universal conversion factor between the
  seemingly different concepts of space and momentum space, in the same way that the speed of light is a universal
  conversion factor between space and time." Freidel, Leigh and Minic 2016 add that at the Planck scale the
  gravitational tension ε/λ ∼ c⁴/G_N sets the squeeze of the modular cell. The reader of r6 had both passages, and
  the reader of r7 the second; the critic tied them to #13's construction of a momentum-space scale, the maximum
  force c⁴/G, from the same pair of scales, and no judge labeled that link. The group states no maximum force and no
  momentum-space light cone (flag 72).
- **One state in two descriptions** [S]. Freidel, Leigh and Minic 2015 §2: the transformation X^μ → Y_μ "corresponds
  to the string Fourier transformation"; App. A items 2 and 8 ("one state, two descriptions"; "Fourier/holography")

- **The magnetic mirror** (C4) [S]. Related only, inside the group: the "magnetic" label of Minic 2020 §3 and the E8

- **Born geometry and S-duality** [S]. Berglund, Hübsch and Minic 2023 (Int. J. Mod. Phys. D) remark that the Born

## 10. Outside the group

*In plain terms: no one outside the group has published the identification itself. Several authors have put dark
matter in some other "dual" space, coupled to ours by gravity, and one independent report of December 2025 puts its
origin on the momentum–energy side of a single quantum state. One physicist has proposed since 2012 that every
charged particle has a magnetically charged partner under our own photon, bound into neutral dark matter, close to
the program's magnetic mirror but in ordinary space.*

- **Ran 2025** [S]. Sheng Ran (Washington University in St. Louis), "Dark Matter from Dual-Manifold Coupling", a
  ResearchHub report posted 24 December 2025, on a framework in which one quantum state has a space–time and a
  momentum–energy description, each with its own dynamics: "Here we show that dark matter arises naturally as a
  structural consequence of the coupling between these two dynamical sectors"; it is "not a new particle species".
  Independent of the group and earlier than the program's notebook; related, since it never makes dark matter the
  counterpart of ordinary matter (the literature check's round 3 found and labeled it). Its dark state's momentum
  profile is localized and topologically wound, with no magnetic charge and no velocity gate (r8): all four judges
  cite it under P3, and none counts it as either form.
- **Dark matter in another dual space, coupled by gravity** [S]. Hauser and Dröscher 2019 (Z. Naturforsch. A,
  online 10 April 2019; abstract only): dark matter of negative mass "is assumed to reside in" a dual de Sitter
  space with imaginary time "and therefore is principally invisible", its gravitational interaction "directly
  perceptible"; Nishimura 2022: a sector of a dual isospin spacetime with no gauge interactions with ours,
  interacting through gravity; Houlden 2015: vacuum states in a "dual space" that interact gravitationally;
  Bolognesi 2012 and 2015: a Born-reciprocal theory whose one matter field is identified with dark matter. None
  makes dark matter the counterpart of ordinary matter (Nishimura's comes nearest: conditionally, his dark sector
  would hold almost all the antiparticles; the verifier of r8), and none is the momentum side of one phase space.
  Hauser and Dröscher's abstracts do not say that the coupling is by gravity alone (the same verifier).
- **A magnetic dual of every particle, outside the group** [S]. David R. Winn (Fairfield University) has proposed
  since 2012 that every charged particle of the Standard Model has a magnetically charged partner under our own
  photon. Winn 2012 (arXiv, 18 June 2012), abstract: "A SUSY is proposed that interchanges bosons-fermions, and also
  transforms particle electric charge to magnetic charge, chromoelectric charge to chromomagnetic charge"; p. 1:
  "there are no electrically charged superpartners, only neutral or magnetic charged partners"; p. 2: "The resulting
  strong magnetic force between any pair-produced monopoles explains the absence of detected monopoles heretofore",
  and the partners' magnetically neutral bound states are proposed as dark matter. He restated it in talks and
  posters of 2025 and 2026 (the APS Global Physics Summit, SUSY 2025, Lepton-Photon 2025, Invisibles25, the APS
  Division of Particles and Fields 2026; a talk at Aspen in March 2025 is known by its title only) and in a 40-page
  draft of August 2025: "For each SM elementary particle (quarks, leptons, vector bosons, Higgs), there is a Dual
  (effectively dark) sector particle that preserves flavor and preserves masses at tree level up to field
  self-energy corrections." (the written summary of the Lepton-Photon talk, p. 1). The
  partners are elementary point particles, not solitons; nothing is placed in momentum or phase space, and no Born
  reciprocity appears; and "In this early stage simplistic description of a Dual, interaction terms and a Lagrangian
  are not yet proposed" (same page). For P.R.'s conjectures, as round 2's verifier labels it and its refuter, who
  downloaded every file again and found all 72 quotations again, confirms: partial for the shared-photon form of the
  magnetic mirror, C4 (#11 §11.4), the strongest overlap, in the verifier's words a close variant: one magnetic analog
  per charged particle, our own photon, and hiding by binding into magnetically neutral composites, the second of the
  two routes §11.4 names, but without the momentum polarization, the conjecture's defining clause. Related: C1's
  identification (a dual of every particle as dark matter, under an electric–magnetic, chirality and spin duality
  rather than in phase space; partial under the broad reading of C1, in the verifier's judgment); C1's monopole form,
  C2 and C3 (magnetically charged dark matter, abundant monopoles, and the null result explained by binding, all in
  ordinary space); and, the refuter adds, C6 (Bose condensates of sneutrinos as dark matter). It narrows the
  literature check's C4 finding: one analog per charged particle is published, the placement in momentum is not
  (flag 73).
- **Conference abstracts** [S]. A sweep of conference abstracts, slides and proceedings from 2015 to 2026, a route no
  earlier agent took (90 full-text queries on CERN Indico, 113 contributions and 3,288 attachment files screened, and
  contribution lists and proceedings sets of 19,740 records), checked by a refuter, found the identification, in its
  phase-space sense, only in the group's own talks, and neither form. Its refuter qualifies the first finding: Winn's
  talks state a dual of ordinary matter as dark matter under electric–magnetic duality, and talks on mirror matter
  one under parity. The nearest to the forms are monopole dark matter in ordinary space and an excited-dark-matter
  threshold that opens above a velocity, the reverse of the program's knee; a talk of 2010 on dark-matter candidates
  from a curved momentum space, with no counterpart among ordinary particles, is related to C1
  (`review/dual-sector/round2/`). Fermilab Indico, the APS meeting archive and INFN Indico refused automated access,
