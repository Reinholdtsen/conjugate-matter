# Literature check of P.R.'s conjectures: brief for search and verification agents

You are one of several agents checking whether the published literature already contains P.R.'s conjectures (P.R. is
the program's originator; see the root `CLAUDE.md`). The result will decide how the program states what is new in it.
Two failures matter equally:

- **missed prior art:** calling a conjecture new when a paper already states it;
- **inflated prior art:** calling it published when a paper only shares words or a theme with it.

A "not found" must rest on searches you record. An "anticipates" must rest on text you quote and check.

## The program, for orientation

- **Born reciprocity** is the symmetry (x, p) → (p, −x) of quantum kinematics (Born 1938, 1949). The Comatter program
  takes it as exact and spontaneously broken: the vacuum selects a polarization, a choice of which variables act as
  "position". Ordinary matter is x-polarized, localized and organized in position. **Conjugate matter** is p-polarized,
  organized and localized in momentum. Both share one universe and gravity; ordinary gauge forces do not cross
  polarizations (postulate P4). Read `wiki/papers/broken-born-checkpoint-v2.md` (#11) §1 and §3–§4 for the framework.
- **The program's cosmology:** a·ã = 1, so the scale factor of the conjugate (momentum) description is the inverse of
  ours; as our universe expands, the conjugate universe contracts (#11 §10). A fluid with equation of state w̃ in the
  dual frame acts on ours as w_eff = −w̃.

## Tools

`work/literature-check/tools/lit.py` (run `python3 work/literature-check/tools/lit.py` from the repo root for help).
It talks to INSPIRE-HEP, arXiv, Crossref and arXiv's HTML full texts, throttles itself, and caches every fetch under
`work/literature-check/cache/`, which all agents share.

- `search 'INSPIRE query' [--size N] [--sort mostcited|mostrecent] [--abstracts]`: INSPIRE syntax: `t "phrase"`
  (title), `a Name` (author), `abstracts.value:"phrase"`, `fulltext "phrase"` (the full text of arXiv papers, very
  useful), `refersto:recid:N`, with `and`, `or`, `not`.
- `show ID ...`: metadata and abstract (ID = arXiv id, `recid:N` or `doi:...`). `doi DOI`: Crossref metadata.
- `cites ID` and `refs ID`: the citation graph, forward and backward.
- `arxiv 'query'`: the arXiv API, which covers cond-mat, math-ph and physics that INSPIRE lacks (`all:`, `ti:`,
  `abs:`, `au:`, `AND`, `OR`, quoted phrases).
- `crossref 'query'`: journal papers and books not on arXiv.
- `text ID --grep REGEX [--grep ...]`: the full text of an arXiv paper, each match in context.
- `quote ID 'verbatim text'`: checks that the quote occurs in the abstract or full text. Run it on every quote you
  report and record its verdict.

Also load WebSearch and WebFetch with ToolSearch (`select:WebSearch,WebFetch`) for sources the tool cannot reach:
publisher pages, books, non-arXiv journals. WebSearch takes `mode`: "standard" by default, "extended" for niche
queries where standard comes back thin. Google Scholar cannot be fetched.

## Method

1. Read the conjecture's wiki page and source sections first, so you search for the claim, not just its words.
2. Use at least five search modalities: INSPIRE title and abstract; INSPIRE full text; the citation graph forward and
   backward from the closest papers you find; the arXiv API; Crossref or WebSearch for venues outside arXiv. Vary the
   vocabulary: the same idea appears as "dual", "reciprocal", "conjugate", "momentum-space", "phase-space", "mirror",
   "T-dual", "doubled", "metaparticle", "Born geometry", "modular spacetime", "trans-Planckian", "co-tangent bundle".
3. Record every query with its database and hit count.
4. Before labeling a paper, read its abstract, and for any claim beyond the abstract, the passage in the full text.
5. Quote verbatim, check each quote with `lit.py quote`, and record the verdict. If the tool cannot reach the text,
   read it with WebFetch and record "checked by WebFetch only".
6. Never cite from memory. A paper you recall but cannot retrieve goes under `unverified_leads`, marked †.
7. For each conjecture, name the closest paper found and say exactly how it differs.
8. State the venue: journal, arXiv preprint only, conference proceedings, book, or non-reviewed. Do not otherwise
   grade venues.

## Relation labels

- **anticipates:** the paper states the conjecture in substance, the same physical claim and not just the same words.
  Differences of framework are allowed; name them.
- **partial:** the paper states a central part of the conjecture, one of its forms, or a close variant.
- **related:** the paper shares the conjecture's theme or mechanism but does not state it.
- **evidence-against:** the paper contains a result or bound that weighs against the conjecture.
- **collision:** the paper uses the same words for a different thing (for example "monopole in momentum space" for
  Berry curvature, "phase-space density" for the Tremaine–Gunn bound, "magnetic mirror" for plasma confinement).
  Report the ones a reader is likely to meet, a handful per conjecture at most.
